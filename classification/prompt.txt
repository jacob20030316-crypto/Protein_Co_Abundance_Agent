5. Recheck similar modules across tissue pairs and revisit affected assignments whenever a category changes. Each module receives one principal theme and one category.

Use member composition, biological coherence and supporting evidence together. Select the functional category based on the principal biological theme, not the strength of annotation support. Do not create a functional category defined by nonsignificant enrichment, missing interactions, evidence weakness, or heterogeneity. Such properties belong in the explanation or evidence-gap record. A nonsignificant module with an interpretable program remains eligible for that program's functional category.
For the principal theme, identify the group of members supporting it and explain why their functions belong together. A network's highest-degree protein alone is insufficient to determine the entire module's function. When evidence genuinely supports no module-level interpretation, record the specific evidence gap instead of fabricating a theme. No target proportion is prescribed for unresolved assignments.
Cellular origin and possible contamination are composition interpretations, not demonstrated findings. Describe what proteins are present; keep any source hypotheses separate from the category's biological definition.
 Enrichment and interaction annotations contribute to the interpretation alongside the member functions. Describe inferred mechanisms as hypotheses. When source evidence is insufficient to make an assignment, identify the module and the specific information needed in the coverage record.
