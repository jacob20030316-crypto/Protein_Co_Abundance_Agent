# Conserved co-abundance modules: breast and lung

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: breast and lung  
**Genes (10)**: CP, COL1A1, COL1A2, C3, COL6A1, COL6A2, COL6A3, HBA1, LUM, BGN

### Function

This is a conserved extracellular-matrix and collagen-architecture module. The primary evidence is the coherent enrichment of collagen trimers and collagen complexes (GO:CC collagen trimer; q_global = 4.85e-07), (GO:CC complex of collagen trimers; q_global = 1.26e-06), extracellular-matrix structural constituents (GO:MF extracellular matrix structural constituent; q_global = 3.21e-06), (GO:MF extracellular matrix structural constituent conferring tensile strength; q_global = 3.27e-06), external encapsulating structure (GO:CC external encapsulating structure; q_global = 6.98e-06), extracellular matrix (GO:CC extracellular matrix; q_global = 6.98e-06), collagen fibril organization (GO:BP collagen fibril organization; q_global = 0.002), and interstitial matrix (GO:CC collagenous component of interstitial matrix; q_global = 0.0025), (GO:CC solid phase of interstitial matrix; q_global = 0.0381), (GO:CC interstitial matrix; q_global = 0.0466). The module contains coordinated type I collagen genes, type VI collagen genes, the matrix proteoglycans LUM and BGN, and a smaller complement and plasma-associated component involving C3 and CP. The integrin-signaling, ECM-receptor interaction, focal-adhesion, and PI3K-Akt terms (KEGG Integrin signaling - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 5.35e-05), (KEGG ECM-receptor interaction - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000209), (KEGG PI3K-Akt signaling pathway - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00182), (KEGG Focal adhesion - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00226) support a role in cell-matrix adhesion and matrix-dependent signaling rather than merely passive protein secretion. The protein-digestion-and-absorption term (KEGG Protein digestion and absorption - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 2.4e-05) is best interpreted as a collagen-related annotation overlap, not as evidence that the module performs a digestive function. The extracellular-vesicle and vesicle terms (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.026–0.0304; see the table) indicate that many module products can occur extracellularly or in vesicle-associated material, but they do not supersede the dominant collagen and stromal interpretation.

**Cell composition.** The module is strongly dominated by secreted extracellular-matrix proteins: COL1A1, COL1A2, COL6A1, COL6A2, COL6A3, LUM, and BGN are secreted to or associated with extracellular matrix, while C3 and CP are secreted to blood. HPA single-cell evidence repeatedly links the collagen and proteoglycan genes to fibroblasts, stromal cells, fibro-adipogenic progenitors, hepatic stellate cells, and related mesenchymal populations. Most genes are not detected as immune-cell enriched, although C3 is immune-cell enriched, LUM is associated with basophils, COL6A2 has group-enriched T-cell signals, and HBA1 is erythrocyte and blood-cell associated. CP and C3 are liver-enriched and blood-secreted, and HBA1 is a likely blood or erythrocyte-associated signal. Thus this is not primarily a housekeeping module or a conventional blood-cell module, but its shared detection in breast and lung could partly reflect common stromal or fibroblast content, extracellular matrix abundance, vascular material, and admixture of circulating plasma proteins. HPA protein detection is limited in both breast and lung, so lack of tissue immunostaining should not be treated as evidence that the module is absent at the protein level.

### STRING and hu.MAP

COL1A1 and COL1A2 are strongly connected to one another and to the COL6 chains, LUM, and BGN; the COL6 chains are mutually connected; and BGN connects the collagen and proteoglycan components. These relationships are consistent with known collagen assembly, fibril organization, and matrix proteoglycan biology. ### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a newly discovered collagen pathway or a previously unknown protein complex. Its central relationships, especially the COL1A1-COL1A2 pair, the COL6 chain assembly, and links to LUM and BGN, are established and are supported by STRING, hu.MAP, functional annotations, and extensive literature. Absence of a relationship from hu.MAP or absence of a named STRING complex would not prove novelty. The potentially new observation is the reproducible co-abundance of this particular extracellular-matrix and plasma-associated combination across breast and lung, together with its module-level organization in the analyzed dataset. That observation remains provisional: it could represent a shared fibroblast or stromal program, matrix abundance, vascular material, or plasma contamination rather than a novel molecular complex. Demonstrating novelty would require replication in independent breast and lung cohorts, cell-composition adjustment, comparison with existing tissue proteomic co-abundance modules, and validation that the C3-CP and collagen components remain coordinated within relevant cell populations.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared mesenchymal extracellular-matrix program in breast and lung in which fibroblast or related stromal cells produce type I and type VI collagen, while LUM and BGN organize collagen fibrils and regulate matrix spacing. The resulting matrix may influence integrin, focal-adhesion, and growth-factor signaling (KEGG Integrin signaling - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 5.35e-05), (KEGG ECM-receptor interaction - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000209), (GO:MF platelet-derived growth factor binding; q_global = 0.00172), (KEGG PI3K-Akt signaling pathway - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00182), (KEGG Focal adhesion - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00226). C3 and CP may represent a coupled extracellular-fluid or local complement component that links matrix remodeling with innate inflammatory signaling, whereas HBA1 may reflect erythrocyte or vascular admixture. This hypothesis predicts that module abundance will track fibroblast or stromal-cell abundance and matrix deposition, and that the complement component may vary with vascular or inflammatory content rather than being an obligatory part of the collagen-producing cell program.

It contributes to globally significant extracellular-space, extracellular-region, endoplasmic-reticulum-lumen, integrin-signaling, and extracellular-vesicle annotations (GO:CC endoplasmic reticulum lumen; q_global = 3.08e-05) (KEGG Integrin signaling - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 5.35e-05) (KEGG ECM-receptor interaction - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000209), (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.00478), (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.01), (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.026–0.0304; see the table). HPA identifies C3 as liver-enriched, blood-secreted, and immune-cell enriched in a monocyte population, while also associating it with alveolar type two cells and stromal-related cell types. Its central role in complement activation and its extensive disease literature make it important for interpreting the non-collagen component.
- **Conclusion**: Established evidence supports C3 as a complement and extracellular-fluid signal that may connect inflammation with the matrix-rich module. It should not be assumed to be a core fibroblast structural gene; its co-abundance may reflect local complement activity, plasma contribution, or cellular admixture. A direct functional coupling between C3 and collagen organization is a hypothesis requiring targeted validation.
