# Conserved co-abundance modules: ovary and rectum

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: ovary and rectum  
**Genes (10)**: SLC3A2, AGPS, HSD17B4, DNAJC10, MMGT1, ETFA, OCIAD2, ALDH18A1, YARS2, TFRC

### Function

No GO/KEGG term passed global correction, and STRING supplied no functional enrichment. The gene-level annotations nevertheless indicate a coherent but heterogeneous organellar-metabolic theme: peroxisomal fatty-acid and ether-lipid metabolism through HSD17B4 and AGPS, mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation and electron transfer through ETFA, mitochondrial amino-acid and proline-related metabolism through ALDH18A1, mitochondrial translation through YARS2, and endoplasmic-reticulum protein quality control through DNAJC10 and MMGT1. TFRC and SLC3A2 add iron uptake and amino-acid transport. The network therefore appears to capture coordinated cellular metabolism, membrane and organelle homeostasis, and protein-handling activity rather than a narrowly defined pathway. These interpretations are functional hypotheses from member annotations, not statistically enriched GO/KEGG findings.

**Cell composition.** The module is not predominantly composed of secreted or plasma proteins: only TFRC is annotated as secreted to blood, while the remaining genes are intracellular or membrane-associated. HPA indicates generally low immune-cell specificity, with immune-cell enhancement for TFRC and HSD17B4, so a blood-cell explanation is possible for part of the signal but is not the dominant interpretation. The genes show mixed tissue enhancement, including liver, kidney, salivary gland, tongue, and epididymis, and strong but diverse cell-type enhancement rather than a single shared cell identity. Protein detection is much more frequent in rectum than ovary, whereas TFRC, MMGT1, and ETFA are detected in both tissues; SLC3A2 is not detected in either tissue. The module may therefore reflect shared metabolically active or organelle-rich cells, technical or compositional effects, or a conserved cellular program rather than a tissue-specific ovary-rectum pathway.

**GO/KEGG enrichment**: 0 terms pass global BH (q_global < 0.05).

### STRING and hu.MAP

There are no high-confidence STRING interactions among the module genes and no STRING functional enrichment. Thus, the co-abundance edges should not be interpreted as established physical interactions or as a validated protein complex. hu.MAP provides one partial complex connection, (hu.MAP huMAP3_10725.1), containing TFRC and MMGT1 together, but the complex is much broader than this module and most of its members are outside the module. This supports a limited known relationship involving iron-related trafficking and membrane or ER organization, while the remaining module structure is not explained by currently catalogued STRING or hu.MAP relationships.

**STRING interactions (score >= 0.7)**: 0 of 45 gene pairs.
**hu.MAP 3.0 complexes with >= 2 module members**: huMAP3_10725.1 (TFRC, MMGT1; 2/59)

### Novelty

The shared co-abundance pattern may be a new tissue-pair association because it is not accompanied by a dense set of previously catalogued STRING interactions or by a module-sized hu.MAP complex. However, absence from those resources does not establish biological novelty: co-abundance can arise from shared cell composition, organelle abundance, metabolic state, sample handling, or indirect regulation, and the genes have individually established functions and disease associations. The defensible conclusion is that the coordinated ovary-rectum module is a potentially novel systems-level observation that requires replication, cell-composition adjustment, and independent molecular validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a conserved organelle and metabolic state shared by selected cells in ovary and rectum, in which peroxisomal lipid processing and mitochondrial fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism are coordinated with ER quality control, membrane insertion, iron uptake, and amino-acid transport. TFRC and MMGT1 may connect membrane or trafficking biology to this state, while the apparent module-level coherence could also be generated by shared cell types or organelle abundance rather than direct regulation among the proteins.

The module is best interpreted as a broadly connected intracellular gene-expression and RNA-management module. The overlap across these terms indicates coordinated activity in RNA binding, RNA processing, ribonucleoprotein organization, translation-related functions, RNA surveillance, and associated protein-complex turnover. The gene-level annotations support this interpretation: ADAR edits RNA, DHX9 unwinds RNA and DNA-RNA complexes, HNRNPR participates in spliceosomal RNA processing, SF3B3 is part of the U2 spliceosome, U2SURP is involved in RNA processing, UPF1 supports mRNA export and nonsense-mediated decay, EPRS1 and IARS1 support translation through tRNA aminoacylation, and RPL12 and RPL9 are ribosomal proteins. **Cell composition.** This is not a plasma or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and the proteins are predominantly intracellular, with cytosolic, nucleoplasmic, nucleolar, or related locations. All module genes have low tissue specificity, and most have low immune-cell specificity; one gene is not detected in immune cells. Therefore, the shared ovary-rectum signal is more consistent with a broadly expressed housekeeping-type program than with a blood-cell or plasma-protein signature. HPA protein detection is present for nearly all genes in ovary and all genes in rectum, supporting technical detectability in both tissues. A subset of genes shows cell-type-enhanced patterns, including esophageal epithelial cells, reproductive cell populations, syncytiotrophoblasts, erythrocyte progenitors, and neutrophils, but there is no dominant enriched blood-cell pattern. Shared cell states or common cellular requirements could therefore contribute, but the available evidence does not identify a specific infiltrating blood-cell origin.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular pathway. Many of its central relationships and functions are already established in STRING, hu.MAP, HPA, and the gene annotations, especially RNA processing, RNA surveillance, translation, ribosome function, and proteasome activity. Its potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance of these otherwise distinct functional components across ovary and rectum. That cross-tissue organization may reflect a shared regulatory or cellular-state program, but the FACT SHEET does not provide an external comparison against previous co-abundance modules, tissue-pair analyses, or published network studies. Consequently, novelty remains provisional: the module is a potentially new tissue-pair-level organization of known biology, not evidence of a new gene function or new physical complex. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship is not sufficient to establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved ovary-rectum module represents a coordinated intracellular quality-control and gene-expression state in which transcription and chromatin access feed RNA processing, editing, splicing, export, translation, and surveillance, with proteasomal turnover maintaining protein quality. ADAR, DHX9, HNRNPR, SF3B3, and U2SURP could coordinate RNA maturation and remodeling; UPF1 could couple processed transcripts to export and nonsense-mediated decay; EPRS1, IARS1, RPL12, and RPL9 could support translation; and PSMC2 and PSMC6 could remove damaged or obsolete proteins. CAD, ACTL6A, and SUPT16H may reflect coupling to nucleotide supply, chromatin remodeling, and transcriptional elongation. This is a mechanistic hypothesis inferred from co-abundance and annotations, not a demonstrated causal pathway; it should be tested with perturbation, interaction assays, RNA-processing measurements, and cell-composition controls in both tissues.

### Novelty

The biological components are not new: the CCT chaperonin, spliceosome, RNA helicases, and RUVBL2-associated nuclear functions are established. What may be new is the conserved co-abundance configuration shared by ovary and rectum, particularly the way a CCT-centered subnetwork is coupled to spliceosomal, RNA-binding, translation-related, and membrane-trafficking genes. However, the fact sheet does not establish that this exact module has not appeared in another cohort or analysis. Lack of STRING or hu.MAP support for some edges is evidence of incomplete prior interaction knowledge, not proof of novelty. The defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance pattern assembled from largely established molecular machinery, rather than a newly discovered molecular function.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared ovary–rectum module reflects a conserved cellular state in which CCT-mediated folding and stabilization supports RNA-processing and nuclear-body machinery, including spliceosomal components and RUVBL2-linked telomere or chromatin functions. In reproductive or ciliated-cell contexts, this state may be intensified by the cell-type expression patterns of CCT5, CCT7, CCT8, and RUVBL2. The observed co-abundance could therefore coordinate proteostasis with maturation, localization, and surveillance of structured RNAs and their associated proteins. This hypothesis predicts that module activity should track CCT complex abundance, spliceosome function, and telomere or Cajal-body phenotypes, but the co-abundance data alone do not demonstrate causal regulation or direct assembly of the full module.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new discovery: the MCM complex, CMG complex, replication initiation, FEN1-mediated DNA processing, and RFC-associated replication functions are established. The module also substantially recapitulates known STRING relationships and hu.MAP complexes, which argues against claiming a newly discovered molecular complex. The appropriate conclusion is that the module is a biologically credible tissue-shared replication-associated module with a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance context, but not a demonstrated new replication complex or pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved ovary-rectum module reports the abundance of actively cycling, progenitor-like or otherwise proliferative cells shared between the tissues. PSPC1 and TXLNA may represent a peripheral nuclear or cytosolic co-abundance connection rather than core replication machinery. This remains a hypothesis: co-abundance cannot by itself establish cell proliferation, physical recruitment, causal regulation, or replication stress in either tissue.
