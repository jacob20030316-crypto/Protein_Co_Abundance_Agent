# Conserved co-abundance modules: circulatory_system and rectum

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: circulatory_system and rectum  
**Genes (14)**: A1BG, SERPINC1, APOH, C1R, C1S, CA1, CP, CFH, C7, FUCA1, HPX, HP, KNG1, PLG

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating extracellular protease-regulation and innate-defense module. The strongest primary evidence is blood microparticle association (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 1.98e-06), complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 1.89e-05), extracellular localization (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.00335), proteolysis and serine-type peptidase activity (GO:BP proteolysis; q_global = 0.00738)(GO:MF serine-type endopeptidase activity; q_global = 0.0112), regulation of coagulation and hemostasis (GO:BP regulation of blood coagulation; q_global = 0.0189)(GO:BP regulation of hemostasis; q_global = 0.0189)(GO:BP regulation of coagulation; q_global = 0.0294), humoral immune response (GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.0256)(GO:BP humoral immune response mediated by circulating immunoglobulin; q_global = 0.0294), and complement activation (GO:BP complement activation; q_global = 0.038). The complete extracellular-region overlap (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.00335) and near-complete extracellular-space overlap (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.021) indicate that this is primarily a secreted or extracellular protein program rather than an intracellular rectal program. The Staphylococcus aureus infection term (KEGG Staphylococcus aureus infection - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0221) is consistent with complement-mediated host defense, but should not be interpreted as evidence that the module specifically represents infection. Overall, the module combines classical complement components, complement regulation, coagulation and fibrinolysis, kinin biology, heme handling, and plasma protein transport.

**Cell composition.** The module is dominated by plasma and secreted proteins. HPA identifies most members as secreted to blood or otherwise extracellular, and the STRING annotations independently emphasize plasma proteins, blood microparticles, signal peptides, glycoproteins, and secreted proteins (STRING PMID (2022) Construction of 2DE Patterns of Plasma Proteins: Aspect of Potential Tumor Markers.; within-query FDR = 2.39e-12)(STRING Component Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 4.38e-09)(STRING COMPARTMENTS Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 4.67e-09)(STRING Keyword Signal; within-query FDR = 1.05e-08)(STRING Keyword Glycoprotein; within-query FDR = 4.26e-08)(STRING Keyword Secreted; within-query FDR = 1.07e-07). The HPA profile is strongly liver-enriched and hepatocyte-associated, while most genes are not detected as immune-cell specific. This argues against interpreting the module as a resident rectal epithelial program or a conventional blood-cell transcriptional program. Its recurrence in circulatory_system and rectum may instead reflect plasma exposure, blood-derived material, extracellular vesicles, vascular or stromal interfaces, or a shared systemic component present in both tissue datasets. CA1 and FUCA1 provide a more tissue-associated edge, including rectal detection and colonocyte or enterocyte-related cell annotations, but they are peripheral to the dominant liver-derived plasma pattern. HPA therefore supports a sample-composition or shared extracellular-milieu explanation as an important alternative to tissue-specific biology.

### Novelty

This module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular discovery. Its dominant signal is already well represented by known complement, coagulation, fibrinolysis, kinin, plasma-protein, and blood-microparticle biology in STRING and hu.MAP. The presence of many liver-derived secreted proteins also makes a shared plasma or sample-composition signal biologically plausible. There may nevertheless be novelty in the specific co-abundance configuration across circulatory_system and rectum, particularly in how the peripheral members such as CA1 and FUCA1 are connected to the plasma network. However, absence from a reference interaction database would not prove novelty, and the available evidence does not establish that this tissue-pair configuration is a previously unknown molecular complex. The defensible conclusion is a potentially useful cross-tissue co-abundance observation built largely from established biology.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared circulating extracellular-protein pool enters or is sampled by both circulatory_system and rectum, producing coordinated abundance of liver-derived complement regulators, protease zymogens, anticoagulants, heme-binding proteins, and plasma glycoproteins. In the rectum, this signal may be reinforced at vascular, stromal, or epithelial interfaces and by blood microparticles. The module could therefore represent a homeostatic interface that balances complement activation, coagulation, fibrinolysis, kinin signaling, and oxidative or heme-related stress. This hypothesis predicts that the module will track plasma contamination or vascular exposure and systemic inflammatory or hemostatic state more strongly than rectum-specific cellular identity. It requires validation with cell-resolved measurements, plasma-depletion controls, and independent tissue cohorts.

### Function

The module is most consistent with a contractile smooth-muscle and cell–matrix adhesion program. Primary enrichment supports cytoskeleton in muscle cells (KEGG Cytoskeleton in muscle cells - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 1.63e-06), myofibril and contractile muscle fiber (GO:CC myofibril; q_global = 2.3e-06) (GO:CC contractile muscle fiber; q_global = 2.72e-06), sarcomere (GO:CC sarcomere; q_global = 0.000142), muscle contraction (GO:BP muscle contraction; q_global = 0.00151), actin binding (GO:MF actin binding; q_global = 0.00203), focal adhesion (GO:CC focal adhesion; q_global = 0.000865) (KEGG Focal adhesion - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00251), integrin signaling (KEGG Integrin signaling - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.028), and cell–matrix adhesion (GO:BP cell-matrix adhesion; q_global = 0.0405). Wound healing, morphogenesis, stress fibers, and platelet aggregation (6 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 7.21e-05–0.0406; see the table) indicate that the program also links force generation to adhesion, remodeling, and hemostatic cell behavior. The coherent combination of actin–myosin machinery, integrin-associated proteins, focal-adhesion components, and extracellular-matrix interaction is stronger than any isolated annotation. The supplementary STRING results independently reinforce myofibril, sarcomere, smooth-muscle contraction, muscle development, and focal-adhesion interpretations (6 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 8.28e-06–0.0003).

**Cell composition.** This is not primarily a plasma-protein module: only a minority of genes are secreted, including F13A1, COL4A2, and OGN, while most proteins are intracellular, membrane-associated, or cytoskeletal. HPA shows a strong smooth-muscle and vascular pattern, with frequent enrichment in smooth muscle cells, vascular smooth muscle cells, hepatic stellate cells, and related contractile or stromal populations. Several genes are also associated with megakaryocytes, platelets, neutrophils, basophils, eosinophils, and other blood-cell populations. The shared circulatory-system and rectum signal could therefore partly reflect vascular smooth muscle, perivascular or stromal cells, and shared blood-associated cell types rather than a function unique to either tissue. The rectum protein evidence is broadly compatible with the module, whereas the absence of matching circulatory-system tissue protein data limits direct tissue-level comparison. HPA also indicates that most genes have enhanced cell-type specificity, so this does not look like a generic housekeeping module.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new functional concept: smooth-muscle contraction, sarcomeric actin organization, integrin signaling, focal adhesion, and matrix attachment are established processes, and many module relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel observation is the coordinated co-abundance of this contractile–adhesion program across circulatory_system and rectum. That cross-tissue organization may be useful as a systems-level finding, but the fact sheet does not establish tissue-specific causality, cellular purity, or independence from shared vascular, stromal, smooth-muscle, megakaryocyte, or platelet composition. Therefore, this should be described as a plausible cross-tissue conserved module rather than a definitively new molecular mechanism. Lack of database representation would not prove novelty, and here database support is substantial.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved force-transmission and tissue-remodeling program shared by vascular or visceral smooth-muscle and stromal compartments in circulatory_system and rectum. Integrin receptors, extracellular matrix, ILK–LIMS1–VCL adhesion machinery, and actin crosslinkers may couple matrix attachment to actomyosin contraction through MYLK, MYL9, CALD1, TPM1, and TPM2. This could support vascular or intestinal wall tone, cellular migration, wound repair, and platelet-associated adhesion. The hypothesis predicts that the module will be strongest in samples with smooth-muscle, vascular, stromal, or platelet-related representation; it does not establish that every member is expressed by the same cell or that the co-abundance reflects direct regulation.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to be a newly discovered complement pathway: its central genes, interactions, pathway annotations, and hu.MAP associations are already well established. The most conservative conclusion is that this is a biologically coherent, previously known plasma-complement program observed in a particular cross-tissue context, with a potentially informative but unproven tissue-pair signature.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: variation in the abundance of liver-derived circulating complement proteins creates the shared module signal in both circulatory-system and rectal samples. C3 and factor B may represent alternative-pathway activation, factor I may provide counter-regulation, and the C8 subunits may represent the terminal membrane-attack-complex arm. PGLYRP2 could connect antibacterial peptidoglycan processing with complement-associated defense, while EXOC7, TXN, and BLVRB may reflect local epithelial, vesicular, redox, or blood-associated covariation. This hypothesis predicts that the module will track plasma contamination or vascular exposure and complement activity more consistently than rectum-specific transcription; it requires validation with matched plasma measurements, cell-resolved expression, and protein-level localization.
