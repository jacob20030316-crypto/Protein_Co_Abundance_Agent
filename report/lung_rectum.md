The strongest interpretation is neutral amino-acid transport and cellular nutrient handling, supported by the sole globally corrected GO term (GO:MF neutral L-amino acid transmembrane transporter activity; q_global = 0.0228), involving SLC1A5, SLC25A13 and SLC3A2. The module also contains an RNA-processing and RNA-export strand centered on HNRNPK, PTBP1, FUS, PSPC1, XPO4 and XPO5; a mitochondrial organization strand involving PHB1, PHB2, TIMM50 and SLC25A13; and an ER protein-folding and trafficking strand involving HYOU1, UGGT1, SCFD1 and SEC24C. Overall, the module appears to combine nutrient transport, RNA handling, mitochondrial maintenance and proteostasis rather than representing a single canonical pathway.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein-dominated module. HPA reports low tissue specificity for almost all genes, low immune-cell specificity for nearly all genes, and only a small minority of secreted genes. Protein detection is generally compatible with both lung and rectum, although SLC1A5 and SLC3A2 are not detected in the supplied lung and rectum protein records, while HYOU1 is detected in rectum but not lung. The composition therefore does not strongly suggest blood contamination or a blood-cell-driven module. However, many genes are cell-type enhanced, so shared epithelial or other cellular-state composition could contribute. SLC25A13 is liver-enriched and XPO4 is skeletal-muscle-enhanced, but these isolated annotations do not define the module.

### Novelty

The exact conserved lung--rectum co-abundance organization may be a new systems-level observation, but it should not be described as a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. Several central proteins, interactions and complexes are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the genes have substantial prior functional and disease literature. The potentially novel feature is the cross-tissue co-abundance connection among amino-acid transport, RNA metabolism, mitochondrial function and ER proteostasis. This remains provisional because the supplied evidence does not include independent replication, comparison with published co-abundance modules, or perturbation experiments. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone would not establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared lung and rectum cellular populations, SLC1A5--SLC3A2-mediated amino-acid uptake and SLC25A13-mediated mitochondrial substrate exchange are coordinated with RNA processing, nuclear export, mitochondrial maintenance and ER protein-quality control. HYOU1 and UGGT1 could support folding and quality control, PHB1 and PHB2 could support mitochondrial organization, and the HNRNPK--PTBP1--FUS--PSPC1 group could regulate RNA fate. This may represent a general nutrient-stress or proteostasis state rather than a blood-cell or secreted-protein signature. The hypothesis requires cell-resolved measurements, perturbation experiments and replication in both tissues.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and the individual annotations are predominantly intracellular, especially mitochondrial or peroxisomal. Protein detection is broadly compatible with both lung and rectum, although AGPS was detected in rectum but not lung and POLDIP2 had no reported IHC detection. ### Novelty

The mitochondrial biology represented by this module is not new: PNPT1, GFM1, DARS2, TRAP1 and ALDH18A1 have established mitochondrial functions, and parts of the module recur in hu.MAP complexes and STRING relationships. The potentially new result is the conserved co-abundance organization shared by lung and rectum, including genes that are not connected by the available prior-interaction resources. That claim should remain provisional: absence from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty, and co-abundance does not prove direct interaction. The appropriate conclusion is a potentially novel cross-tissue systems-level association built from largely established mitochondrial components.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated control of mitochondrial gene expression, RNA quality control, translation and metabolic resilience across lung and rectum. PNPT1-mediated mitochondrial RNA handling could support mitochondrial translation together with DARS2, GFM1, MRPL45 and GTPBP10, while TRAP1 could buffer proteotoxic or oxidative stress. ALDH18A1 and AGPS could connect mitochondrial performance to amino-acid and lipid metabolism, and MAVS could couple mitochondrial state to antiviral signaling. POLDIP2, TBRG4, SEC63 and NUP210 may represent linked stress, organelle-interface or cellular-state effects rather than a single physical complex. This model predicts coordinated changes in mitochondrial RNA processing, translation and respiratory competence, but the co-abundance data alone cannot establish directionality, causality, or direct molecular binding.

### Novelty

The biological function is not new in a general sense: the collagen, laminin, proteoglycan, nidogen, and integrin-related relationships are established and are represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel result is the conservation of this particular co-abundance configuration across lung and rectum, including the way the fibrillar-matrix branch, basement-membrane branch, and vascular or contractile branch co-vary. However, a new network topology is not sufficient to claim a new mechanism. Novelty would require replication in independent cohorts, control for fibroblast, pericyte, smooth-muscle, and vascular-cell abundance, and experimental or perturbational testing of the proposed cross-branch relationships. The absence of a complete hu.MAP complex or a STRING edge for some genes is evidence of limited prior annotation, not proof of novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared stromal or perivascular cell population in lung and rectum produces a coordinated matrix-remodeling program. Type I collagen and collagen VI form the fibrillar and beaded structural scaffold, while decorin, lumican, biglycan, and fibrillin organize fibrils and microfibrils. Laminin chains, nidogen, and perlecan assemble and anchor a basement-membrane layer. These matrix components engage integrin-linked adhesion and PI3K-Akt signaling, while MYH11, TNS1, CAVIN proteins, and related membrane or cytoskeletal genes reflect the mechanical response of vascular or smooth-muscle-associated cells. The co-abundance module may therefore report coordinated matrix deposition, matrix-cell adhesion, and tissue mechanical support rather than a single intracellular pathway. This remains a hypothesis because co-abundance and database interactions do not demonstrate directionality, secretion by the same cell, or causal signaling.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular complex or as novel gene function. Many of its relationships are already established by STRING and hu.MAP, and its dominant processes are canonical cellular functions. Its potentially novel aspect is the reproducible conservation of a combined aminoacylation, RNA-processing, translation, proteostasis, and chromatin-associated program across lung and rectum. That interpretation remains a systems-level observation rather than proof of a new mechanism. Absence of a particular edge from STRING or absence of a gene from a hu.MAP complex would not establish novelty, because databases are incomplete and the co-abundance relationship may be indirect or condition-dependent. Independent replication, control for cell composition, and comparison with additional tissues would be required before claiming a new conserved module.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a coordinated intracellular resource-allocation program in which transcriptional and chromatin-associated RNA production is coupled to RNA processing, spliceosome function, aminoacyl-tRNA charging, translation initiation, protein folding, and proteasomal quality control. In lung and rectum, shared cellular states or epithelial activity may increase these processes together, producing the conserved co-abundance pattern. The proposed coupling between the spliceosomal arm and the aminoacylation and translation arms is mechanistic and plausible, but it is not demonstrated by co-abundance alone. It should be tested using perturbation of representative genes, measurement of splicing and translational output, and adjustment for cell-type composition.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not new: replication, mismatch repair, double-strand break repair, chromatin remodeling, RNA processing, and nuclear transport are established functions of the constituent genes. The module-level observation may nevertheless be a new integrative finding if this particular co-abundance pattern is reproducibly conserved between lung and rectum. Its novelty would lie in the cross-tissue coordination of these nuclear programs and in the specific combination of replication, repair, RNA processing, pore, and purine-synthesis components, not in discovery of a new function for any individual gene. The partial STRING and hu.MAP coverage argues that some relationships are already known, while the incomplete physical-network coverage leaves room for tissue-contextual or regulatory co-abundance relationships. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone cannot establish novelty, so the appropriate conclusion is a potentially novel tissue-pair co-abundance module with largely established molecular components, requiring independent replication and perturbation experiments for a stronger novelty claim.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared lung-rectum module reflects a coordinated nuclear maintenance state in which proliferative or stressed cells couple DNA replication with replication-fork surveillance, mismatch and double-strand-break repair, chromatin remodeling, RNA maturation, and regulated nuclear transport. MCM2, MCM7, and PCNA could mark replication-associated activity; MSH2 and MRE11 could provide mismatch and double-strand-break surveillance; SMARCA4 could connect DNA transactions to chromatin accessibility; DDX42, DDX46, CSTF3, and SRRT could coordinate RNA processing; and NUP93, NUP155, NUP205, and KPNA2 could support exchange across the nuclear envelope. GART and GMPS may supply purine-nucleotide capacity for nucleic-acid synthesis. This is a mechanistic model rather than a demonstrated physical assembly: testing should determine whether the module tracks proliferation, replication stress, DNA damage, or cell-state composition in matched lung and rectum samples.

### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a wholly new molecular complex. Its principal components and many relationships are already known: nucleolar ribosome-biogenesis machinery is supported by STRING, the GO annotations, and established gene functions, and the MCM submodule is directly represented in hu.MAP. The potentially novel finding is the conserved co-abundance organization of these established components across lung and rectum, including their joint association with a replication-linked state and the particular bridges connecting the nucleolar and MCM portions. That is a systems-level observation rather than evidence for a new biochemical interaction. Absence of a gene or pair from hu.MAP or STRING cannot establish novelty, because those resources are incomplete and emphasize experimentally characterized complexes. Confirmation would require replication in independent cohorts, control for cell composition and proliferation, and preferably orthogonal protein-level or perturbation evidence.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared lung-rectum module represents a conserved proliferative epithelial or progenitor-associated state in which increased demand for protein synthesis is coordinated with ribosome assembly and genome duplication. In this model, the nucleolar network centered on DDX21, NOP56, PES1, RRS1, NOP2, PDCD11, and related factors supports rRNA maturation and ribosomal subunit production, while the MCM4-MCM5-MCM6 group supports replication initiation. RSL1D1 and RRS1 may help connect the nucleolar and chromosome-associated portions of the module. This is a mechanistic interpretation of co-abundance and annotation concordance, not proof that these proteins physically form one unified complex or that any selected gene causally drives the tissue-level state.
