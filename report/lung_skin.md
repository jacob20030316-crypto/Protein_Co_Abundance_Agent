### Function

This is a mitochondrial bioenergetics and gene-expression module. The strongest primary evidence is for mitochondrial gene expression (GO:BP mitochondrial gene expression; q_global = 1.08e-09), mitochondrial translation (GO:BP mitochondrial translation; q_global = 1.03e-06), mitochondrial ribosomes and ribosomal subunits (KEGG Ribosome - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 4.36e-07)(GO:CC organellar ribosome; q_global = 4.63e-07)(GO:CC mitochondrial ribosome; q_global = 4.63e-07)(GO:CC ribosome; q_global = 3.27e-06)(GO:CC ribosomal subunit; q_global = 6.95e-06)(GO:CC organellar small ribosomal subunit; q_global = 0.00179)(GO:CC mitochondrial small ribosomal subunit; q_global = 0.00179)(GO:MF structural constituent of ribosome; q_global = 0.00527)(GO:CC small ribosomal subunit; q_global = 0.00609)(GO:CC organellar large ribosomal subunit; q_global = 0.0359)(GO:CC mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit; q_global = 0.0359), mitochondrial membranes and protein-containing complexes (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 1.12e-09)(GO:CC mitochondrial inner membrane; q_global = 1.99e-08)(GO:CC mitochondrial protein-containing complex; q_global = 2.43e-08)(GO:CC organelle inner membrane; q_global = 6.94e-08)(GO:CC mitochondrial envelope; q_global = 5.58e-07)(GO:CC mitochondrial membrane; q_global = 1.01e-06), and mitochondrial RNA processing (GO:BP mitochondrial RNA processing; q_global = 0.0462). The module also contains respiratory-chain and electron-transfer components (GO:BP mitochondrion organization; q_global = 0.0198)(GO:MF electron transfer activity; q_global = 0.0317), supporting a coordinated program linking mitochondrial RNA handling and translation to oxidative phosphorylation. The network topology is consistent with this interpretation: a highly connected central gene links a dense mitoribosomal translation cluster, a complex-I assembly and electron-transfer cluster, and a smaller mitochondrial RNA-processing group. Translation and protein biosynthesis terms (GO:BP translation; q_global = 0.0365)(GO:BP protein biosynthetic process; q_global = 0.0365) should be interpreted mainly as mitochondrial translation in this context.

**Cell composition.** The module is not predominantly a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module, and it is not strongly blood-cell enriched. HPA reports only a small secreted component, while most genes have low immune-cell specificity; therefore blood contamination is not the leading explanation. Many genes have low tissue specificity and the module is detected at the protein level in both lung and skin, consistent with a broad cellular mitochondrial program rather than a lung- or skin-exclusive pathway. The tissue-enhanced signals are distributed across muscle, tongue, adipose tissue, heart muscle and other tissues, while single-cell enrichment is spread across several epithelial, neural, reproductive and muscle-associated cell types. This pattern leaves open a contribution from shared cell types or tissue composition, but does not support a simple immune or circulating-protein explanation.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial function itself is not a new biological mechanism: the component genes, STRING relationships and hu.MAP complexes already document mitoribosomes, mitochondrial RNA metabolism, complex-I assembly and cristae organization. The potentially novel aspect is the conservation of this co-abundance program across lung and skin and the way these established mitochondrial subsystems are linked in one tissue-pair module. That observation cannot by itself establish a new disease mechanism or a new molecular interaction. Novelty remains provisional because it would require replication in independent cohorts, control for cell composition and technical effects, and comparison with existing tissue co-expression modules. The absence of an individual gene from STRING or hu.MAP would not, by itself, demonstrate novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved lung-skin module reflects coordinated maintenance of mitochondrial capacity in shared metabolically active or epithelial-associated cell populations. A central mitochondrial assembly or translation-linked regulator may couple mitoribosome production and mitochondrial RNA quality control to complex-I assembly, respiratory electron transfer and cristae integrity. In this model, coordinated expression would help sustain mitochondrial protein synthesis and oxidative phosphorylation, while disruption of RNA processing, translation, complex-I assembly or cristae organization could produce a common vulnerability across lung and skin. This is a mechanistic model derived from co-abundance and annotation; it is not established causality.

Its central subnetworks correspond to established CCT, proteasome, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, nuclear-transport and purine-biosynthesis machinery, and these relationships are supported by STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of these otherwise distinct systems across lung and skin, including their particular coupling in this tissue pair. That is a systems-level observation, not proof of a new biochemical interaction. Absence of some genes from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because database coverage, evidence thresholds, indirect regulation and context-specific co-abundance can all produce missing edges. A genuine novelty claim would require replication in independent lung and skin cohorts, control for cell composition, comparison with existing tissue modules, and experimental testing of any proposed cross-complex mechanism.

### Novelty

The module may be a new cross-tissue co-abundance observation, but the biological processes and many pairwise relationships are established. ### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a wholly new molecular pathway. **Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a conserved nuclear state in which transcriptional output and RNA maturation are coordinated with chromatin assembly and genome maintenance in lung and skin cells. Its interpretation should therefore be framed as a potentially novel cross-tissue organization of largely established biology, not as discovery of a new ribosome-biogenesis complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both lung and skin, a shared cell-state or cellular-composition context coordinates nucleolar rRNA maturation, assembly of ribosomal subunits, RNA surveillance or remodeling, and protein synthesis.
