# Conserved co-abundance modules: breast and kidney

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: breast and kidney  
**Genes (6)**: ABRAXAS1, AKAP8, NELFE, NUP88, RBM15, ZC3HC1

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a nuclear regulatory co-abundance module rather than as a defined pathway. Its genes connect DNA-damage response and cell-cycle control through ABRAXAS1, RNA processing and RNA modification through RBM15, nucleocytoplasmic transport through NUP88, nuclear signaling and mitotic chromosome regulation through AKAP8, control of the G2/M transition through ZC3HC1, and transcriptional pausing through NELFE. ABRAXAS1 is the network hub and is connected to every other module gene, while the remaining genes form a hub-and-spoke structure with no additional connections. This organization supports coordinated nuclear regulation, but not a single established molecular complex or pathway. No GO or KEGG term passed global correction, so there is no globally significant pathway annotation for this module. The absence of a significant term does not invalidate the module.

**Cell composition.** The module is composed entirely of intracellular nuclear or nucleoplasmic proteins according to the HPA evidence: secreted proteins and blood-cell-enriched proteins are not represented. Most genes have low tissue specificity, and all have low immune-cell specificity, arguing against a module that is primarily explained by plasma proteins, circulating blood cells, or a strongly tissue-restricted lineage. One gene is tissue enhanced in bone marrow, while several genes show cell-type-enhanced or cell-type-enriched patterns in reproductive, epithelial, or other specialized cell populations. Protein detection is broadly concordant between breast and kidney for most module genes, although ABRAXAS1 is not detected by HPA immunohistochemistry in either tissue. The shared signal may therefore reflect broadly present nuclear biology, shared proliferative or transcriptional states, or mixtures of cell types rather than a breast-and-kidney-specific secretory program.

**GO/KEGG enrichment**: 0 terms pass global BH (q_global < 0.05).

### STRING and hu.MAP

No high-confidence STRING interaction was present among the module genes, and no STRING functional-enrichment result was reported. No hu.MAP complex contained multiple module members. Thus, the co-abundance relationships are not corroborated here by the tested high-confidence physical-interaction or complex resources. This does not disprove functional coordination: the genes participate in distinct nuclear processes, and co-abundance can reflect shared regulation, cell-state composition, or indirect coordination rather than direct binding. The strongest network relationship is the association of ABRAXAS1 with each of RBM15, NUP88, AKAP8, ZC3HC1, and NELFE, but this relationship should be treated as module-specific co-abundance evidence rather than an established physical interaction.

### Novelty

The module is potentially novel as a cross-tissue co-abundance pattern because it combines a BRCA1-associated DNA-repair factor with RNA regulation, nuclear transport, mitotic scaffolding, cell-cycle control, and transcriptional elongation factors, while neither STRING nor hu.MAP identifies a shared interaction framework. However, absence from those resources alone does not establish novelty. The genes and their individual functions are already established, and the network may reflect indirect shared regulation or common nuclear cell-state variation. A defensible claim is therefore that the specific breast-and-kidney co-abundance organization is a candidate novel observation requiring replication, rather than a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: the module reflects a coordinated nuclear state in which DNA-damage surveillance and repair, RNA maturation or modification, nucleocytoplasmic transport, transcriptional pausing, and mitotic entry are jointly regulated. ABRAXAS1 may mark the central co-regulated state because it is the hub and has established roles in BRCA1-associated repair and checkpoint control. In cells undergoing proliferation or responding to genomic stress, altered abundance of this hub could covary indirectly with RBM15, NUP88, AKAP8, ZC3HC1, and NELFE as nuclear transport, RNA handling, transcriptional elongation, and cell-cycle progression are adjusted. This is a hypothesis generated by the network and annotations, not an established mechanism demonstrated by the supplied evidence.

### Novelty

This is plausibly a new tissue-shared co-abundance observation, but it cannot be called a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. The module combines well-established genes and an established chromatin-binding theme, and its biological interpretation is therefore not wholly novel. What may be novel is the coordinated abundance of this particular gene set across breast and kidney. The lack of high-confidence STRING interactions and the absence of a multi-member hu.MAP complex support the distinctiveness of the network pattern, but repository absence alone cannot prove novelty. Independent replication, comparison with larger tissue cohorts, and testing against cell-composition and technical covariates would be needed before claiming a new conserved biological program.

### Novelty

The biological theme is not novel in the broad sense: integrin alpha-v-beta-three, cadherin, ALCAM, receptor phosphatase, and plasma-membrane adhesion biology are established. The ITGAV-ITGB3 relationship is already strongly represented in STRING, and PTPRJ-ALCAM co-membership recurs in hu.MAP complexes. What may be novel is the conserved co-abundance organization of this particular gene combination across breast and kidney, especially the coupling of the established integrin pair with ALCAM, CDH2, PTPRJ, membrane-repair or vesicle genes, and endoplasmic-reticulum genes. The available evidence is insufficient to call the module a new biological mechanism: database absence for several pairwise relationships does not prove novelty, and the HPA immune and cell-type signals provide an alternative explanation based on shared cellular composition. Novelty should therefore be stated as a testable network-level observation rather than a discovery of an unknown pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared vascular, epithelial, stromal, or immune-associated cell population in breast and kidney generates a coordinated membrane-adhesion program. In this model, the ITGAV-ITGB3 receptor provides an extracellular-matrix and signaling anchor; ALCAM and CDH2 stabilize cell-cell contacts; PTPRJ tunes receptor and adhesion signaling; and MYOF, IKBIP, and RRBP1 support membrane trafficking, repair, or secretory-cell-state requirements. The apparent links to foam-cell differentiation and lipid regulation may reflect integrin-mediated inflammatory or vascular context rather than a primary lipid-storage module. This hypothesis predicts that the module will track specific cell populations and adhesion states more strongly than bulk tissue identity, and that its co-abundance will weaken after rigorous adjustment for immune and vascular cell fractions.
