# Conserved co-abundance modules: kidney and ovary

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: kidney and ovary  
**Genes (10)**: SLC1A5, MTHFD1L, FAM3C, ICAM1, L1CAM, ALDH18A1, PVR, HM13, TFRC, VAPB

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a cell-surface interaction and adhesion module with a prominent host–microbe interface signature. The globally corrected evidence is concentrated on host interaction, symbiotic interaction, virus receptor activity, exogenous protein binding, viral processes, cell-surface localization, signaling receptor activity, molecular transducer activity, and CAM interaction (12 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00143–0.032; see the table). ICAM1, PVR, SLC1A5, and TFRC recur across most of these terms, while VAPB contributes to the host-interaction and viral-process annotations. The module therefore appears to combine adhesion and receptor biology with nutrient uptake, membrane trafficking, and host-pathogen interface functions. The viral annotations should not be interpreted as evidence that the tissue is infected; they indicate that several module proteins are established host receptors or host factors used in viral entry or processing. The mitochondrial genes ALDH18A1 and MTHFD1L, together with SLC1A5, also suggest metabolic support for membrane-associated cellular activity, but the primary functional conclusion is receptor-rich cell-surface interaction biology rather than a single canonical intracellular pathway.

**Cell composition.** The module is not dominated by plasma or secreted proteins: most members are membrane, organellar, or intracellular proteins. HPA nevertheless identifies secreted behavior for TFRC, FAM3C, and PVR, so extracellular or blood-derived signal cannot be excluded. Several genes have low tissue specificity, and the HPA single-cell evidence spans vascular, epithelial, neuronal, trophoblast, macrophage, monocyte, plasma-cell, and platelet-associated populations. Immune-cell enrichment is present for subsets including monocytes, plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and B-cell populations, while ICAM1 is associated with endothelial and myeloid cells and TFRC with erythroid progenitors and myeloid cells. This raises a meaningful possibility that the kidney–ovary shared signal reflects common vascular, stromal, epithelial, or infiltrating immune-cell states, or blood-related material, rather than a program unique to kidney and ovary. Protein detection is broader in kidney than ovary, although several members are detected in both tissues. HPA therefore supports a mixed-cellular-composition interpretation and argues against treating the module as a uniformly ovary- or kidney-specific program.

STRING reports no high-confidence pairwise interactions among the module genes. The STRING enrichment results are therefore annotation-based and supplementary (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 0.00046–0.0186), not evidence that the module forms a known physical interaction network. Because each hu.MAP complex contains many proteins outside this module, these observations support functional neighborhoods rather than a single compact complex. Overall, the co-abundance edges are not explained by an established STRING interaction network, and only part of the module has complex-level support.

### Novelty

The co-abundance module may represent a new cross-tissue expression pattern, because its complete gene combination is not supported by a corresponding high-confidence STRING network and is only sparsely represented in hu.MAP. However, absence from these resources does not establish biological novelty. The individual functions of the principal genes are well established, and the enrichment themes are recognizable combinations of adhesion, receptor, immune-interface, nutrient-transport, and membrane-trafficking biology. The defensible conclusion is that the cross-tissue co-abundance association is potentially novel, whereas the component biology is not demonstrated to be novel. Independent replication, cell-composition adjustment, and perturbation or interaction experiments would be needed before claiming a new mechanistic module.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney–ovary module reflects a common cell-surface surveillance and trafficking state present in a mixture of vascular, epithelial, and immune-associated cells. In this state, ICAM1 and PVR provide adhesion and immune-interface functions; SLC1A5 supports neutral amino-acid and glutamine transport; TFRC supports receptor-mediated iron uptake; HM13 and VAPB support membrane-protein processing and vesicle or endoplasmic-reticulum organization; and mitochondrial ALDH18A1 and MTHFD1L support the metabolic requirements of the active cells. The repeated host–virus annotations may arise because this cellular state exposes or uses receptors that viruses exploit, not because viral infection is necessarily present. A further possibility is that shared blood or infiltrating myeloid-cell content contributes substantially to the observed co-abundance. This is a hypothesis derived from the integrated evidence, not an established causal mechanism.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular mechanism. Several of its central relationships and complexes are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and its primary functional interpretation is consistent with established RNA processing, translation, nucleolar, and nuclear-transport biology. A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of these established components across kidney and ovary, especially the combination of RNA metabolism, translation initiation, stress protection, mitochondrial organization, and reproductive-cell-associated expression. The fact sheet does not establish whether this exact module has previously been reported, and absence of a complete matching module in STRING or hu.MAP would not prove novelty. The defensible conclusion is therefore that the module is biologically coherent and may represent a context-specific co-abundance configuration, while claims of a new pathway or new physical complex require independent replication and perturbation evidence.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module coordinates RNA handling with protein production and cellular stress adaptation in shared kidney and ovarian cell populations. A plausible sequence is that DDX46 and SF3A1 support spliceosomal RNA processing, NSUN2 and NOL11 support RNA modification or ribosome-related maturation, EIF3C and EIF4G1 couple processed transcripts to translation initiation, QARS1 and YARS1 support translational fidelity, and HSP90AA1 with HSPH1 buffer proteotoxic stress. ATXN2 and ATXN2L may provide an RNA-granule or stress-responsive regulatory connection, while NUP155 and RCC1 may link RNA transport and nuclear organization to cell-cycle or germ-cell states. DCAF13 and AATF could connect nucleolar activity, transcriptional regulation, and reproductive-cell biology. This model is a hypothesis derived from the combined annotations and network structure; the co-abundance data alone do not establish directionality, physical interaction, or causal regulation.

### Novelty

This module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex. Its central relationships and many of its assemblies are already represented by STRING and hu.MAP, and the gene annotations independently support RNA processing. What may be novel is the conserved co-abundance organization of this particular set across kidney and ovary, including the coordinated behavior of spliceosomal, mRNA-export, RNA-editing, translation, and trafficking components. That claim remains provisional: absence of a relationship from STRING or hu.MAP is not evidence of novelty, and the module could reflect shared cell states, ubiquitous RNA metabolism, or technical abundance effects. Demonstrating novelty would require replication in independent kidney and ovary cohorts, control for cell composition, and comparison with tissue coexpression and protein-complex resources.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in kidney and ovary cells, a conserved nuclear RNA-processing state coordinately assembles RNA-binding and spliceosomal factors with RNA-export, exon-junction, RNA-editing, RNA-decay, and translation-coupling components. The ALYREF-RBM15-POLDIP3-RAE1 axis may connect processing to export, while the HNRNPL-HNRNPR-MATR3-DHX9 and SRSF7-TRA2B axes may tune transcript recognition and alternative splicing. ADAR could add RNA-editing-dependent regulation, and PYM1, ribosomal proteins, and IGF2BP3 could connect mature transcripts to surveillance or translation. A secondary possibility is that broadly expressed RNA machinery, together with selected germ-cell or hematopoietic contexts, produces the conserved module without a single physical complex. This is a mechanistic proposal, not established by co-abundance alone.

### Novelty

The exact shared kidney–ovary co-abundance module may be a new systems-level observation, because the supplied evidence defines a tissue-pair-specific co-abundance structure rather than a previously named molecular complex. However, it should not be called a newly discovered molecular pathway. Its principal components, including ER quality-control proteins, SNARE or vesicle proteins, integrins, adhesion receptors, Rab GTPases, and membrane enzymes, have substantial prior annotation, literature, STRING support, and hu.MAP representation. The novelty is therefore most plausibly the conserved co-abundance combination and its cross-tissue reproducibility, not the discovery of previously unknown gene functions or physical interactions. Lack of STRING or hu.MAP coverage alone would not establish novelty, and the existing STRING and hu.MAP evidence argues for a mixture of known biology and potentially novel systems-level coordination.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both kidney and ovary, a shared epithelial or stromal cell program coordinates ER folding and glycosylation with ER-to-Golgi export, endosomal recycling, and delivery of adhesion receptors to the plasma membrane. SEC22B, VAMP7, SEC61A1, CANX, DDOST, MOGS, and EMC1 could support membrane-protein maturation and vesicle flow; RAB8A, RAB10, and RAB21 could route cargo; and ITGA3–ITGB1, ITGAV–ITGB1, ALCAM, CD44, and EGFR could translate extracellular attachment or growth-factor inputs into cell adhesion, migration, and survival responses. MYOF may contribute membrane fusion or repair, while RHOG may couple surface-receptor organization to actin-dependent shape and motility. The neutrophil and other cell-type enrichments raise an alternative hypothesis that part of the signal reflects shared immune or vascular contamination or common cell types. These possibilities require cell-resolved expression, perturbation, and tissue-specific validation; co-abundance alone does not establish physical interaction or causal direction.

### Novelty

The constituent biology is not new: spliceosomal proteins, RNA-binding proteins, nucleolar proteins, and genome-maintenance proteins have established annotations, STRING relationships, and hu.MAP memberships. A defensible conclusion is that the module is biologically well supported but its tissue-pair-specific co-abundance organization remains a candidate finding requiring comparison with published tissue proteomics, transcriptomics, and prior co-expression modules.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in kidney and ovary, coordinated abundance of a broadly expressed nuclear RNA-processing program maintains efficient transcript maturation while integrating spliceosomal activity with nucleolar RNA and ribosome-related functions. The central spliceosomal arm may involve RBM10, RBM22, SNW1, PRPF4, PRP4K, and RBM39, while DNTTIP2, RRP1B, and RSL1D1 may connect this arm to nucleolar RNA handling. LMNB1 and NBN could couple RNA-processing demand to nuclear architecture and genome surveillance. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than evidence of direct physical interaction or causal regulation; it should be tested by perturbation, RNA-splicing measurements, and cell-type-resolved analyses in both tissues.

This is unlikely to be a new molecular pathway or previously unknown protein complex. **Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved kidney-ovary module reflects a shared vascular or sample-fluid compartment in which liver-derived plasma proteins travel as lipoprotein- and microparticle-associated cargo. ### Novelty

The biological machinery itself is not new: SRP-mediated ER targeting, HSPA5-dependent ER chaperoning, PDIA4-mediated folding, and aminoacyl-tRNA charging are established functions. What may be new is the observation that these genes co-vary as a conserved kidney–ovary module in the analyzed data, particularly the connection between the SRP pair, ER quality-control genes, trafficking-related SCFD1, and translation-support genes. The fact sheet does not provide a prior published module with this exact composition, and the targeted web searches did not establish such a report. That supports describing the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern as potentially novel, but not as proven novel. Lack of a hu.MAP complex or absence of some STRING edges is insufficient by itself to claim discovery; replication in independent cohorts, control for cell composition, and comparison with existing tissue co-expression resources would be needed.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module captures a conserved secretory-pathway workload state. Increased production of proteins destined for membranes or the ER may increase demand for SRP-mediated targeting through SRP68 and SRP72, followed by HSPA5-assisted folding and PDIA4-mediated disulfide processing. SCFD1 may couple this biosynthetic load to ER–Golgi vesicle trafficking, while HSPA8 and the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases may reflect upstream cytosolic chaperoning and translation capacity. XRCC6 may be a correlated stress or cellular-state marker rather than a direct component of the secretory mechanism. This hypothesis predicts coordinated changes in ER-targeted translation, unfolded-protein handling, and ER-to-Golgi trafficking, but the co-abundance data alone do not establish directionality or physical interaction.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular complex. Most of its central relationships and several of its subnetworks are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the primary GO and KEGG interpretation is consistent with established spliceosome, CCT, proteasome, ribosome-biogenesis, and RNA-metabolism biology. A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance of these established systems across kidney and ovary, particularly the way spliceosomal, chaperone, ribosomal, and proteasomal components are coordinated in the tissue-pair data. That tissue-pair organization is a hypothesis-generating observation, not proof of a new physical interaction or a new pathway. Absence of a gene from a particular STRING or hu.MAP complex does not establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: a shared cellular state in kidney and ovary increases production and processing of RNA, requiring coordinated spliceosome and ribonucleoprotein assembly, CCT-mediated folding of newly produced proteins, and proteasomal removal of misfolded or damaged proteins. DDX5 and the spliceosomal U-five components could organize or reflect increased pre-mRNA processing, while the CCT subunits could maintain folding capacity and support Cajal-body or telomerase-RNA-related functions. The nucleolar genes could sustain ribosome biogenesis, and the proteasome genes could provide matched protein-quality control. The co-abundance module may therefore represent a conserved biosynthetic and nuclear-RNA-processing state shared by particular kidney and ovarian cell populations. This causal model remains untested by co-abundance data and requires perturbation, localization, and cell-composition-aware validation.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new discovery: the principal genes have established roles in rRNA processing, ribosome assembly, nucleolar regulation, RNA binding, and translation, and many of the central pairwise relationships are already represented in STRING. The exact kidney-ovary conserved co-abundance pattern may still be a new dataset-level observation, because the supplied resources do not show that this precise cross-tissue module has previously been defined. However, absence of the exact module from hu.MAP or absence of some individual edges from STRING cannot prove novelty. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue organization of largely known molecular components, rather than a newly discovered biological pathway or protein complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney-ovary module reflects coordinated nucleolar production and quality control of ribosomal RNA, coupled to translational capacity and cell-state regulation. In this model, DDX10 and DDX21 remodel RNA structures, UTP14A supports U3-snoRNA-associated small-subunit maturation, and NOP2 and PES1 support rRNA modification and large-subunit maturation. MYBBP1A may connect this machinery to nucleolar transcriptional or stress responses, while UCHL5 may influence turnover of associated nuclear proteins. The EPRS1-IARS1 pair could couple ribosome supply to amino-acid charging and translational demand. RFC4 may mark a linked replication or repair state rather than a core ribosome-biogenesis component. This model is consistent with the enrichment and interaction evidence, but direct causality, physical co-complex formation across all members, and the reason for conservation between kidney and ovary require experimental testing.

### Novelty

The biological function is not a new discovery: the MCM complex, CMG-related replication machinery, DNA replication, repair and RRM1-dependent nucleotide production are established biology, and the dense STRING and hu.MAP support confirms that much of the structure is already known. The potentially novel result is the reproducible co-abundance module shared between kidney and ovary, especially the way the MCM core is accompanied by NASP, RRM1 and GLMN. However, the fact sheet does not provide a comparison with previously published tissue co-abundance modules, independent cohorts, perturbation data or replication across datasets. Therefore this should be described as a potentially distinctive tissue-pair co-abundance observation, not as a definitively new molecular complex or newly discovered mechanism. Absence of GLMN or RRM1 from hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because database coverage and complex-detection limits can explain that absence.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney–ovary module reflects coordinated abundance of proliferating progenitor-like cells or cycling tissue compartments. In this model, the MCM core licenses and unwinds replication forks, NASP supports histone delivery and chromatin assembly during genome duplication, and RRM1 supplies deoxyribonucleotides needed for replication and repair. GLMN may mark a vascular or cellular-state component associated with the replication-rich samples rather than being a physical member of the replisome. The module could therefore represent a replication-capacity or replication-stress state shared by selected cell populations in kidney and ovary. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal pathway; testing would require cell-type deconvolution, cell-cycle measurements, independent cohorts and perturbation or functional assays.

The module does not have a globally significant GO or KEGG functional annotation: no term passed global multiple-testing correction. ### Novelty

This may be a novel co-abundance observation, but the evidence is not sufficient to call it a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. The lack of STRING support, lack of globally significant GO or KEGG enrichment, sparse graph structure, and limited hu.MAP overlap argue against an already well-established coherent module. However, absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone cannot prove novelty, because those resources are incomplete and the signal may represent tissue composition, coordinated expression, or a condition-dependent state. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially interesting cross-tissue co-abundance pattern requiring independent replication and experimental validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney-ovary module reflects coordination between nucleolar or ribosome-production activity and antiviral or innate RNA-surveillance state. In this model, POLR1G, UTP4 and NLE1 represent transcriptional and ribosome-biogenesis capacity, while ZC3HAV1 represents an RNA-quality-control and antiviral-response component; MMTAG2 may link these processes or mark a related nuclear cellular state. The hypothesis is based on the gene annotations and the sparse network topology, not on significant enrichment or demonstrated physical interactions. It should be tested by examining whether module abundance tracks ribosome-biogenesis markers, interferon-response markers, cell-type proportions, and direct protein or RNA interactions in independent kidney and ovary datasets.
