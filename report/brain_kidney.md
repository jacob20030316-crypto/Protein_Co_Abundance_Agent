### Function

The module is a mitochondrial energy-metabolism module. The strongest primary evidence is mitochondrial localization and matrix enrichment (GO:CC mitochondrion; q_global = 9.07e-10)(GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 9.9e-09), together with cellular respiration, aerobic respiration, precursor-energy generation, the TCA cycle, carbon metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, and organic-acid metabolism (GO:BP cellular respiration; q_global = 0.000385)(GO:BP tricarboxylic acid cycle; q_global = 0.000623)(KEGG Citrate cycle (TCA cycle) - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000623)(GO:BP energy derivation by oxidation of organic compounds; q_global = 0.00133)(GO:BP aerobic respiration; q_global = 0.00155)(GO:BP generation of precursor metabolites and energy; q_global = 0.00382)(KEGG Carbon metabolism - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00963)(KEGG Oxidative phosphorylation - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0196). A substantial enzymatic component also supports fatty-acid beta-oxidation and lipid oxidation (GO:BP monocarboxylic acid catabolic process; q_global = 0.00444)(GO:BP fatty acid beta-oxidation; q_global = 0.0104)(GO:BP fatty acid oxidation; q_global = 0.016)(GO:BP lipid oxidation; q_global = 0.016)(GO:BP fatty acid catabolic process; q_global = 0.0259). The genes span complementary steps: CS, IDH3A, SDHA, and SUCLG1 support the TCA cycle; DLAT links pyruvate-derived acetyl groups to the cycle; ETFA, ACADS, HADH, CRAT, and DECR1 support fatty-acid and acyl-CoA metabolism; UQCRC1 and UQCRC2 represent complex III; ATP5F1C represents ATP synthase; and NUBPL supports respiratory-complex assembly. The module therefore most plausibly reflects coordinated mitochondrial oxidative metabolism rather than a narrow signaling pathway. Parkinson disease enrichment (KEGG Parkinson disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0396) is pathway-gene overlap and should not be interpreted as evidence that the module specifically causes Parkinson disease.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma or secreted-protein module: the secreted-gene count is zero, and the proteins are detected in both brain and kidney by HPA. It is also not predominantly blood-cell derived: most genes have low immune-cell specificity, with only limited immune-cell enhancement. The composition is instead strongly mitochondrial and metabolically biased. HPA shows a mixture of low tissue specificity and tissue-enhanced genes, with recurring enrichment in tongue, skeletal muscle, liver, kidney, and heart muscle, and recurring cell-type associations with enterocytes, hepatocytes, parietal cells, and other differentiated metabolic or epithelial cells. Thus, the shared brain-kidney signal is more consistent with conserved oxidative-metabolic capacity, or shared metabolically active cell populations, than with blood contamination. HPA detection alone establishes protein presence, not identical cell-type origin or causal activity.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial pathway itself is established and is strongly represented in public interaction and complex resources, so the module should not be described as a newly discovered mitochondrial function. A potentially new aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of this particular gene set across brain and kidney, especially the way fatty-acid oxidation, TCA-cycle, respiratory-chain, ATP-synthesis, and mitochondrial-maintenance genes are coordinated. However, the fact sheet does not compare this exact module with prior co-abundance modules, and absence from hu.MAP or STRING is not evidence of novelty. The defensible conclusion is that the module may be a novel cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built from largely known mitochondrial biology; confirmation would require comparison with published co-abundance networks and independent cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both brain and kidney, variation in mitochondrial oxidative demand coordinately changes fatty-acid entry and oxidation, TCA-cycle flux, electron transfer, proton-gradient utilization, and ATP production. The core formed by CS, DLAT, IDH3A, SDHA, SUCLG1, ATP5F1C, UQCRC1, and UQCRC2 could couple carbon-substrate oxidation to respiratory-chain output, while ACADS, ETFA, HADH, CRAT, and DECR1 could adjust lipid-derived reducing equivalents and acyl-CoA handling. NUBPL, FMC1, PPA2, VDAC2, AKAP1, and AIFM1 could connect assembly, phosphate or metabolite handling, mitochondrial organization, and stress or apoptotic responses. This is a mechanistic model suggested by co-abundance and annotations, not proof of physical co-regulation, flux changes, or a shared disease mechanism.

### Function

The module is most consistent with a broadly expressed membrane–organelle homeostasis program linking endoplasmic-reticulum function, mitochondrial organization, lipid metabolism, redox protection, and membrane trafficking. This interpretation is supported primarily by the gene functions and by the supplementary STRING component terms (6 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 0.00059–0.0361), which point to membrane, endoplasmic-reticulum, organelle-membrane, and palmitoyltransferase associations. ### Novelty

The conserved brain–kidney co-abundance pattern appears potentially novel as a network-level observation, because the module is not represented by a matching hu.MAP complex and only a sparse subset of gene pairs has high-confidence STRING support. However, absence from STRING or hu.MAP does not prove novelty: co-abundance can reflect shared regulation, cell composition, organelle state, or condition-dependent biology rather than stable physical interaction. The underlying biology is not wholly new, because the individual genes and the anchored relationships involving mitochondrial organization, sphingolipid synthesis, Golgi palmitoylation, Rho signaling, and redox control are established. The defensible claim is therefore a potentially new cross-tissue co-regulation pattern assembled from partly known biology, requiring replication and perturbational testing.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated maintenance of membrane lipid composition and organelle integrity across brain and kidney cells. ER lipid synthesis and protein-folding functions could be coupled to Golgi palmitoylation and plasma-membrane trafficking, while mitochondrial cristae organization, mitochondrial fission, heme handling, and antioxidant activity buffer the resulting metabolic and oxidative stress. RHOG–ELMO2 signaling and ALCAM could connect this organelle and membrane state to cell adhesion or motility. A shared cell-state or stress-response program, rather than direct physical assembly of all module proteins, may generate the conserved co-abundance pattern. This hypothesis predicts coordinated changes in sphingolipids, sterols, ER stress, mitochondrial morphology, and oxidative-stress markers when the module is perturbed.

A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of these metabolic and extracellular components across brain and kidney, especially the way a mitochondrial catabolic core is coupled to a circulating and vascular signature. However, novelty cannot be established from this fact sheet alone: absence from STRING or hu.MAP is not proof of a new interaction, and no comparison with previously reported cross-tissue modules, matched cohorts, or independent datasets is provided. The defensible conclusion is that this may be a new systems-level module configuration built from largely established biology, requiring external replication and comparison before being called a new finding.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated metabolic and extracellular homeostasis in cells that share high energetic and detoxification demands, with a mitochondrial core processing branched-chain amino acids, propionyl-derived substrates, aldehydes, lipids, and biogenic amines, while extracellular and circulating proteins report vascular, basement-membrane, complement, coagulation, and vesicle-associated processes. In brain, the metabolic arm could be especially relevant to GABA and catecholamine handling through ABAT, MAOA, and MAOB; in kidney, it could reflect proximal-tubule and related epithelial metabolism. The shared abundance pattern may be strengthened by liver-derived plasma proteins present in both tissue preparations or by common vascular and extracellular compartments. This hypothesis predicts that cell-resolved measurements and removal of blood-derived signal would separate the mitochondrial metabolic submodule from the extracellular circulating submodule, while perturbation of propionyl-CoA or aldehyde metabolism would alter the abundance of the central metabolic genes together.

### Novelty

The biological components are not individually novel: integrin adhesion, ER folding and glycosylation, vesicle traffic, cytoskeletal coupling, and immune or vascular functions are all established. Many of the central relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, especially the ITGB1 integrin complex and the HSPA5–PDIA4–HYOU1 ER chaperone neighborhood. The potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance organization of these processes as one conserved brain–kidney module. That claim is suggestive rather than definitive because the module may reflect shared vascular, epithelial, immune, or blood-derived composition, and because co-abundance does not establish mechanism. Therefore this should be presented as a new systems-level observation or candidate cross-tissue module, not as discovery of a new protein complex or a previously unknown gene function.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in a shared vascular, epithelial, or immune-associated cell population, integrin receptors and CD44 organize adhesion to extracellular matrix and neighboring cells, with FLNA, FLNB, IQGAP1, and TLN1 coupling those receptors to the actin cytoskeleton. In parallel, ER folding, disulfide-bond formation, glycan processing, and membrane-protein insertion by HSPA5, PDIA4, ERO1A, GANAB, MOGS, STT3A, HYOU1, and TXNDC5 maintain the surface receptor and secretory cargo pool. TFRC, SLC1A5, SLC16A3, and mitochondrial or metabolic genes may provide iron, amino-acid, lactate, and biosynthetic support during cell spreading, stress, or repair. Vesicle and membrane-remodeling components such as MYOF, RAB27A, MMP14, and PVR could connect trafficking and extracellular remodeling to adhesion. This model predicts that the module will track cells undergoing matrix interaction, membrane repair, hypoxic or ER stress, and inflammatory remodeling; it does not establish that these processes occur in the same cells without cell-resolved or perturbational validation.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to represent a wholly unknown biological pathway, because its central themes are established and several internal relationships are already supported by STRING and hu.MAP. Its potentially novel contribution is the cross-tissue co-abundance organization that places vesicle and organelle localization, neuronal projection machinery, mitochondrial maintenance, and metabolic components in one conserved brain–kidney module. Absence of some genes from STRING or hu.MAP is not evidence of novelty; it may reflect incomplete interaction coverage or indirect co-regulation. A novelty claim therefore requires comparison with published tissue coexpression modules and independent cohorts, followed by experimental validation of the predicted cross-tissue coordination.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module coordinates polarized-cell logistics in which KIF3A–KIF3B and CLASP2–MARK2 organize microtubule tracks and cargo movement, while EXOC1, TRAPPC11–TRAPPC12, STAM, VPS4A, and related factors direct vesicle delivery, recycling, sorting, and organelle positioning. BECN1 may couple this trafficking system to autophagy and organelle quality control, while mitochondrial factors maintain energy production and redox capacity required for sustained transport. In brain this could support neuronal projection and axonal or dendritic cargo handling; in kidney it could support polarized epithelial trafficking. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal pathway.

### Novelty

The individual respiratory-chain, branched-chain-catabolic, and plasma-protein relationships are not new: they are supported by STRING and by hu.MAP complexes. The potentially novel observation is the conserved co-abundance organization of these established mitochondrial subsystems across brain and kidney, together with a smaller circulating-protein component. Database absence for some module genes does not establish novelty, because missing STRING or hu.MAP links can reflect incomplete coverage, indirect regulation, tissue context, or technical limitations. On the supplied evidence, this should be described as a potentially new tissue-pair co-abundance module or systems-level association, not as a newly discovered protein complex or newly discovered molecular function. Establishing novelty would require comparison with prior brain–kidney co-abundance studies, independent cohorts, and preservation or perturbation tests.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved metabolic-resilience program that coordinates mitochondrial complex-I electron transfer with branched-chain amino-acid, fatty-acid, and organic-acid catabolism in energetically demanding brain and kidney cells. In this model, NDUFV1, NDUFA10, NDUFA9, NDUFS1, NDUFS2, NDUFS5, NDUFV2, and NDUFA13 maintain respiratory electron flow and the proton motive force; DBT, BCKDHA, BCKDHB, IVD, HIBCH, MCCC1, AUH, ACADM, HADHB, and SUCLA2 provide carbon and reducing-equivalent input; NNT may help maintain mitochondrial reducing power; and ATP6V1D with ATP6V1H may connect proton-gradient biology to organelle acidification. APOA1, APOE, and ALB may represent lipid transport or circulating-protein exposure superimposed on the intracellular metabolic signal. This is a mechanistic hypothesis inferred from co-abundance and annotations, not proof of direct regulation or physical coupling between every listed subsystem.

### Novelty

The module-level finding may be novel, particularly because it is a conserved co-abundance pattern across brain and kidney that combines chromatin regulation, transcription, RNA processing, and mitochondrial RNA-related genes. However, the supplied evidence does not establish that the biological program itself is new: several relationships and partial complexes are already known, especially EP300–SIN3A, PIH1D1–RPAP3, ZNF638–PPHLN1, and RPAP3–PIH1D1. Lack of STRING or hu.MAP coverage cannot by itself demonstrate novelty. A defensible conclusion is that the cross-tissue co-abundance organization is a candidate novel systems-level observation, while its mechanistic novelty requires independent replication, comparison with published co-expression modules, and experimental testing.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module represents a shared nuclear gene-expression maintenance program operating in both brain and kidney. A core involving EP300, SIN3A, RNF2, PSIP1, and related chromatin or transcription factors may coordinate chromatin state and transcriptional output, while CPSF4, ZNF638, GRSF1, and mitochondrial RNA-associated components may connect transcription to RNA processing and RNA quality control. The known EP300–SIN3A relationship and the epigenetic and RNA-metabolism signals (GO:BP RNA biosynthetic process; q_global = 0.0166) through (GO:BP RNA metabolic process; q_global = 0.0415) make this model plausible, but co-abundance alone cannot establish direct regulation, physical complex formation, or causal direction.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular complex. Its core biology is already well established by the globally corrected GO terms, the STRING preribosome network, and several hu.MAP complexes. However, the conserved co-abundance module itself may still be a useful systems-level finding because it links known nucleolar machinery with RNA processing, translation, metabolism, nuclear transport, stress handling, and cell-cycle components across brain and kidney. The absence of hu.MAP membership for many central nucleolar genes does not prove novelty, and the absence of a matching STRING edge does not prove that a relationship is biologically new. A defensible novelty claim is therefore that this particular cross-tissue co-abundance organization and its preservation between brain and kidney may be novel or under-described, whereas the individual functions and many molecular interactions are established. Independent replication, comparison with published tissue co-expression modules, and perturbation or temporal data would be needed to establish genuine novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this module is a conserved cellular-capacity program that coordinates nucleolar ribosome production with the RNA-processing and translational systems required to sustain protein synthesis. In this model, DDX21, PDCD11, WDR3, EMG1, NOP2, and their connected preribosome factors organize rRNA processing and ribosomal-subunit maturation; RNA-binding and splicing factors help route mature transcripts; aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and ribosomal proteins support translation; and metabolic, redox, chromatin, and nuclear-pore components adjust RNA production and protein synthesis to cellular growth or stress. The shared brain-kidney signal may therefore reflect common demands for high-quality RNA surveillance and protein homeostasis rather than a tissue-specific pathway. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of direct physical cooperation among every module member.
