### Function

The strongest interpretation is a conserved mitochondrial metabolic and proteostasis module, with an additional endoplasmic-reticulum chaperone and amino-acid/iron-uptake component. Primary evidence comes from the globally corrected mitochondrial matrix and mitochondrial envelope terms (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.0294) and (GO:CC mitochondrial envelope; q_global = 0.038), together with protein-folding chaperone binding (GO:MF protein-folding chaperone binding; q_global = 0.0442). The module contains mitochondrial translation and import machinery, tricarboxylic-acid-cycle and respiratory components, amino-acid metabolism and transport proteins, heme biosynthesis, and ER protein-folding factors. The melanosome and pigment-granule terms (GO:CC melanosome; q_global = 0.00925) and (GO:CC pigment granule; q_global = 0.00925) are statistically robust but should be interpreted cautiously: their overlapping genes are largely HSPA5, HSPA8, PDIA4, SLC1A5, SLC3A2 and TFRC, which can represent shared trafficking, chaperone, membrane and metabolic biology rather than a dedicated pigment program. The mitochondrial interpretation is independently supported by the STRING mitochondrial annotations (STRING Keyword Transit peptide; within-query FDR = 0.0019), (STRING Component Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.0044), (STRING Keyword Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.0055), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.0099), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Mitochondrial envelope; within-query FDR = 0.034), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Mitochondrial matrix; within-query FDR = 0.034) and (STRING Component Mitochondrial inner membrane; within-query FDR = 0.0434). No KEGG term was provided among the globally corrected primary terms.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma or secreted-protein module and is not strongly blood-cell enriched. The HPA summary indicates generally low tissue specificity, generally low immune-cell specificity, and only a small secreted component. Protein detection is common in both ovary and prostate, supporting a shared cellular program rather than a signal restricted to one tissue. Several genes show cell-type enhancement in reproductive or epithelial populations, including epididymal principal cells, prostatic basal or hillock cells, oocytes, trophoblast-related populations and retinal pigment epithelial cells. The HPA pattern also includes muscle-associated and other tissue-enhanced genes, so the module may partly reflect broadly conserved energetic or biosynthetic demands and shared cell composition. The blood-cell observations for TFRC, SLC1A5 and other isolated genes do not support interpreting the entire module as a blood-contamination signature.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to be wholly novel because many of its relationships and functional themes are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, especially mitochondrial respiration, mitochondrial translation, chaperoning, amino-acid transport and intracellular trafficking. Its potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance of these components across ovary and prostate, particularly the coupling of mitochondrial metabolic capacity with ER proteostasis, amino-acid uptake and iron handling. That cross-tissue configuration should be considered a new systems-level observation rather than a newly discovered molecular complex. Absence of some genes from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because those resources emphasize experimentally supported physical or functional relationships and may not capture tissue-specific co-abundance.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in a shared ovary-prostate cell population, nutrient availability and biosynthetic demand may coordinate amino-acid uptake through SLC1A5-SLC3A2 and iron uptake through TFRC with mitochondrial substrate metabolism, respiratory-chain activity, mitochondrial protein import and translation. Mitochondrial and ER chaperone systems, represented by TRAP1, HSPA5, HSPA8, PDIA4 and DNAJC10, may buffer the resulting proteotoxic and metabolic stress. The melanosome or pigment-granule signal may reflect an organelle-trafficking or secretory-cell state that shares chaperone and transporter machinery, rather than literal pigment production. This mechanism is a testable hypothesis from co-abundance and annotation; the data do not establish directionality, causality, cell-type origin or direct physical assembly.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a conserved proliferative, genome-maintenance and gene-expression module. The strongest primary evidence is for nucleic acid binding (GO:MF nucleic acid binding; q_global = 7.14e-05), DNA replication (KEGG DNA replication - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000356) (GO:BP DNA-templated DNA replication; q_global = 0.00626) (GO:BP DNA replication; q_global = 0.0275), replication initiation (GO:BP regulation of DNA-templated DNA replication initiation; q_global = 0.00318) (GO:BP regulation of DNA-templated DNA replication; q_global = 0.00318) (GO:BP DNA replication initiation; q_global = 0.00758), the DNA replication preinitiation, MCM and CMG complexes (GO:CC DNA replication preinitiation complex; q_global = 0.0009) (GO:CC MCM complex; q_global = 0.0009) (GO:CC CMG complex; q_global = 0.0009), helicase activity (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.0106–0.0438; see the table), and break-induced replication repair (GO:BP double-strand break repair via break-induced replication; q_global = 0.0009). FEN1 and RFC4 connect replication with lagging-strand processing and repair, while the translation-initiation and ribonucleoprotein terms (GO:BP translational initiation; q_global = 0.0196) (GO:CC ribonucleoprotein complex; q_global = 0.0209) (GO:MF ribosome binding; q_global = 0.0286) indicate coordinated RNA handling and protein synthesis. Thus, the module is not a single narrow pathway; it is centered on DNA licensing and fork activity, with associated RNA processing, translation, ribosome interaction, purine or lipid biosynthetic, and nuclear organization signals. The coherent MCM core and its network connectivity make a cell-cycle or replication-state interpretation stronger than an interpretation based only on generic nucleic-acid binding.

**Cell composition.** The module is overwhelmingly intracellular rather than plasma-derived or secreted: the HPA summary identifies only a single secreted gene, and the proteins are mainly cytosolic, nucleoplasmic, nucleolar, nuclear-speckle, or nuclear-membrane proteins. Most genes have low tissue specificity, consistent with broadly expressed housekeeping or proliferation-associated biology. HPA does not support a predominantly immune-cell or blood-cell module, since most genes have low immune-cell specificity. However, many genes are cell-type enhanced in erythrocyte progenitors, megakaryocyte progenitors, trophoblast populations, and germ-cell populations, and several show enhancement in lymphoid tissue, bone marrow, or testis. Therefore, the shared ovary–prostate signal may reflect conserved proliferating or differentiating cell populations, including germ-cell or progenitor-like components, rather than a tissue-specific secreted program. Protein detection is generally compatible with the module in both tissues, although individual genes such as MCM6, MCM5, NSUN2, YTHDF2, EIF3L, FASN, PSAT1, HSPH1, NUP155, EMG1, and PPM1G show tissue-specific detection differences.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered DNA-replication, MCM, spliceosome, translation, or SRP mechanism, because these constituent relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP and are supported by established annotations. A potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance of these connected programs as one conserved module across ovary and prostate. That tissue-pair-level organization could reflect a shared proliferative, germ-cell, progenitor, or differentiation state, but the fact sheet does not establish whether this cross-tissue module has been reported previously. Absence of a particular edge or complex from STRING or hu.MAP is not evidence of novelty. The appropriate conclusion is that the module-level tissue-pair observation may be novel, whereas its principal molecular components and many of its relationships are established.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared population of cycling or differentiating cells in ovary and prostate co-regulates replication licensing, replication-fork progression, DNA repair, RNA maturation, translation, and biosynthetic capacity. The MCM complex may provide the central replication-state signal; FEN1 and RFC4 may couple fork progression to Okazaki-fragment processing and repair; RNA-processing and translation factors may support the increased production and maturation of transcripts and proteins required by those cells. The enrichment of progenitor and germ-cell-associated HPA profiles is compatible with this model, but it does not prove cellular composition or causality. An alternative explanation is that the module reflects a general abundance or cell-cycle program shared by samples rather than direct regulation among all module genes.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a newly discovered mitochondrial complex or pathway. Its central relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and its functional interpretation is strongly concordant with established mitochondrial biology. A potentially novel aspect is the observation that these respiratory-chain, ATP-synthase, membrane-organization, and fatty-acid-oxidation genes form a conserved co-abundance module across ovary and prostate in this dataset. However, absence of some genes or relationships from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty, and the fact sheet does not provide an external publication comparison. The defensible conclusion is therefore that this is a biologically coherent tissue-pair co-abundance finding built largely from known mitochondrial machinery, with novelty limited to its dataset-specific organization and conservation across the two tissues.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared population of metabolically active cells in ovary and prostate coordinately increases mitochondrial inner-membrane respiratory capacity, coupling complex-I electron transfer through complex III to ATP synthase activity, while using long-chain fatty-acid oxidation to supply reducing equivalents. IMMT may help preserve inner-membrane and cristae architecture, and AIFM1 may link respiratory state to mitochondrial stress and apoptotic competence. The observed module could therefore represent a coordinated mitochondrial reserve or energy-demand program rather than a single physical complex. This hypothesis can be tested by examining whether module abundance tracks mitochondrial mass, respiratory flux, fatty-acid utilization, cristae organization, and cell-type composition independently in ovary and prostate.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular complex. Much of its internal structure is already known: the proteasome block, aminoacyl-tRNA-synthetase block, RNA-processing relationships, and ACTL6A-RUVBL2-associated complexes are represented in STRING or hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of these established machinery components across ovary and prostate, including their joint coupling to RNA surveillance, translation, chromatin or telomere-related factors, and cell-junction-associated genes. That cross-tissue co-regulation may be a new observation, but it requires replication in independent datasets and control for cell composition, proliferation, disease state, and technical effects. Absence of a particular edge from STRING or hu.MAP would not establish novelty, especially because those resources emphasize curated or experimentally supported relationships and do not represent every co-abundance association.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: in shared ovary and prostate cell states, increased demand for protein synthesis, RNA maturation, nuclear gene regulation, and proteome quality control produces coordinated abundance of the proteasome, RNA-processing, translation, chaperone, and genome-maintenance machinery. The proteasome could remove damaged or short-lived regulatory proteins, while DDX5, DDX21, HNRNPH1, SF3B3, U2SURP, and UPF1 could coordinate RNA processing and surveillance. ACTL6A and RUVBL2 could connect this state to chromatin remodeling, DNA repair, replication, or telomere regulation. The reproductive-cell HPA pattern raises the possibility that this is especially prominent in germ-cell-like or highly proliferative states, but that interpretation is a hypothesis and cannot be established from co-abundance alone.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular complex. Many of its internal relationships and assemblies are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the core functions are well established by the globally corrected GO and KEGG results. The potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance of this combination as a conserved module shared by ovary and prostate, particularly the coupling of spliceosome and RNA-binding proteins with CCT chaperonin components, nuclear export factors, and tissue-relevant cell-type signals. However, the fact sheet does not establish whether this exact tissue-pair module has been reported previously, nor does absence from a database prove novelty. The appropriate conclusion is that this is a biologically credible and potentially informative tissue-shared co-abundance pattern, but a new biological mechanism or new protein complex would require comparison with published tissue co-expression modules, independent cohorts, perturbation data, and direct interaction experiments.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both ovary and prostate, a shared demand for regulated RNA maturation creates coordinated abundance of spliceosomal, RNA-binding, RNA-export, and RNA-surveillance proteins. DHX9 and the hnRNP proteins could organize or remodel RNA–protein assemblies; SRSF7 and TRA2B could help specify splice-site selection; EFTUD2, SNRNP200, and PPIL1 could execute spliceosomal remodeling; ALYREF, NUP214, and RAE1 could couple mRNA maturation to nuclear export; and CCT3, CCT5, and CCT8 could maintain folding and assembly of selected RNA-processing proteins. BCLAF1, LARP7, and associated transcriptional regulators could link this post-transcriptional program to transcriptional output. The tissue-shared signal may therefore reflect a conserved RNA-quality-control and gene-expression state, with modulation by reproductive or cell-type composition rather than a tissue-exclusive pathway. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal relationship.
