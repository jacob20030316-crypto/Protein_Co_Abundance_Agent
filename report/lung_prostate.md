# Conserved co-abundance modules: lung and prostate

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: lung and prostate  
**Genes (10)**: HIBADH, ARMC10, ECI2, FMC1, AK4, L2HGDH, OPA1, PON2, RDH13, DNAJC19

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a mitochondrial co-abundance module involved in mitochondrial organization, energy production, nucleotide balance, substrate metabolism, and protection from metabolic or oxidative stress. The primary evidence is (GO:CC mitochondrion; q_global = 0.031), in which nearly the entire module maps to the mitochondrion. The constituent annotations support complementary mitochondrial activities: OPA1 is involved in inner-membrane organization and mitochondrial fusion; AK4 supports nucleotide interconversion and oxidative-phosphorylation regulation; FMC1 supports ATP synthase assembly; DNAJC19 supports mitochondrial protein import and cardiolipin regulation; ECI2 supports fatty-acid beta-oxidation; HIBADH supports valine catabolism; and L2HGDH supports conversion of L-2-hydroxyglutarate to a tricarboxylic-acid-cycle intermediate. RDH13 and PON2 provide additional links to retinoid metabolism and cellular antioxidant defense. STRING independently gives supplementary mitochondrial support through (STRING Component Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.00016), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.00083), and (STRING Keyword Mitochondrion; within-query FDR = 0.0047). The sparse co-abundance graph is therefore consistent with a coordinated mitochondrial state rather than a single narrowly defined biochemical pathway.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma-protein or blood-cell module. Only PON2 is reported as secreted to blood, while RDH13 is intracellular and membrane-associated; the other genes are not secreted according to the HPA summary. HPA indicates low immune-cell specificity for most genes, with selected enrichment in myeloid dendritic cells, classical monocytes, or basophils, but no dominant blood-cell signature. The tissue pattern is mixed rather than lung- or prostate-specific, including enhancement in kidney, liver, and brain and cell-type associations with proximal tubule cells, hepatocytes, and several specialized epithelial or neural cell types. This breadth argues against interpreting the module as a prostate-specific or lung-specific lineage program. It may instead reflect a shared mitochondrial requirement across resident epithelial and other metabolically active cells. Protein detection is broad in both focal tissues, although AK4 and RDH13 show tissue-specific detection differences that should be considered when interpreting abundance.

### Novelty

The module may represent a useful tissue-conserved co-abundance finding, but the fact sheet does not establish it as a completely new biological mechanism. Its mitochondrial interpretation is strongly supported by (GO:CC mitochondrion; q_global = 0.031) and by the supplementary STRING results, while the individual mitochondrial functions are already established in the literature. What may be novel is the coordinated abundance pattern shared between lung and prostate, especially the specific combination of mitochondrial dynamics, protein import, ATP synthase assembly, redox protection, and intermediary metabolism. However, the lack of STRING interactions and hu.MAP complexes alone does not prove novelty. Demonstrating novelty would require comparison with prior tissue co-abundance modules, replication in independent cohorts, and testing whether the pattern persists after controlling for cell composition, mitochondrial content, and technical effects.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared mitochondrial maintenance and metabolic adaptation program in lung and prostate cells. Coordinated regulation of mitochondrial dynamics through OPA1, import and membrane-related processes through DNAJC19, ATP synthase assembly through FMC1, and nucleotide handling through AK4 could help sustain energy output. In parallel, ECI2, HIBADH, and L2HGDH could coordinate fatty-acid, branched-chain amino-acid, and organic-acid metabolism, while RDH13 and PON2 could limit retinoid- or oxidative-stress-associated damage. This model predicts that module abundance will track mitochondrial functional state rather than a direct protein complex, and that apparent tissue conservation will depend partly on shared metabolic cell types.

The strongest interpretation is a conserved intracellular proteostasis and nucleocytoplasmic-transport module. The primary evidence is global enrichment for nucleocytoplasmic carrier activity (GO:MF nucleocytoplasmic carrier activity; q_global = 0.00141), molecular carrier activity (GO:MF molecular carrier activity; q_global = 0.00417), protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum (KEGG Protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0288), and ATP-dependent protein-folding chaperone activity (GO:MF ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone; q_global = 0.0416). The remaining genes suggest connected support for nucleotide synthesis, translation, DNA replication or repair, protein maturation, and intracellular trafficking, but these functions are secondary to the carrier and chaperone signals. The module therefore most plausibly reflects coordinated handling, folding, quality control, and compartmental transport of cellular proteins and nucleic-acid-associated cargo.

**Cell composition.** This does not look like a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module. The HPA summary identifies only a small secreted subset, and the annotated secretome examples are described as intracellular or membrane-associated rather than as a dominant circulating component. Protein detection is generally concordant in lung and prostate, although individual genes such as CSE1L, NAA15, MCM2, and HSP90AA1 show tissue-specific detection differences. ### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular pathway. Its principal components and many of its pairwise relationships are already established by STRING, hu.MAP, gene annotations, and the primary enrichment results. What may be novel is the conserved co-abundance configuration shared by lung and prostate, particularly the coupling of karyopherin transport with endoplasmic-reticulum quality control and nucleotide or genome-maintenance processes in these tissues. The supplied evidence does not establish whether this exact cross-tissue module has been reported previously, and absence from STRING or hu.MAP would not demonstrate novelty. A defensible conclusion is that the biology is largely known, whereas the tissue-pair-specific co-abundance organization is a candidate new observation requiring replication in independent cohorts and comparison with published co-expression modules.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module represents a cellular adaptation program in which increased demand for nuclear import and export is coupled to chaperone-mediated folding and endoplasmic-reticulum quality control. In this model, CSE1L, IPO4, TNPO1, XPO1, and XPO5 coordinate cargo movement through the nuclear pore; HSPA8, HSPH1, and HSP90AA1 stabilize or remodel transported proteins; and HSPA5, PDIA4, and GANAB process proteins entering or residing in the endoplasmic reticulum. MTHFD1, GMPS, and related metabolic genes may provide nucleotide and biosynthetic support for protein synthesis, replication, and repair. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway. It predicts that the module should track cellular stress, high protein-synthesis or trafficking demand, and changes in nuclear-cargo localization in both tissues.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma-protein, secreted-protein, or blood-cell module. HPA indicates broadly low tissue specificity for most genes, low immune-cell specificity for nearly the entire module, and only a small secreted component. Most proteins are intracellular and nuclear or nucleoplasmic, with HPA protein detection in both lung and prostate for nearly all assessed genes. The module is therefore unlikely to be explained mainly by plasma carryover or a shared blood-cell infiltrate. Some cell-type-enhanced signals involve spermatogenic, trophoblast, progenitor, and selected epithelial or specialized cell populations. These signals may reflect proliferative or differentiation states shared by subsets of cells, but they do not overturn the broad housekeeping-like nuclear interpretation. C1QBP and THEM6 warrant attention as exceptions because HPA reports blood secretion or an extracellular prediction, respectively; they are peripheral to the dominant nuclear network.

### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a newly discovered molecular complex. Many of its core relationships are already represented by high-confidence STRING links and hu.MAP complexes, especially the nuclear pore, spliceosome, MCM replication, DNA-repair, and cleavage-polyadenylation components. Its potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance organization shared by lung and prostate, including coordination between these established nuclear subsystems. That tissue-pair-level organization is not demonstrated to be novel by the supplied evidence alone, and absence of a complete module match in STRING or hu.MAP would not establish novelty. A defensible conclusion is that this is a biologically credible conserved regulatory state with possible systems-level novelty, rather than a proven new pathway or complex. Independent replication in additional cohorts, assessment of cell composition and cell cycle, and comparison with published lung-prostate co-expression modules would be required to support a stronger novelty claim.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared lung-prostate module marks a coordinated nuclear state in which active or changing cell populations increase demand for RNA maturation, nuclear transport, genome replication, and repair. In this model, the NUP subnetwork organizes transport and nuclear-envelope exchange; the spliceosomal, helicase, and cleavage-polyadenylation components process transcripts; the MCM, FEN1, and MRE11 components protect replication and repair DNA; and transcriptional or chromatin regulators couple these processes to cell-state transitions. The cross-subnetwork co-abundance may therefore arise from proliferative, differentiating, or stress-responsive cells rather than from direct physical binding between every module member. This is a mechanistic proposal, not an established causal relationship from the co-abundance data.
