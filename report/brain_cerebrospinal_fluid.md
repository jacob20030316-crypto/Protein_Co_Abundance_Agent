### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating extracellular and plasma-protein co-abundance program rather than as a securely established brain-specific biochemical pathway. Its strongest biological coherence is the combination of lipoprotein-associated proteins, extracellular matrix or adhesion proteins, heme and vitamin transport proteins, complement-related proteins, coagulation-related proteins, and protease-regulatory proteins. This interpretation is supported by the dense STRING relationships among APOA2, APOB, APOC1, APOC3, FGA, GC, HPX, and HRG, and by the repeated hu.MAP overlap in complexes C1 through C12. The module also contains a smaller brain-associated structural or signaling component represented by SPOCK3, LAMA4, DPP10, ADGRB2, LRP1B, and SPARC. No GO or KEGG term passed global correction, so no pathway enrichment is claimed from GO or KEGG. The lack of a globally significant term does not invalidate the module, but it means that the functional interpretation rests mainly on gene-level annotations, network structure, HPA composition, STRING, and hu.MAP evidence.

**Cell composition.** The module is dominated by secreted proteins and by genes enriched in liver and hepatocytes, especially APOA2, APOB, APOC1, APOC3, FGA, GC, HPX, HRG, ITIH1, and CFH. Many are annotated as secreted to blood or as plasma proteins. This makes blood-derived protein carryover, vascular exposure, or shared extracellular material a major alternative explanation for the brain and cerebrospinal-fluid concordance. The module is not primarily a blood-cell module: most genes are not detected in immune cells or have low immune-cell specificity, although a smaller subset has myeloid, monocyte, neutrophil, or macrophage associations. HPA also identifies a brain-associated subset, including SPOCK3, DPP10, ADGRB2, LRP1B, LAMA4, SPARC, and several genes with ependymal, neuronal, glial, or choroid-plexus annotations. Brain protein detection is mixed, while cerebrospinal-fluid-specific HPA protein evidence is unavailable. Overall, the composition favors a circulating liver-derived extracellular signature with a possible contribution from brain barriers, glia, choroid plexus, or other shared extracellular compartments, rather than a purely neuronal module.

### Novelty

The module as a whole should not be presented as a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. Its central liver-derived plasma component is consistent with well-established lipoprotein, coagulation, transport, complement, and extracellular-protein biology, and much of that component is represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance of this circulating signature with a smaller brain-associated extracellular or membrane-signaling subset in the brain and cerebrospinal-fluid tissue pair. However, absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone does not establish novelty, and the strong secreted-protein composition means that shared blood exposure, barrier-associated material, or sample composition could generate the pattern. Novelty therefore remains a hypothesis requiring replication in independent cohorts, adjustment for blood contamination and cell composition, and testing of protein or transcript localization.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a liver-derived circulating extracellular-protein program that is sampled in both brain-related material and cerebrospinal fluid, with a possible interface component involving brain barriers, choroid plexus, glia, or extracellular matrix. Lipoprotein-associated proteins may form the central abundance scaffold, while GC, HPX, HRG, CFH, FGA, and ITIH1 contribute transport, heme handling, complement regulation, coagulation, and extracellular-matrix organization. A brain-associated branch involving SPOCK3, LAMA4, DPP10, ADGRB2, LRP1B, and SPARC may represent local extracellular or membrane-associated biology that covaries with the circulating signal. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal pathway; it should be tested by measuring matched blood, brain, and cerebrospinal-fluid samples and by examining whether the brain-associated genes remain correlated after controlling for plasma protein contamination and cellular composition.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular pathway. Several of its central relationships are already represented in STRING, including the BCAN–VCAN–PTPRZ1 group, NCAM1–contactin relationships, CNTN1–NFASC, and the collagen pair, while hu.MAP contains recurrent complexes involving the same genes. The likely novel aspect is the coordinated co-abundance of these neural adhesion, extracellular-matrix, vascular, and secreted components across brain and cerebrospinal fluid in this particular dataset and tissue pairing. That context-specific organization may be biologically informative, but it requires replication, control for blood and vascular contribution, and comparison with previously reported brain or cerebrospinal-fluid modules. Lack of a STRING or hu.MAP entry for individual genes is not evidence of novelty by itself.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a soluble and membrane-associated neural-interface program in which neuronal and glial adhesion molecules organize extracellular-matrix contacts around axons, synapses, and myelination-associated surfaces, while proteoglycans and collagens stabilize or remodel the surrounding matrix. Shedding, secretion, vesicular release, or leakage from these interfaces could make the program visible in cerebrospinal fluid. PTPRZ1, BCAN, and VCAN may provide a proteoglycan-rich glial matrix scaffold; NCAM1, NTM, CNTN1, CNTN2, and NFASC may connect that scaffold to neuronal and axonal surfaces; and CACNA2D1 may link the adhesion environment to neuronal excitability and calcium-channel organization. The vascular, collagen, ITIH4, and immunoglobulin components could represent a parallel vascular or blood-derived contribution rather than a core neural mechanism. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal pathway.
