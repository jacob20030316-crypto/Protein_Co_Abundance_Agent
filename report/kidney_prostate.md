This is a mitochondrial energy-metabolism and oxidative-phosphorylation module. The strongest primary evidence is the coordinated enrichment of mitochondrion and mitochondrial membrane components (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.67e-06–3.63e-05; see the table), oxidative phosphorylation and cellular respiration (12 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 6.52e-05–0.000602; see the table), respiratory-chain complexes and electron transport (GO:CC respiratory chain complex; q_global = 0.000464), (GO:BP respiratory electron transport chain; q_global = 0.0062), (GO:BP electron transport chain; q_global = 0.00918), (GO:BP aerobic electron transport chain; q_global = 0.0171), and ATP synthesis coupled to the proton motive force (GO:BP proton motive force-driven ATP synthesis; q_global = 0.00109), (GO:BP proton motive force-driven mitochondrial ATP synthesis; q_global = 0.0103), (GO:BP ATP synthesis coupled electron transport; q_global = 0.0216), (GO:BP mitochondrial ATP synthesis coupled electron transport; q_global = 0.0216). The disease-pathway terms involving reactive oxygen species and several neurodegenerative disorders (KEGG Chemical carcinogenesis - reactive oxygen species - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000744), (KEGG Prion disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00574), (KEGG Parkinson disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00645), (KEGG Huntington disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0121), (KEGG Alzheimer disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0178), and (KEGG Pathways of neurodegeneration - multiple diseases - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0444) should be interpreted as pathway overlap rather than evidence that the module specifically causes those diseases. Overall, the module most plausibly represents coordinated mitochondrial respiratory-chain activity, substrate oxidation, proton-gradient utilization, and ATP supply.

**Cell composition.** The module is not composed of plasma or secreted proteins: HPA reports no secreted genes, and the proteins are predominantly intracellular, especially mitochondrial. Blood contamination or a dominant immune-cell signal is unlikely to be the main explanation because most genes have low immune-cell specificity, although a small subset shows immune-cell enhancement or group enrichment. The HPA profile is broadly compatible with metabolically active epithelial and parenchymal cell types, including hepatocytes, gastrointestinal and esophageal epithelial cells, muscle-related cells, renal cells, and some reproductive-cell populations. Many genes have tissue enhancement rather than uniform housekeeping behavior, but the core respiratory-chain genes are broadly relevant to oxidative metabolism. Protein detection is widespread in kidney and also substantial in prostate, supporting biological presence in both tissues. The shared module therefore most likely reflects conserved mitochondrial metabolic state or shared metabolically active cell populations, rather than circulating protein carryover.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial oxidative-phosphorylation function itself is not a new biological discovery: it is supported by established annotations, STRING relationships, and hu.MAP complexes. The cross-tissue co-abundance module shared by kidney and prostate could still be a novel observation about coordinated abundance in these tissues, but the fact sheet does not provide an external reference network, replication cohort, preservation statistic, or literature comparison sufficient to establish novelty. The presence of many known complex members argues that the module captures a real conserved biological program, while the peripheral genes and tissue-sharing pattern may provide the more potentially original aspect. Absence of a particular gene pair from STRING or hu.MAP would not by itself demonstrate novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both kidney and prostate, variation in the abundance of metabolically active cells or their mitochondrial state coordinately regulates substrate oxidation, electron transport, proton-gradient formation, ATP synthase activity, metabolite transport, and protection from mitochondrial oxidative stress. The central respiratory machinery may be coupled to substrate entry and redox buffering through ETFA, IDH3A, SUCLG1, SFXN1, SLC25A1, and PRDX3, while VDAC2, PHB2, and AIFM1 may connect mitochondrial energetics with membrane organization and stress or apoptotic responses. This is a mechanistic model derived from the combined annotations and network structure; the co-abundance data alone do not prove physical interaction, causal regulation, or disease involvement.

### Novelty

The module should not be called wholly novel. Much of its biology is already established: the branched-chain catabolic relationships, complex-I relationships, and several metabolic enzyme associations are represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the globally corrected enrichment recapitulates known mitochondrial metabolism. The potentially new aspect is the tissue-pair co-abundance organization: these established metabolic genes appear as one conserved kidney-prostate module, and the specific cross-pathway co-abundance architecture may not have been previously described in this analysis. However, absence of a particular edge from STRING or hu.MAP is not evidence that the edge is novel, because those resources emphasize different types of molecular evidence and have incomplete coverage. A defensible conclusion is that this is a biologically credible, partly rediscovered mitochondrial metabolic module whose tissue-shared co-abundance configuration requires validation before being claimed as a new mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in kidney and prostate, a shared metabolically active epithelial or parenchymal cell state coordinates branched-chain amino-acid breakdown, fatty-acid oxidation, organic-acid catabolism, and respiratory-chain electron transfer. The BCKD-related genes DBT and BCKDHA, the branched-chain and acyl-CoA enzymes ACADSB, IVD, ACAT1, and ECHS1, and the electron-transfer machinery represented by ETFDH and the complex-I subunits may therefore rise and fall together as a mitochondrial substrate-oxidation program. ALDH6A1, MPST, and TST could connect carbon metabolism with aldehyde or sulfur-compound handling, while CAT and NAPRT may help buffer oxidative and redox demands. This is a mechanistic model inferred from co-abundance, annotations, and known interactions; the fact sheet does not demonstrate metabolic flux, direct regulation, or causality. Testing would require cell-type-resolved expression, protein measurements, respiratory phenotyping, and substrate or metabolite measurements in kidney and prostate.

## Module 6

**Tissue pair**: kidney and prostate  
**Genes (6)**: IBA57, MPI, DLAT, PDHB, TFAM, TXNRD2

### Function

The module is most consistently interpreted as a mitochondrial energy-metabolism module, with a particularly strong connection to pyruvate oxidation. The primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (GO:CC pyruvate dehydrogenase complex; q_global = 0.0229) and the mitochondrial matrix (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.0383). DLAT and PDHB are the genes supporting (GO:CC pyruvate dehydrogenase complex; q_global = 0.0229), while DLAT, IBA57, PDHB, TFAM, and TXNRD2 support (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.0383). The annotations indicate complementary biology: DLAT and PDHB are components of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, TFAM supports mitochondrial transcription and mitochondrial DNA maintenance, IBA57 supports mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster assembly, and TXNRD2 supports mitochondrial redox homeostasis. MPI contributes a carbohydrate-metabolism and glycosylation-related function but is not included in either primary enriched term. Thus, the most defensible function is coordinated mitochondrial metabolic maintenance centered on pyruvate dehydrogenase activity, with associated mitochondrial genome, iron-sulfur, and redox functions. The STRING transit-peptide and mitochondrial-matrix findings (STRING Keyword Transit peptide; within-query FDR = 0.0129) and (STRING Component Mitochondrial matrix; within-query FDR = 0.0177) are concordant supplementary evidence, and the STRING pyruvate-dehydrogenase-complex finding (STRING Component Mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase complex; within-query FDR = 0.0299) reinforces the pathway interpretation without replacing the globally corrected evidence.

**Cell composition.** This does not resemble a plasma-protein, secreted-protein, or blood-cell module. The HPA summary reports no secreted genes, low immune-cell specificity for every gene, and mitochondrial or cytosolic intracellular locations. All module proteins were detected in both kidney and prostate, supporting biological presence in both tissues rather than an interpretation based on missing protein measurements. Most genes have low tissue specificity, although DLAT and PDHB are tissue-enhanced in tongue. The single-cell annotations are heterogeneous: enrichment appears in late spermatids, cardiomyocytes, prostatic glandular cells, adipocytes, trophoblasts, enterocytes, esophageal apical cells, and late primary spermatocytes. This pattern is compatible with broadly reused cellular energy biology, while the reproductive-cell and prostatic-glandular annotations may be relevant to the kidney–prostate sharing. The evidence does not support blood contamination as the principal explanation, but co-abundance could still reflect shared metabolically active cell states or common mitochondrial content across samples.

### Novelty

The module is not a wholly new molecular-complex discovery because the DLAT–PDHB relationship is already strongly represented in STRING and repeatedly represented in hu.MAP (hu.MAP huMAP3_04919.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_08216.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_07514.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09761.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10899.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10412.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14339.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11877.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14864.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_12248.1). The pyruvate-dehydrogenase interpretation is also established by the gene annotations and by the globally corrected enrichment (GO:CC pyruvate dehydrogenase complex; q_global = 0.0229). However, the kidney–prostate conserved co-abundance configuration may still be a novel systems-level observation. In particular, the specific association of the known pyruvate-dehydrogenase core with TFAM, IBA57, TXNRD2, and MPI in this cross-tissue context is not established by the reported STRING or hu.MAP evidence. The novelty claim should remain qualified: absence from the listed interaction and complex resources does not prove that the relationships are novel, and the module could reflect shared mitochondrial abundance, common cell states, or coordinated regulation rather than direct molecular interaction. The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore partial novelty at the tissue-pair co-abundance level, not discovery of a new protein complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved kidney–prostate module reflects a shared mitochondrial metabolic state in which pyruvate dehydrogenase activity supplies acetyl-CoA and respiratory metabolism, while TFAM maintains the mitochondrial genetic program, IBA57 supports maturation of mitochondrial iron-sulfur proteins, and TXNRD2 limits oxidative stress. MPI may connect this state to carbohydrate flux and glycosylation precursor supply. Under this model, coordinated abundance across tissues arises from shared demands for mitochondrial energy production and quality control, potentially amplified in metabolically active or reproductive/prostatic cell populations. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than proof of direct regulation or physical interaction; testing would require independent measurements of enzyme activity, mitochondrial respiration, redox state, mitochondrial DNA status, and cell-type composition.

- **Conclusion**: Established evidence supports IBA57 as a mitochondrial iron-sulfur assembly factor that broadens the module beyond pyruvate oxidation. Its proposed connection to coordinated respiratory or metabolic capacity is a hypothesis, because the reported data do not establish a direct interaction with PDHB or the other module members.
