# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and kidney

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and kidney  
**Genes (9)**: A2M, ALB, APOA1, CLU, C3, COL4A2, COL6A2, FBN1, HBA1

### Function

The strongest interpretation is an extracellular, circulating protein module linking lipid transport, complement and humoral immunity, extracellular vesicles, and matrix or basement-membrane biology. The globally corrected terms identify blood microparticles (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 8.94e-07), endoplasmic-reticulum lumen (GO:CC endoplasmic reticulum lumen; q_global = 8.25e-06), complement activation (GO:BP complement activation; q_global = 0.00151), receptor-ligand and signaling-regulatory activities (GO:MF receptor ligand activity; q_global = 0.00338)(GO:MF signaling receptor activator activity; q_global = 0.00403)(GO:MF signaling receptor regulator activity; q_global = 0.00438), extracellular localization (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.00473)(GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.00904), complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00981), vesicle lumens (GO:CC secretory granule lumen; q_global = 0.0109)(GO:CC cytoplasmic vesicle lumen; q_global = 0.0113)(GO:CC vesicle lumen; q_global = 0.0117), humoral immunity (GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.0116), integrin signaling (KEGG Integrin signaling - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0119), high-density lipoprotein particles (GO:CC spherical high-density lipoprotein particle; q_global = 0.013), platelet alpha-granule lumen (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.027), and extracellular vesicles or organelles (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.0356–0.0377; see the table). Together, these results support a secreted plasma-associated immune and lipid-handling module with an extracellular-matrix component. The complement arm is represented by C3, A2M, and CLU; the lipid and carrier arm by APOA1 and ALB; and the matrix arm by COL4A2, COL6A2, and FBN1. HBA1 provides an erythrocyte-associated signal rather than evidence for a core intracellular pathway.

**Cell composition.** The module is strongly dominated by secreted or extracellular proteins: HPA classifies most genes as secreted, with many secreted to blood and the matrix proteins secreted to extracellular matrix. APOA1, ALB, C3, CLU, and A2M are liver-associated or circulating proteins, while HBA1 is erythrocyte and bone-marrow or lymphoid associated. The HPA patterns also point to hepatocytes, fibroblasts, pericyte-related or stromal populations, and selected immune-cell populations. Protein detection is limited in colon and kidney for many members, although COL6A2, C3, and A2M have relevant tissue detection. Therefore, the shared colon-kidney signal should not automatically be interpreted as a resident epithelial program. It may reflect plasma carryover, vascular or stromal material, shared extracellular matrix, or shared cell types; the HBA1 signal specifically raises the possibility of erythrocyte contamination or blood content. The liver enrichment and plasma-proteome associations also make systemic protein abundance a plausible contributor.

### Novelty

This is not a wholly new molecular interaction module: the plasma-protein core is already represented by high-confidence STRING relationships and repeated hu.MAP complexes, and the matrix relationships are also known in STRING. The co-abundance observation may nevertheless be novel at the level of a conserved colon-kidney tissue pattern, particularly because it links the circulating complement or lipid cluster with the COL4A2-COL6A2-FBN1 matrix branch. That claim remains provisional because the fact sheet does not provide an external catalogue of tissue co-abundance modules, replication cohorts, or an independent comparison with previously published colon-kidney modules. The absence of COL4A2, COL6A2, or FBN1 from the listed hu.MAP complexes does not establish novelty; it mainly indicates that those relationships are not represented in the supplied complex evidence. Overall, the safest conclusion is a potentially novel tissue-pair co-abundance configuration assembled from largely known extracellular biology.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: a shared vascular or stromal extracellular environment in colon and kidney coordinates circulating lipoprotein and carrier proteins with complement regulation and matrix organization. APOA1 and ALB may mark the plasma or lipid-transport context, while C3, A2M, and CLU may mediate or buffer complement, protease, inflammatory, and extracellular-vesicle activity. In parallel, COL4A2, COL6A2, and FBN1 may represent basement-membrane or connective-tissue remodeling that alters ligand presentation and integrin-related signaling. HBA1 may reflect blood exposure rather than a causal member of the mechanism. This hypothesis predicts that the module will track vascularity, stromal content, plasma leakage, or inflammatory state more strongly than a colon- or kidney-specific epithelial function. It requires validation using cell-resolved measurements, blood-depletion or perfusion controls, and independent tissue cohorts.

**Cell composition.** This does not resemble a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and the proteins are predominantly intracellular, with nucleolar or nuclear localizations. Most genes have low tissue specificity, and nearly all have low immune-cell specificity; consequently, the module is not best explained as a blood-cell or plasma carryover signature. HPA protein detection is present for the great majority of genes in both colon and kidney, supporting a genuine shared tissue program, although detection alone does not establish cell-of-origin. A minority of genes show cell-type-enhanced patterns in proliferative, germ-cell, epithelial, renal-tubule, or progenitor populations. TOP2A and MKI67 in particular may mark a proliferative component, while the shared nucleolar program likely reflects a broadly distributed housekeeping function that is increased in cells requiring ribosome production.

### Novelty

The biological function is not a new discovery: the module recapitulates established nucleolar, preribosome, small-subunit-processome, and rRNA-processing biology, and many of its relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance observation across colon and kidney: the supplied evidence does not establish whether this exact tissue-pair module, its abundance pattern, or its integration of core ribosome-biogenesis factors with proliferation and RNA-processing factors has been reported previously. Absence of a gene or pair from hu.MAP or STRING would not prove novelty, especially because those resources emphasize physical complexes and curated or predicted interactions rather than tissue-specific co-abundance. A defensible conclusion is therefore that the module is a biologically credible conserved repurposing or rediscovery of known machinery, with tissue-pair-specific novelty remaining unproven and requiring comparison against published co-expression modules and independent tissue cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both colon and kidney, a shared population of metabolically active or renewing epithelial cells coordinates nucleolar pre-rRNA production, small-subunit assembly, RNA surveillance, and translation capacity. The dense core formed by HEATR1, NOL6, NOL10, NOL11, NOP56, PDCD11, TBL3, UTP4, UTP15, WDR3, and WDR43 may represent synchronized regulation of successive stages of SSU-rRNA maturation, while DDX21 and DHX9 could help remodel RNA structures and coordinate transcription or RNA processing. The TOP2A–MKI67 branch may indicate that the module is partly coupled to cell proliferation rather than being a purely constitutive ribosome-biogenesis signature. This hypothesis predicts that module activity should track nucleolar activity, pre-rRNA processing, and proliferative state in matched colon and kidney samples, but it should be tested experimentally rather than inferred as causation from co-abundance.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular complex. Its central CCT submodule and its translation-initiation submodule are already supported by STRING and hu.MAP, and the component functions are well established in the supplied annotations. What may be novel is the observation that these known cellular systems co-abundance together across colon and kidney in this particular tissue-pair analysis, including the less directly connected RNA-processing, aminoacylation, and purine-biosynthesis genes. That interpretation remains provisional because absence of an edge from STRING or hu.MAP does not prove biological novelty, and co-abundance can arise from shared cell state, expression breadth, or coordinated biosynthetic demand. The appropriate claim is therefore a potentially informative cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built around known machinery, rather than a proven novel pathway or complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared colon-kidney module reflects a conserved intracellular demand for proteostasis and biosynthetic throughput. The CCT/TRiC subunits may fold or stabilize proteins required for translation, cytoskeletal organization, and nuclear RNA-associated functions, while EIF4G1 and EIF3C support translation initiation; DDX46, NSUN2, and XRN2 support RNA maturation or turnover; and GMPS, IMPDH2, LARS1, and YARS1 help maintain nucleotide or aminoacyl-tRNA resources. Under this model, coordinated activity of these systems would preserve protein production and RNA handling in cells with sustained renewal or metabolic demands. The telomerase, Cajal-body, and reproductive-cell annotations may indicate links to proliferative or germ-cell-like biosynthetic states, but this is a hypothesis requiring validation by cell-resolved data, perturbation, and independent cohorts.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not new: the functions of BOP1, PES1, GTPBP4, KRR1, and RRP12 in nucleolar RNA processing and the SNRNP200-EFTUD2 relationship in spliceosomal machinery are already represented in STRING, hu.MAP, curated annotations, and the literature. However, the conserved co-abundance module shared by colon and kidney may still be a new systems-level observation, because the fact sheet does not establish that this exact tissue-pair module, its particular gene combination, or its cross-tissue conservation has previously been reported. The module should therefore be described as a potentially novel tissue-co-abundance configuration built from largely established molecular machinery, not as discovery of a new molecular complex or an entirely new biological function. Absence of hu.MAP membership for several genes is insufficient by itself to establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both colon and kidney, coordinated abundance of a nucleolar rRNA-maturation program supports production and quality control of ribosomal large-subunit precursors, while coupling to spliceosomal RNA-processing capacity through SNRNP200 and EFTUD2 helps maintain mature transcript production. The shared module may therefore reflect a common demand for high-fidelity RNA maturation and protein-synthesis capacity across these tissues. This hypothesis predicts that perturbing the BOP1-PES1-GTPBP4-centered nucleolar arm would alter preribosome or LSU-rRNA maturation, whereas perturbing the SNRNP200-EFTUD2 arm would preferentially affect spliceosomal assembly and pre-mRNA processing. The co-abundance data support coordination, but they do not by themselves prove physical interaction, causal direction, or tissue-specific regulation.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a newly discovered molecular complex. Many of its relationships and substructures are already documented by STRING and hu.MAP, and the individual gene functions are established. Nevertheless, the conserved co-abundance of this particular combination across colon and kidney may represent a potentially new systems-level observation, especially because it joins transcription, chromatin remodeling, RNA export and splicing, and ribosome biogenesis in one cross-tissue abundance pattern. The evidence supports novelty of the cross-tissue co-abundance configuration as a hypothesis, not proof of a new pathway. Absence of database links for some pairs does not establish novelty, because the module contains indirect relationships and databases are incomplete. Confirmation in independent cohorts, testing of preservation across additional tissues, and perturbation or activity measurements would be needed to establish that the cross-tissue module is biologically distinct rather than a recurring expression signature of broadly active nuclear machinery.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: coordinated activity of transcription and chromatin-remodeling machinery may increase or stabilize production of RNA transcripts, while TREX-associated export, spliceosome components, RNA-binding factors, and nucleolar processome components coordinate maturation, export, and ribosome production. Chromatin and DNA-damage regulators may help maintain transcriptional competence and genome integrity during this activity. In this model, the shared colon-kidney abundance pattern reflects a conserved demand for coupled nuclear RNA production, processing, surveillance, and translation-support functions. This is a hypothesis based on the convergence of (GO:BP nucleic acid metabolic process; q_global = 3.26e-07), (GO:BP RNA biosynthetic process; q_global = 3.7e-07), (GO:CC nuclear protein-containing complex; q_global = 5.36e-07), (GO:BP RNA processing; q_global = 0.000126), (GO:BP mRNA processing; q_global = 0.000484), (GO:MF RNA binding; q_global = 0.00264), (GO:BP RNA splicing; q_global = 0.00776), (GO:CC ribonucleoprotein complex; q_global = 0.0451), STRING links (STRING Process Nucleic acid metabolic process; within-query FDR = 5.01e-08), (STRING Process RNA metabolic process; within-query FDR = 2.55e-06), (STRING Process RNA processing; within-query FDR = 1.35e-05), and hu.MAP assemblies (hu.MAP huMAP3_09943.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_11539.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_09101.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_00746.1); the co-abundance data do not by themselves demonstrate physical assembly, causal regulation, or increased pathway activity.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular complex. Its central biology is already grounded in known nuclear RNA-processing, transcription, nuclear-pore, and ribonucleoprotein relationships from STRING and hu.MAP. However, the specific coordinated co-abundance pattern across colon and kidney, and the grouping of several established subassemblies across tissues, may be a new systems-level observation. Absence of some genes or edges from STRING or hu.MAP does not prove novelty because those resources are incomplete and measure physical or curated relationships rather than tissue co-abundance. The defensible claim is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance organization of largely known nuclear RNA machinery, requiring replication in independent tissue cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared demand for maintaining nuclear gene expression in colon and kidney cells. A coordinated program may couple transcription initiation and elongation through GTF3C1, GTF3C2, UBTF, LARP7, SIN3A, and TCERG1 with RNA remodeling and splicing through DDX5, RBM39, SF3A1, SRRT, SNIP1, and SYMPK. NUP107 and NUP93 may connect RNA maturation with nucleocytoplasmic trafficking, while UPF2 may contribute to surveillance after export. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal pathway: co-abundance cannot establish physical assembly, directionality, or tissue-specific regulation. Perturbation experiments should test whether changing central RNA-processing genes alters coordinated RNA processing, nuclear export, and abundance of other module members.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to be a newly discovered molecular complex, because its central MCM pattern is already strongly represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and its functional interpretation is supported by established replication annotations (STRING Keyword DNA replication; within-query FDR = 2.75e-10)(STRING Component MCM complex; within-query FDR = 2.86e-08)(STRING KEGG DNA replication; within-query FDR = 3.7e-08)(hu.MAP huMAP3_05529.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_14758.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_08554.1). A potentially useful finding is instead the conserved co-abundance of this known replication machinery, together with FEN1, RRM1, NASP, SMC2, KATNAL1 and OGFOD1, across colon and kidney. That cross-tissue organization may be novel in this dataset, but novelty cannot be claimed solely from the absence of some peripheral genes from STRING or hu.MAP. Demonstrating a new finding would require comparison with prior tissue co-abundance studies, independent cohorts, cell-composition analyses and ideally experimental validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module captures a shared proliferative-state program in colon and kidney. The MCM proteins may form the replication-licensing and helicase core, NASP may support histone handling and replication-coupled chromatin assembly, RRM1 may help supply deoxyribonucleotides, FEN1 may process Okazaki-fragment and repair intermediates, and SMC2 may coordinate chromosome organization during cell division. KATNAL1 and OGFOD1 may represent peripheral or state-associated links rather than established components of the replication core. The progenitor-cell enrichment in HPA suggests that cellular composition or the presence of cycling progenitors could contribute to the shared signal. This model predicts that module abundance should track proliferation and replication markers, and that its apparent conservation should weaken after adjustment for cell-cycle state and cell-type composition.

### Novelty

The biological core is not a new discovery: the LMNB1–LMNB2 association, lamin-tail annotation, nuclear-lamina localization, and several lamin-containing complexes are already established by STRING, hu.MAP, and curated gene annotations. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance configuration linking the lamin pair with MCM3AP, ZC3H13, and SP3 across colon and kidney. That configuration is hypothesis-generating rather than proven to be a new physical complex, especially because the non-lamin links lack reported STRING or hu.MAP support. Absence from those resources alone cannot establish novelty; the appropriate conclusion is that the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern may be novel or under-characterized, but requires replication, perturbation, and physical-interaction testing.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved nuclear structural-regulatory state in which the LMNB1–LMNB2 lamina scaffold coordinates nuclear-envelope organization with transcription, RNA processing, and genome-maintenance activities. MCM3AP may connect the lamina to DNA replication and mRNA export, while ZC3H13 may connect nuclear architecture to RNA methylation and processing, and SP3 may connect the state to transcriptional control. The observed co-abundance could arise from coordinated regulation in shared cellular states, including proliferative or lineage-specific states, rather than direct binding among every module member. This hypothesis should be tested by measuring nuclear localization, chromatin association, and perturbation effects on RNA processing, transcription, and replication.
