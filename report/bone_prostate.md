### Function

The dominant function is mitochondrial inner-membrane respiration and energy production. Globally corrected evidence supports mitochondrial inner membrane and mitochondrial envelope localization (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 2.06e-06–7.22e-05; see the table), respiratory-chain organization (8 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000251–0.0235; see the table), aerobic electron transport and ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport (11 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000257–0.0404; see the table), and oxidative phosphorylation (KEGG Oxidative phosphorylation - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000595) (GO:BP oxidative phosphorylation; q_global = 0.000982). The module also contains a distinct, coherent DNA-replication and repair component formed by MCM2, MCM5, MCM6 and MCM7, supported by the MCM, CMG and DNA-replication initiation terms (9 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00329–0.0465; see the table). Thus, this is best interpreted as a mitochondrial respiratory module with a linked cell-proliferation or genome-maintenance signal, rather than as a purely mitochondrial pathway. The fatty-liver, diabetic-cardiomyopathy, Parkinson-disease and reactive-oxygen-species KEGG annotations (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000184–0.0407; see the table) should be regarded as disease-context mappings of respiratory-chain genes, not evidence that the module specifically causes those diseases.

**Cell composition.** The module is not predominantly composed of plasma or secreted proteins and is not strongly blood-cell enriched. HPA reports low tissue specificity for many genes, with mitochondrial and broadly expressed housekeeping-like respiratory genes prominent; tissue-enhanced genes occur particularly in muscle, tongue, liver and bone marrow. Most genes have low immune-cell specificity, arguing against a primarily blood-cell-derived module, although isolated genes have immune-cell annotations. Six genes are secreted or associated with secretion, including C1QBP, NUCB2, RNPEP, ALCAM, SORD-related annotations and PHB1-related secretome information, but this is a minority pattern and does not explain the module as a whole. Prostate protein detection is widespread in the available HPA summary, whereas bone has no matching HPA tissue-protein data, so the shared bone–prostate signal may partly reflect broadly expressed cellular machinery or shared metabolically active cell types rather than a bone- or prostate-specific program. The presence of MCM genes and selected bone-marrow-associated annotations may also indicate a proliferative-cell contribution, but the HPA summary does not establish that this is the principal source.

### Novelty

The module is not a wholly new molecular complex: its strongest portions recapitulate well-established respiratory-chain, cytochrome, prohibitin and MCM assemblies documented by STRING and hu.MAP. Its potentially novel aspect is the co-abundance of these otherwise distinct mitochondrial and DNA-replication or repair programs across the bone–prostate tissue pair, together with weaker links involving genes such as OCIAD1, TMED5, FAM114A2, PTRHD1 and MAP2K3. However, the fact sheet does not compare this module with previously published bone–prostate co-abundance modules, tissue-composition controls or independent cohorts. Consequently, novelty of the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern cannot be claimed; it should be described as a potentially new systems-level association built largely from known biological components. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP entry for individual genes is not evidence of novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved bone–prostate module reflects coordinated demand for mitochondrial respiratory capacity together with genome replication or repair in metabolically active or proliferative cells. The respiratory subnetwork could provide ATP and redox support, while the MCM subnetwork marks cells entering or maintaining replication competence. Prohibitin proteins, C1QBP, CHCHD4, SCO1 and related mitochondrial-organizing factors could provide functional coupling between mitochondrial state, stress responses and nuclear genome maintenance. This model predicts that module activity should track mitochondrial respiration and proliferative-cell abundance, rather than representing a direct physical supercomplex. It should be tested using cell-type-resolved data, independent bone and prostate cohorts, and measurements of respiratory function and replication activity.

### Novelty

The biological theme is not new: focal adhesions, integrin signaling, actin linkage, platelet-associated adhesion, and smooth-muscle or vascular functions are already established for many of these genes, and the STRING and hu.MAP evidence confirms prior knowledge. What may be new is the observation that this particular combination of genes forms a conserved co-abundance module across bone and prostate. That module-level tissue-pair observation cannot be established as previously reported or novel from STRING or hu.MAP absence alone. The limited hu.MAP coverage for many genes and the absence of some module-specific edges from STRING do not prove novelty. A cautious conclusion is that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built largely from a known focal-adhesion network; demonstrating genuine novelty would require comparison with prior co-abundance studies, tissue-specific datasets, and independent cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a shared stromal, vascular, smooth-muscle, or platelet-associated adhesion state in bone and prostate. Integrin alpha five, talin, kindlin, ILK, parvin, vinculin, and filamin proteins could couple extracellular-matrix engagement to actin remodeling, contractility, migration, and wound repair, while gelsolin, annexins, myosin light-chain kinase, and related proteins could tune calcium-sensitive membrane and cytoskeletal responses. The repeated HPA association with smooth-muscle cells, megakaryocytes, platelets, and blood vessels raises the alternative hypothesis that part of the cross-tissue signal is driven by shared cellular composition or blood contamination rather than identical tissue-cell biology. This should be tested by cell-type deconvolution, microscopy or spatial data, and perturbation of integrin-linked adhesion components in relevant bone and prostate cell models.

### Novelty

The module may represent a novel conserved co-abundance pattern shared by bone and prostate, but the available evidence does not justify claiming a newly discovered molecular mechanism. **Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared cell-state program in which cytoskeletal remodeling and vesicle trafficking support migration, adhesion, membrane turnover, and inflammatory communication in bone and prostate.
