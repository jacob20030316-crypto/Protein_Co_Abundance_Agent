# Conserved co-abundance modules: liver and lung

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: liver and lung  
**Genes (29)**: SLC3A2, ACSL3, ATP1A1, CCAR1, TUFM, EMC1, TBRG4, GTF2I, ME2, MTFP1, NDUFAF4, NUP62, ALDH18A1, POLDIP2, PNPT1, NUP210, PITRM1, RAB10, RAB21, SEC63, SRRT, SUCLA2, SUPV3L1, DARS2, TECR, TMEM214, TMX1, TNPO1, VDAC2

### Function

The strongest supported function is mitochondrial RNA processing, based on (GO:BP mitochondrial RNA processing; q_global = 0.0414), which links PNPT1, SUPV3L1, and FASTKD4. This is biologically coherent with the mitochondrial localization and annotations of PNPT1 and SUPV3L1, including mitochondrial RNA processing, RNA degradation, and mitochondrial RNA end processing. The broader network also contains mitochondrial respiratory-chain assembly, mitochondrial translation, mitochondrial metabolism, mitochondrial dynamics, and mitochondrial protein quality-control components. Additional branches involve nuclear transport, ER protein translocation, membrane lipid metabolism, vesicle trafficking, and ion transport. These broader themes are mechanistic context rather than additional globally significant GO or KEGG findings. No STRING functional-enrichment term was reported. Overall, the module most plausibly represents a conserved organelle-maintenance program centered on mitochondrial RNA metabolism, with connected ER, nuclear-envelope, and membrane functions.

**Cell composition.** The module does not appear to be a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: HPA identifies only one secreted gene, and nearly all genes have low immune-cell specificity. Thus, a blood-cell or plasma carryover explanation is not supported by the supplied HPA evidence. Most genes have low tissue specificity, while the tissue-enhanced genes are distributed across several tissues rather than concentrated specifically in liver or lung. Many genes are detected at the protein level in both liver and lung, supporting genuine representation in both tissues, although protein detection is incomplete for some genes. Cell-type-enhanced annotations are widespread and include hepatocytes, alveolar cells, epithelial populations, progenitor populations, and reproductive cell types. This breadth suggests a largely intracellular, broadly used organelle and membrane program rather than a liver- or lung-specific cell identity. Differences in cell composition between samples could still contribute to co-abundance, but the low immune specificity argues against a simple blood contamination explanation.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular complex. Several of its relationships are already represented in STRING or hu.MAP, especially the PNPT1-SUPV3L1 mitochondrial RNA-processing relationship and parts of the nuclear-transport, ER, mitochondrial, and membrane-associated complexes. Conversely, the integrated liver-lung co-abundance pattern and its specific cross-organelle wiring are not established merely because the complete gene set is absent from a reference database. Database absence alone cannot prove novelty. The defensible conclusion is that this may be a new tissue-pair co-abundance configuration or a new systems-level association of partly known biology, but its novelty requires independent replication, comparison with published tissue co-expression modules, and experimental or perturbational validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared liver-lung module reflects coordinated maintenance of mitochondrial gene expression and organelle proteostasis, centered on the PNPT1-SUPV3L1 RNA-processing axis and coupled to mitochondrial translation, respiratory-chain support, metabolite exchange, and mitochondrial dynamics. Coupling to nuclear pores, transportin, ER translocation, lipid handling, and vesicle traffic may coordinate import and export of proteins and metabolites required for organelle function. The co-abundance signal could therefore arise from a conserved cellular stress-resilience or high-metabolic-demand program operating across liver and lung cells. This is a hypothesis rather than proof of direct regulatory control among all module members.

## Module 1

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a conserved nuclear RNA-management and ribonucleoprotein module. The primary evidence is enrichment for ribonucleoprotein complex (GO:CC ribonucleoprotein complex; q_global = 0.0102), RNA processing (GO:BP RNA processing; q_global = 0.0165), and nuclear protein-containing complex (GO:CC nuclear protein-containing complex; q_global = 0.0282). Its genes span pre-mRNA processing and RNA-binding functions through HNRNPR and ACIN1, RNA helicase and RNA-surveillance functions through DHX9 and UPF1, and nucleolar ribosome-biogenesis functions through NOP56, PDCD11, and RPL12. SUPT16H adds a chromatin-transcription and transcription-elongation connection. Thus, the most coherent interpretation is coordinated RNA processing, RNA quality control, rRNA maturation, and nuclear gene-expression infrastructure rather than a tissue-specific metabolic pathway. The globally corrected terms support this interpretation; the STRING terms (STRING RCTM Metabolism of RNA; within-query FDR = 0.0015), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Ribonucleoprotein complex; within-query FDR = 0.0046), (STRING Component Ribonucleoprotein complex; within-query FDR = 0.0096), and (STRING RCTM Major pathway of rRNA processing in the nucleolus and cytosol; within-query FDR = 0.0487) are concordant supplementary evidence.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and the proteins are detected in liver and nearly all are detected in lung. Most genes have low tissue specificity and low immune-cell specificity, which is consistent with broadly expressed housekeeping-type RNA and chromatin machinery. The module is therefore unlikely to be explained primarily by circulating proteins or a dominant blood-cell signal. Some genes show cell-type enrichment, including AMPD2 in brain and several myeloid cell categories, but these are isolated features rather than a module-wide immune signature. AMPD2 is also protein-detected in liver but not lung, so it may represent a context-dependent peripheral component rather than the core conserved program.

### Novelty

The module is not demonstrably a newly discovered molecular complex. Several of its central relationships and functions are already supported by STRING, hu.MAP, curated gene annotations, and extensive literature. Its potentially novel aspect is the conserved liver-lung co-abundance organization that brings nucleolar rRNA machinery, RNA helicases, RNA-binding proteins, RNA surveillance, and chromatin-transcription machinery into one cross-tissue module. However, absence of a relationship from STRING or hu.MAP would not establish novelty, and the fact sheet does not provide an independent perturbation, biochemical interaction, or longitudinal validation. The appropriate conclusion is that this is a potentially informative cross-tissue network configuration, not yet a proven new pathway or complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared liver-lung module represents a conserved cellular RNA-homeostasis program that coordinates transcriptional access to chromatin, pre-mRNA handling, RNA surveillance, and ribosome biogenesis. SUPT16H may couple transcription elongation to the RNA-processing environment; DHX9 and HNRNPR may coordinate RNA remodeling and splice-associated handling; UPF1 may monitor and remove defective transcripts; and NOP56 with PDCD11 and RPL12 may support maturation and utilization of ribosomal RNA. The co-abundance pattern could therefore reflect a shared demand for reliable gene expression in hepatocytes and lung cells. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not an established causal model; it should be tested by perturbing selected genes and measuring splicing, RNA surveillance, rRNA maturation, and cellular stress responses in relevant liver and lung cell systems.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular pathway. Its central proteasome, CCT chaperonin, aminoacyl-tRNA-synthetase, and RNA-binding relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the primary GO/KEGG terms correspond to established biology. A potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance organization of these partly distinct systems across liver and lung, especially the way proteasome, folding, translation, RNA processing, ER quality control, and transport-related genes are connected in one cross-tissue module. However, co-abundance novelty requires comparison with prior tissue co-expression studies and independent replication; absence of a relationship from STRING or hu.MAP alone would not establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a shared cellular proteostasis state in liver and lung in which newly synthesized or stress-affected proteins are folded by CCT chaperones, surveyed by ER quality-control machinery, and routed through ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal turnover when damaged or surplus. Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and translation-initiation machinery may couple this state to protein synthesis, while RNA-binding and nuclear proteins may coordinate transcript processing or localization. The co-abundance signal could therefore arise from coordinated demand for protein synthesis, folding, quality control, and degradation in common parenchymal or epithelial cell states. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of direct physical cooperation among every module member.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular pathway. Its central aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase component is already well established by globally significant GO/KEGG enrichment, dense STRING relationships and hu.MAP complexes. Several peripheral relationships are also known, including spliceosomal, spectrin, dynein-dynactin and vesicle-trafficking associations. The potentially novel aspect is the tissue-pair co-abundance organization: a conserved liver-lung expression module that brings together a translation-fidelity core with RNA modification, RNA processing, intracellular transport and cytoskeletal components. Absence of some edges from STRING or some genes from hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because those resources capture known physical or curated relationships rather than every co-abundance relationship. Novelty therefore remains a hypothesis requiring replication in independent liver and lung cohorts, comparison with published co-expression modules, and testing whether the peripheral genes track the synthetase core after adjustment for cell composition and overall expression level.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared liver and lung cell populations, increased demand for protein synthesis or proteostatic maintenance coordinately raises aminoacyl-tRNA charging, tRNA modification, translation initiation, RNA surveillance and protein-folding capacity. NSUN2 may help preserve accurate tRNA decoding, while AARS1, IARS1, LARS1, QARS1, TARS1, VARS1, YARS1 and related synthetases support charging and translational fidelity. The connected XPO1, SNRNP200, SNRPD1 and EIF3C genes could couple RNA export, splicing and translation, whereas CLTC, DCTN1, DYNC1H1, USO1 and COPB1 could couple this biosynthetic state to intracellular transport. This model predicts coordinated protein-level changes and sensitivity to translational or proteotoxic stress, but co-abundance alone does not establish causal regulation or a physical complex linking every module member.

This is an extracellular-matrix structural module centered on collagen assembly, basement-membrane organization, and matrix interactions. ### Novelty

This module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular complex or pathway. The collagen-six relationships, COL4A2 associations, ECM functions, and larger collagen-related complexes are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the enrichment is strongly concordant with those established annotations. At the same time, database representation does not determine whether the conserved liver–lung co-abundance module is novel. Its potentially new aspect is the tissue-pair-specific, coordinated abundance pattern linking collagen-six chains, collagen IV alpha two, and perlecan across these tissues. Demonstrating novelty would require comparison with prior liver–lung co-expression studies, independent cohorts, cell-composition-adjusted analyses, and experimental validation. Absence of HSPG2 from STRING or hu.MAP for this module is not evidence of a novel interaction by itself.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared stromal or vascular cell population in liver and lung produces a coordinated basement-membrane and interstitial-matrix program. Collagen-six chains may assemble into beaded microfibrils, collagen IV alpha two may contribute to basement-membrane structure and signaling, and perlecan may cross-link matrix components and regulate access of cell-surface receptors to matrix cues. Through integrin, focal-adhesion, and related signaling interfaces, this matrix could influence tissue stiffness, cell adhesion, vascular or epithelial support, and repair responses. This hypothesis predicts that the module will track fibroblast, hepatic-stellate, vascular, or smooth-muscle abundance and that its associations will weaken after adjustment for those cell populations. It also predicts coordinated protein or spatial localization of the encoded matrix components, which is not established by the co-abundance data alone.
