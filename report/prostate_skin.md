# Conserved co-abundance modules: prostate and skin

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: prostate and skin  
**Genes (8)**: AASS, ADAM10, DSP, FKBP5, AGL, JUP, PLXNB2, TRAPPC4

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a cell-junction and epithelial-adhesion module, centered on the desmosomal pair DSP and JUP. DSP and JUP are the only genes contributing to the globally corrected GO evidence: cardiac muscle cell adhesion, bundle of His cell to Purkinje myocyte communication, related adhesion, and adhesive protein binding (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.0382–0.0382; see the table). Their established roles in desmosome structure and adhesion make an epithelial barrier or tissue-integrity interpretation more plausible than a specific cardiac interpretation. The remaining genes suggest connected processes involving membrane proteolysis and adhesion through ADAM10, receptor-mediated signaling and cell migration through PLXNB2, protein folding and stress-related regulation through FKBP5, glycogen metabolism through AGL, lysine catabolism through AASS, and vesicle trafficking through TRAPPC4. Because the module contains several functionally diverse genes and only the DSP–JUP pair has direct high-confidence interaction evidence, the enrichment should not be interpreted as proof that the whole module is a cardiac-specific program.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: only one gene is annotated as secreted, while the other genes are intracellular, membrane-associated, junctional, vesicular, mitochondrial, nucleoplasmic, or cytosolic. HPA supports an epithelial component, especially skin, esophageal, ocular, and keratinocyte-associated cell types. DSP and JUP are tissue enhanced in esophagus and skin, and both are associated with epithelial cell types. Several genes also have immune or blood-cell associations, including DSP, JUP, PLXNB2, and FKBP5-related cell-type signals, so shared immune-cell contribution or sample composition cannot be excluded. Nevertheless, the module does not look like a purely blood-cell module: most genes have low immune specificity or are not detected in immune cells, and protein detection is broad in skin and mostly present in prostate. The shared prostate–skin signal may therefore reflect epithelial or junctional cell content, with a possible contribution from common immune or stromal populations.

### STRING and hu.MAP

STRING identifies only one high-confidence relationship within the module, DSP–JUP, supporting a known desmosomal or junctional core. STRING reports no functional enrichment for the module, so it provides no additional globally corrected pathway interpretation. hu.MAP independently places DSP and JUP together in several epithelial or junction-associated complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_05845.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_08595.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_09125.1), and (hu.MAP huMAP3_09929.1). ADAM10 and PLXNB2 occur together in separate hu.MAP complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_06775.1) and (hu.MAP huMAP3_09327.1). These results indicate that the module contains at least two established molecular neighborhoods: a DSP–JUP adhesion core and an ADAM10–PLXNB2 membrane-associated group. The other module edges are not supported by the listed STRING or hu.MAP relationships and may represent co-abundance, indirect regulation, shared cell composition, or tissue-specific coordination rather than direct physical binding.

### Novelty

The biological functions represented here are not new: desmosomal adhesion involving DSP and JUP, ADAM10 membrane proteolysis, PLXNB2 signaling, and the other annotated processes are established. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance configuration across prostate and skin, particularly the connection of the DSP–JUP junctional core with metabolic, trafficking, stress-response, and membrane-receptor genes. However, the fact sheet does not provide an external catalogue of previously reported prostate–skin co-abundance modules, so this arrangement cannot be claimed as a new discovery. The limited STRING connectivity and partial hu.MAP support argue for a mixed or context-dependent module, not by themselves for novelty. A novelty claim would require comparison with independent prostate and skin cohorts, published coexpression modules, and tissue-composition controls.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this conserved module reflects coordinated epithelial tissue integrity in prostate and skin. DSP and JUP may define a desmosomal adhesion scaffold, while ADAM10 and PLXNB2 could modulate membrane receptor processing, cell–cell adhesion, and migration around that scaffold. FKBP5, AGL, AASS, and TRAPPC4 may represent linked stress, metabolic, and vesicle-trafficking states that support junction maintenance or reflect the physiological state of the participating epithelial cells. An alternative hypothesis is that the module is partly driven by shared epithelial and immune-cell composition rather than by direct regulation among all genes. This can be tested by single-cell deconvolution, perturbation of DSP or JUP, and measurement of junctional integrity, receptor shedding, trafficking, and metabolic responses.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a mitochondrial energy-production and inner-membrane organization module. The primary evidence is the strong, globally corrected enrichment for mitochondrial membrane, inner mitochondrial membrane, mitochondrial envelope and mitochondrion (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.65e-10–3.32e-06; see the table), together with mitochondrial matrix (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.000433), respiratory-chain complex (GO:CC respiratory chain complex; q_global = 0.00674), oxidative phosphorylation (GO:BP oxidative phosphorylation; q_global = 0.00219) and cellular respiration (GO:BP cellular respiration; q_global = 0.00832). The transporter terms (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000185–0.00128; see the table) and (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00271–0.0054; see the table), (GO:BP monoatomic cation transmembrane transport; q_global = 0.0088) (GO:MF primary active transmembrane transporter activity; q_global = 0.0104) (GO:MF active monoatomic ion transmembrane transporter activity; q_global = 0.0104), (GO:MF proton transmembrane transporter activity; q_global = 0.0128), (GO:MF transporter activity; q_global = 0.0169) and (GO:BP monoatomic cation transport; q_global = 0.0299) indicate coordinated membrane transport, including proton and cation transport. The module also contains genes involved in mitochondrial protein import and inner-membrane organization, including TIMM44, TIMM50, AGK, IMMT and PHB1, as well as fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism genes such as CPT2, ETFA, GOT2, PC, SHMT2 and SLC25A13. The disease-pathway enrichments involving thermogenesis (KEGG Thermogenesis - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00185), diabetic cardiomyopathy (KEGG Diabetic cardiomyopathy - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00311), Alzheimer disease (KEGG Alzheimer disease - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0123), cardiac muscle contraction (KEGG Cardiac muscle contraction - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0224) and neurodegeneration (KEGG Pathways of neurodegeneration - multiple diseases - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0306) should be treated as pathway overlap with mitochondrial biology, not as evidence that the module specifically causes any of those diseases. The module therefore most plausibly represents coordinated mitochondrial respiratory capacity, substrate handling, protein import and membrane homeostasis in cells present in both tissues.

**Cell composition.** This does not look like a plasma-protein, secreted-protein or blood-cell module. The HPA summary identifies only a single secreted gene, and most genes have low immune-cell specificity. The module is detected at the protein level in both prostate and skin for nearly all genes, with only limited exceptions, supporting a genuine tissue-observed cellular program rather than a signal confined to one tissue. Its composition is nevertheless not uniformly tissue-specific: many genes are broadly expressed or have low tissue specificity, while the enriched tissue patterns include tongue, liver, skeletal muscle, heart muscle and parathyroid gland. Cell-type annotations frequently involve hepatocytes, late spermatids, neutrophils, esophageal basal cells and cytotrophoblasts, but the blood-cell summary is predominantly low specificity. Thus, contamination by circulating blood is not the leading interpretation, although shared epithelial, metabolic or other cell-type composition could contribute to the prostate–skin conservation. HPA locations and annotations are dominated by mitochondria and membranes, consistent with the enrichment results.

### Novelty

The transport machinery itself is not a new biological discovery: its components, interactions and complex memberships are already represented by STRING, hu.MAP and gene annotations. The module therefore should not be claimed as a newly discovered transport complex. Its potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization across prostate and skin, particularly the way established nuclear transport genes are co-abundant with lipid metabolism, RNA processing, proteostasis, endoplasmic-reticulum quality control and vesicle-trafficking genes. That cross-tissue network observation is a candidate finding, not proof of a new mechanism. Absence of some genes or edges from STRING or hu.MAP cannot establish novelty, because those resources emphasize known physical or curated relationships and may not capture tissue-specific co-abundance. Replication in independent prostate and skin cohorts, control for cell composition, and perturbation or localization studies would be needed to support a genuinely novel mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved cellular state in which nuclear pore function and karyopherin-mediated transport are coordinated with the supply of biosynthetic metabolites, RNA maturation, protein folding and intracellular trafficking. In this model, XPO1, CSE1L, TNPO1, NUP93 and NUP155 form the transport-centered core; ACLY, FASN and MTHFD1 provide metabolic support for growth or repair; TCERG1 and SRRT couple nuclear transport to RNA processing; and HSPH1, GANAB and CLTC connect the state to proteostasis and vesicular handling. This is a mechanistic model inferred from co-abundance and annotations, not an established causal pathway. It predicts that perturbing nuclear transport should alter the coordinated abundance or localization of selected metabolic and RNA-processing proteins in both tissue contexts.

### Novelty

The biological function of the central submodule is not a new discovery: the MCM complex, CMG complex, replication initiation machinery, MAD2L1 checkpoint function, and SMC2 chromosome-condensation function are established. The STRING and hu.MAP results strongly reinforce that interpretation rather than revealing an uncharacterized complex. A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance of this composite module across prostate and skin, including its peripheral links to trafficking, translation, metabolism, vesicle acidification, apoptosis, and epithelial junction biology. However, the available evidence does not establish that this cross-tissue co-abundance organization is new. Absence of database support for many peripheral relationships cannot prove novelty, because those relationships may be indirect, context-dependent, or specific to the sampled tissues. The appropriate conclusion is that the module contains a well-known replication core embedded in a potentially interesting but presently unvalidated tissue-pair co-abundance context.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved prostate–skin module reflects coordinated abundance of proliferating or progenitor-like cells in both tissues. Within these cells, MCM2, MCM6, and MCM7 may mark replication licensing and fork-initiation capacity, while MAD2L1 and SMC2 may couple that state to spindle surveillance and chromosome organization. The surrounding genes could reflect accompanying demands for intracellular transport, translation, vesicle function, lipid or isoprenoid metabolism, epithelial junction maintenance, and stress or apoptotic regulation. An alternative explanation is that the apparent conservation is partly driven by shared hematopoietic or immune-cell content, especially given the HPA enrichment for bone marrow, lymphoid, neutrophil, and progenitor populations. This hypothesis should be tested by cell-type-resolved expression, proliferation-state markers, and validation of the peripheral module edges in independent prostate and skin cohorts.

### Novelty

The molecular components themselves are not new findings. The CCT chaperonin, proteasome regulatory particle, spliceosomal machinery, translation-initiation machinery, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase relationships, and ribosome-biogenesis relationships are already supported by STRING and hu.MAP. The globally corrected GO/KEGG pattern is also consistent with established functions of these proteins. What may be novel is the observation that these established components form one conserved co-abundance module across prostate and skin, particularly the coordinated association of CCT-mediated folding with telomerase-RNA localization, proteasome activity, RNA processing, and nucleotide metabolism. The fact sheet does not provide an external tissue-pair replication study or literature comparison demonstrating that this exact module has not been reported. Therefore, the appropriate conclusion is a potentially novel tissue-pair co-abundance configuration, not a claim of a newly discovered complex or pathway. Lack of hu.MAP or STRING annotation for individual genes such as HMGN5, PSIP1, or RSL1D1 does not by itself establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both prostate and skin, a shared cellular state increases demand for chaperone-assisted folding, RNA maturation, translation, genome maintenance, and removal of damaged or misfolded proteins. The CCT machinery may help maintain folding of proteins required for nuclear-body and telomere functions, while RUVBL2 and XRCC6-linked genome-maintenance activities support telomere and DNA-repair processes. RNA-processing factors such as SF3A1 and DDX46 may coordinate maturation and localization of nuclear RNAs, including telomerase-associated RNA, and the proteasome group may remove damaged or short-lived components generated by this activity. Nucleotide-production genes may supply the metabolic capacity needed for RNA and DNA synthesis. Under this model, the module is a coordinated proteostasis and genome-surveillance state rather than a single physical complex. The testis-associated HPA pattern raises the additional hypothesis that a germ-cell-like or highly proliferative transcriptional state contributes to the co-abundance signal, but this requires validation with cell-composition markers, independent cohorts, and perturbation experiments.
