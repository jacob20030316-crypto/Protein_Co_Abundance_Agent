# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and testis

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and testis  
**Genes (12)**: ORM2, A1BG, A2M, SERPINA3, SERPINC1, CP, CFB, CFI, C3, HRG, KNG1, GC

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating extracellular defense and hemostasis program centered on complement regulation, protease inhibition, coagulation-related proteins, and acute-phase plasma proteins. The strongest primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for blood microparticle (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 6.66e-11), complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 7.26e-05), platelet alpha-granule structures (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.000118) and (GO:CC platelet alpha granule; q_global = 0.000179), peptidase and endopeptidase inhibitor activities (GO:MF endopeptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.000294) through (GO:MF peptidase regulator activity; q_global = 0.000949), humoral immune response (GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.00359), extracellular vesicle and extracellular-space compartments (GO:CC extracellular exosome; q_global = 0.00368) through (GO:CC cytoplasmic vesicle lumen; q_global = 0.00929) and (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.0174), defense and immune-response regulation (GO:BP defense response; q_global = 0.0138), (GO:BP complement activation; q_global = 0.0148), (GO:BP regulation of immune system process; q_global = 0.0414), and (GO:BP regulation of immune response; q_global = 0.0459), and serine-type endopeptidase inhibition (GO:MF serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0195). The complete membership of the extracellular-vesicle and extracellular-space terms (GO:CC extracellular exosome; q_global = 0.00368) through (GO:CC extracellular vesicle; q_global = 0.0039), (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.00858), (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.0174), and (GO:CC vesicle; q_global = 0.0455) indicates a coherent secreted-protein program rather than a cellular metabolic pathway. C3, CFB, and CFI support complement activation and its restraint; A2M, SERPINA3, SERPINC1, HRG, and KNG1 support protease and coagulation control; and ORM2 is consistent with an acute-phase immune context. Thus, the most defensible functional description is a plasma-derived complement, protease-control, coagulation, and inflammatory-defense module.

**Cell composition.** The module is overwhelmingly composed of secreted proteins: HPA classifies every member as secreted to blood. The HPA profile is strongly liver-centered, with hepatocytes repeatedly represented, while immune-cell detection is absent for most genes and only a small subset shows immune-cell enrichment or enhancement. This argues against interpreting the module primarily as a colon or testis tissue-intrinsic pathway. It is more consistent with plasma carryover, vascular or extracellular material, or a shared systemic compartment present in both tissue sample types. The blood-microparticle and platelet-alpha-granule enrichments (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 6.66e-11), (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.000118), and (GO:CC platelet alpha granule; q_global = 0.000179), together with STRING secreted and blood-microparticle annotations (STRING Component Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 1.17e-12), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 1.23e-12), and (STRING Keyword Secreted; within-query FDR = 4.21e-10), reinforce this interpretation. HPA protein detection is limited in both colon and testis for much of the module, although C3 and GC are detected in testis, GC is detected in both tissues, and A2M and SERPINA3 are detected in both tissues. Therefore, shared co-abundance should not by itself be taken as evidence that these proteins are produced by the same resident cells in colon and testis.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to represent a wholly new molecular system. Its central biology is already well established through complement and coagulation annotations (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 7.26e-05) and (GO:BP complement activation; q_global = 0.0148), extensive STRING relationships, and repeated hu.MAP plasma-protein complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_15040.1) through (hu.MAP huMAP3_07541.1). The shared appearance in colon and testis may nevertheless be a useful dataset-level observation, particularly if the co-abundance relationship is reproducible after controlling for plasma contamination, vascular content, sample handling, and tissue cellular composition. The potentially novel aspect is therefore the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern or its robustness in this specific study, not the underlying functions of the proteins. Absence of an interaction from STRING or hu.MAP would not demonstrate novelty, and the presence of many known relationships argues for cautious rather than strong novelty claims.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this conserved module reflects a shared circulating plasma compartment sampled in both colon and testis. Liver-derived secretion supplies complement, protease inhibitors, acute-phase glycoproteins, metal- and vitamin-binding proteins, and coagulation-associated factors. In extracellular or blood-microparticle material, C3 and CFB could provide alternative-pathway complement activity, CFI and A2M could constrain proteolytic and complement amplification, SERPINC1 could restrain thrombin and related serine proteases, and KNG1 and HRG could connect coagulation, kallikrein-kinin, platelet, and antimicrobial processes. The coordinated abundance may therefore arise from common plasma exposure or vascular material rather than coordinated transcription by colon and testis cells. This hypothesis should be tested with measurements of plasma contamination, vascular or platelet markers, cell deconvolution, and independent protein-level validation in tissue and matched blood.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating plasma defense and protease-control program. The strongest primary evidence is for blood microparticle localization (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 1.41e-06), serine-type and broader peptidase inhibitor activity (GO:MF serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 5.94e-05)(GO:MF endopeptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.000742)(GO:MF peptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.000742)(GO:MF endopeptidase regulator activity; q_global = 0.00127)(GO:MF peptidase regulator activity; q_global = 0.00232)(GO:MF enzyme inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0183)(GO:MF molecular function inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0349), and complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00395). The same genes also support defense response, humoral immunity, acute inflammation, immunoglobulin-mediated immunity, and B-cell-mediated immunity (GO:BP defense response; q_global = 0.00648)(GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.00738)(GO:BP acute inflammatory response; q_global = 0.0112)(GO:BP humoral immune response mediated by circulating immunoglobulin; q_global = 0.016)(GO:BP immunoglobulin mediated immune response; q_global = 0.0428)(GO:BP B cell mediated immunity; q_global = 0.0428). Vesicle, secretory-granule, cytoplasmic-vesicle, and endoplasmic-reticulum lumen annotations (GO:CC vesicle lumen; q_global = 0.0268)(GO:CC secretory granule lumen; q_global = 0.0268)(GO:CC cytoplasmic vesicle lumen; q_global = 0.0268)(GO:CC endoplasmic reticulum lumen; q_global = 0.03) are consistent with a secreted extracellular protein program rather than a tissue-restricted intracellular pathway. Established functions represented in the module include complement regulation, inhibition of coagulation and fibrinolytic proteases, heme transport, lipid transport, iron transport, and immunoglobulin activity. The module therefore does not indicate a single narrowly defined pathway; it represents coordinated abundance of plasma proteins involved in extracellular homeostasis, innate defense, inflammation, and protease balance.

**Cell composition.** This is predominantly a secreted, liver-associated plasma-protein module. HPA assigns secreted status to the great majority of genes and shows liver enrichment for most of the module, with hepatocytes as the leading single-cell association. This composition strongly raises the possibility that the shared colon and testis signal reflects blood or plasma exposure, vascular content, leakage, or a common circulating compartment rather than expression by colon and testis parenchymal cells. The low protein detection in both tissues further supports caution in assigning local tissue production. IGHG1 and IGKC indicate a plasma-cell or B-cell contribution, while TAGLN is associated with vascular and smooth-muscle-related cells. HPA also reports associations with enterocytes, Leydig cells, and several immune populations, so a shared local cell-type contribution cannot be excluded, but the dominant interpretation remains a circulating hepatic secretome signal.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular pathway. Its dominant genes, functions, disease links, plasma localization, and complex membership are already well established, and its STRING and hu.MAP support is substantial. The potentially novel aspect is the coordinated co-abundance of this plasma and hepatic secretome program across the colon and testis tissue groups. That observation may be useful as a cross-tissue signature, but novelty cannot be established from the supplied evidence alone. In particular, absence of a STRING edge or hu.MAP membership for some genes would not demonstrate novelty. A stronger novelty claim would require independent cohorts, correction for blood contamination and tissue composition, comparison with previously published cross-tissue modules, and validation at the protein or transcript level.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects a conserved circulating extracellular defense state in which liver-derived plasma proteins enter or are sampled by both tissues and jointly regulate protease activity, complement activation, coagulation and fibrinolysis, lipid transport, and heme or iron handling. Within this state, SERPING1 may restrain complement activation, SERPINA1 and related serpins may limit inflammatory proteases, SERPINF2 may constrain plasmin-mediated fibrinolysis, HPX may buffer extracellular heme, and APOA1, ALB, and TF may provide lipid, ligand, and iron transport functions. The co-abundance signal could therefore arise from coordinated hepatic secretion and vascular exposure rather than coordinated production by colon and testis cells. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal model; tissue-resolved expression, plasma-depletion controls, and perturbation or functional assays would be needed to test it.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex. Several relationships and the spliceosomal core are already established by STRING and hu.MAP, particularly the SF3A1, DDX42, and DDX46 component and the UPF1-XRN2 relationship. Its potentially novel aspect is the tissue-pair co-abundance configuration that brings spliceosome-associated proteins together with RNA surveillance, decay, capping, export, translation-initiation, and RNA-binding components in colon and testis. The fact sheet does not provide a prior module catalogue, perturbation experiment, or literature comparison for this exact cross-tissue co-abundance pattern, so novelty of the integrated module remains provisional. A defensible conclusion is that the underlying biology is largely established, whereas the specific conserved colon-testis organization and its quantitative co-abundance behavior may be a new observation requiring replication and comparison with independent cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a coordinated RNA quality-control and maturation axis active in both colon and testis. In this model, transcription produces capped and intron-containing transcripts; RNMT supports cap formation, the SF3A1-DDX42-DDX46 spliceosomal group supports intron recognition and splicing, NUP107 supports nuclear transport, and UPF1, CNOT1, CAPRIN1, and XRN2 coordinate surveillance, decay, and turnover of transcripts that are exported or fail processing. SRRT and CCAR1 may organize interfaces between nuclear RNA processing and broader gene-expression regulation, while EIF3C and LARS1 couple transcript availability to translation. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway: co-abundance, annotation, STRING, and hu.MAP evidence establish compatibility with the model but do not establish directionality, physical interaction for every pair, or tissue-specific regulation.

### Novelty

The biological function of the central MCM2–MCM4–MCM6 subnetwork is not novel: it is a well-established component of the replication licensing and CMG-associated machinery, supported by STRING, hu.MAP, GO, KEGG, and gene descriptions. The AARS1–YARS1 relationship is also already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. A potentially novel aspect is the conservation of this particular co-abundance module across colon and testis, together with its specific peripheral combination of replication, RNA modification, translation, aminoacylation, and purine-biosynthesis genes. However, the supplied evidence does not establish that this tissue-pair module is a new biological mechanism. The absence of direct STRING or hu.MAP support for some peripheral edges cannot by itself demonstrate novelty. A defensible conclusion is that the module-level tissue-pair observation may be new, whereas its central molecular machinery is established. Replication in independent cohorts, adjustment for cell composition, and perturbation or interaction experiments would be needed to support a claim of a novel conserved mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module marks a shared replication-competent cellular state present in both colon and testis. The MCM2–MCM4–MCM6 core may represent active or poised replication licensing, while NSUN2, EIF4G1, AARS1, YARS1, and GMPS support the RNA modification, translation, aminoacylation, and nucleotide supply needed by cells undergoing proliferation or differentiation. The enrichment of spermatogenic and hematopoietic progenitor cell types suggests that cell-state composition could generate or amplify the conserved co-abundance pattern. A more mechanistic version of the hypothesis is that replication demand couples to RNA maturation, protein synthesis, and purine production, producing the observed peripheral correlations. This remains a hypothesis because the supplied data establish co-abundance and known complex membership, not causal regulation or direct physical coupling among all module members.
