# Conserved co-abundance modules: rectum and skin

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: rectum and skin  
**Genes (15)**: SH3BP1, CNOT12, ELP3, EML4, EPS15, LACTB, MCM2, MCM4, MCM6, MCM7, MSH2, RAI14, RFTN1, TMOD3, XPO4

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a nuclear DNA-replication and genome-maintenance module. The primary evidence is the coherent set of globally corrected terms spanning break-induced double-strand break repair, the MCM and CMG complexes, replication-initiation control, DNA-replication preinitiation, DNA helicase activity, mitotic and nuclear DNA replication, and the DNA-replication KEGG pathway (25 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000124–0.0427; see the table). MCM2, MCM4, MCM6, and MCM7 form the central replication-associated signal, while MSH2 extends it toward mismatch repair and DNA-damage surveillance through the single-stranded-DNA-binding and double-strand-break-repair terms (GO:MF single-stranded DNA binding; q_global = 0.000762), (GO:BP double-strand break repair; q_global = 0.00256), (GO:MF ATP-dependent activity, acting on DNA; q_global = 0.00582), (GO:MF catalytic activity, acting on DNA; q_global = 0.0239), and (GO:CC chromosome, telomeric region; q_global = 0.0289). The remaining genes do not provide a comparably strong common annotation, but their functions are compatible with cellular organization, trafficking, cytoskeletal regulation, transcriptional or translational regulation, and mitochondrial biology. Thus, the module most likely reflects coordinated abundance of replication-competent or proliferative cells rather than a single pathway involving every gene equally. The DNA-replication interpretation is supported by globally corrected evidence; the more specific interpretation involving replication-stress responses and G1-to-S control is supplementary, based on STRING annotations (4 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 0.00052–0.0013).

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and most proteins are intracellular, with the MCM proteins and MSH2 localized to the nucleoplasm. The module is also not strongly blood-cell specific according to HPA, which reports low immune-cell specificity for most genes and no enriched blood-cell category. Nevertheless, HPA shows frequent enhancement in erythrocyte progenitors, megakaryocyte progenitors, monocyte progenitors, neutrophil progenitors, and related hematopoietic populations. Bone marrow and lymphoid tissue are recurrent enriched tissues, and MCM2, MCM4, MCM6, and MCM7 show enhancement in hematopoietic progenitor populations. This suggests that part of the shared rectum–skin signal could reflect proliferating or progenitor-like cells, or shared cellular admixture, rather than a tissue-specific epithelial program. Protein detection in both rectum and skin for most of the central MCM genes supports their presence in both tissues, while RFTN1 is not detected by IHC in either tissue despite being the top network hub. The composition therefore favors a broadly expressed, proliferation-associated intracellular module with a possible contribution from shared progenitor or cycling cell populations.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new discovery: the MCM replication machinery, its participation in prereplication and CMG complexes, and the role of MSH2 in mismatch repair are established. The module therefore should not be claimed as a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. A potentially novel aspect is the observation that this particular combination of a canonical MCM core with MSH2 and peripheral genes is conserved in the rectum–skin tissue pair. However, conservation of co-abundance across these tissues is an analytical observation, not proof of a new physical interaction or a tissue-specific mechanism. The lack of STRING or hu.MAP support for several peripheral edges does not establish novelty, because absence from those resources can reflect incomplete coverage, context dependence, or indirect co-regulation. The most defensible conclusion is that the module is a new tissue-pair-level co-abundance pattern built around a well-known replication module, with its biological novelty requiring independent validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared rectum–skin module marks a conserved population of cycling, replication-competent, or progenitor-like cells present in both tissues. In this model, coordinated abundance of the MCM2–MCM4–MCM6–MCM7 helicase core reflects licensing and fork progression, MSH2 contributes mismatch repair and genome surveillance, and the peripheral genes capture cell-state features such as cytoskeletal remodeling, vesicle trafficking, nuclear transport, transcriptional regulation, or mitochondrial adaptation. The hematopoietic-progenitor enrichment in HPA raises the alternative possibility that shared progenitor-like or blood-associated cellular admixture contributes to the signal, although the low immune-cell specificity and absence of secreted proteins argue against interpreting it as a simple plasma or immune-cell signature. This hypothesis predicts that module abundance should track proliferation markers, replication-fork activity, or cycling-cell abundance in both tissues, and that the peripheral relationships may weaken when analyzed within purified cell types.

### Novelty

The molecular machinery itself is not a new biological discovery: the CCT chaperonin complex, HSPH1-mediated proteostasis, RUVBL2-associated nuclear functions, and ACLY–FASN lipid metabolism are established concepts, and the CCT core is already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel observation is the conserved co-abundance of this particular combination across rectum and skin. However, the supplied evidence does not establish whether this tissue-pair module has been reported previously, whether it is reproducible in independent cohorts, or whether co-abundance reflects cell composition, shared environmental stress, or coordinated regulation. Absence of a relationship from STRING or hu.MAP would not prove novelty. The defensible conclusion is that the module may represent a new tissue-pair-level co-abundance pattern built from largely known molecular components, but a claim of a new biological mechanism requires independent replication and direct testing.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared, broadly expressed proteostasis program in rectum and skin increases coordinated activity of the CCT chaperonin system, HSPH1-mediated handling of unstable proteins, and RUVBL2-linked nuclear remodeling. Through improved folding or stabilization of proteins involved in RNA processing and nuclear organization, this program could influence Cajal-body RNA localization and telomerase-associated telomere maintenance, as suggested by (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 5.2e-08–5.2e-08; see the table), (GO:BP regulation of telomerase RNA localization to Cajal body; q_global = 5.26e-06), (GO:BP positive regulation of telomerase RNA localization to Cajal body; q_global = 5.26e-06), (8 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.89e-05–4.48e-05; see the table), and (GO:BP regulation of telomere maintenance; q_global = 8.11e-05). The ACLY–FASN branch may represent a metabolic-support component that supplies biosynthetic capacity during stress or remodeling, whereas the extracellular-vesicle annotations may reflect release or detection of intracellular proteins. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal pathway; the co-abundance data do not show that the genes regulate one another or that telomerase activity is altered.

### Novelty

The molecular function itself is not a new discovery: the multisynthetase complex and its component relationships are already documented in STRING, hu.MAP, and the retrieved literature. The module's potentially novel aspect is its conserved co-abundance across rectum and skin and the way the established multisynthetase, RNA-processing, ribosome-biogenesis, and proteostasis genes appear together in this tissue-pair network. The supplied evidence does not establish that this cross-tissue module has been reported previously, because no direct comparison with published rectum-skin co-abundance modules or an independent cohort is provided. Absence of some module edges from STRING or hu.MAP cannot demonstrate novelty, since interaction databases are incomplete and the module measures abundance covariation rather than direct physical binding. The cautious conclusion is that this is a biologically credible, partially novel tissue-pair network observation built around a well-known molecular core, not a newly discovered protein complex or pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared rectum-skin module reflects a conserved cellular program that couples assembly and activity of the cytosolic multisynthetase complex to RNA handling and ribosome production. In this model, the AIMP-associated synthetase core supports accurate tRNA charging and translation, while DHX9, ADAR, DDX21, hnRNP proteins, NOP2, NOP56, MYBBP1A, RSL1D1, and ribosomal proteins coordinate RNA remodeling, processing, surveillance, and ribosome biogenesis. The co-abundance may therefore arise because cells in both tissues adjust translation capacity and RNA metabolism together during epithelial maintenance, differentiation, stress, or repair. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of direct regulation or of a tissue-specific signaling pathway. It should be tested with independent tissue cohorts, cell-composition adjustment, perturbation of AIMP-associated complex components, and measurements of tRNA charging, translation, RNA processing, and ribosome production.

The spliceosomal relationship between SNRNP200 and EFTUD2 is strongly supported by STRING and repeated hu.MAP complexes, and the module also contains well-characterized RNA-processing, trafficking, adhesion and signaling proteins. Conversely, the complete cross-tissue co-abundance arrangement is not explained by one annotated GO or KEGG pathway, and the limited STRING connectivity means that the observed co-abundance pattern is not simply a previously catalogued interaction complex. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship for individual pairs cannot establish novelty. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel systems-level association built partly from established molecular relationships, requiring independent replication and perturbation experiments before being described as a new mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in rectum and skin, a shared cellular state coordinates spliceosome activity, RNA export and decay, and intracellular membrane trafficking. The SNRNP200-EFTUD2 spliceosomal core may support processing of transcripts required for vesicle transport, adhesion and stress-responsive signaling, while CNOT1, GIGYF2 and USP10 may tune the stability, translation or ubiquitination of those transcripts and proteins. OCIAD2 may serve as a network-level bridge between RNA-related factors, nuclear transport components and endosomal or mitochondrial processes. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than an established causal explanation; the co-abundance data do not demonstrate physical binding, directionality or shared regulation.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new molecular pathway: focal adhesion, integrin signaling, caveolar organization, actomyosin contractility, and ILK-PARVA adhesion assemblies are already established by STRING, hu.MAP, and the gene annotations. That claim remains dataset-specific and should be validated in independent cohorts, by cell-composition adjustment, and experimentally. The absence of some genes from hu.MAP or the absence of particular edges from STRING does not establish novelty, because database coverage and interaction evidence are incomplete. Thus the appropriate conclusion is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built from largely established molecular machinery.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in rectum and skin, a shared population of contractile, vascular, stromal, or myoepithelial-like cells coordinates extracellular-matrix attachment with actomyosin force generation. ### Function

No GO/KEGG term passed global correction, and STRING functional enrichment was absent; therefore, the module should not be assigned a formally enriched pathway. Its gene-level annotations nevertheless suggest a coordinated cellular-maintenance program spanning endoplasmic-reticulum protein quality control, mitochondrial protein import and metabolite exchange, peroxisomal fatty-acid processing, and cytokine-responsive transcription. UGGT1 and HYOU1 support ER glycoprotein quality control, folding, stress response, and ER-to-Golgi trafficking. TIMM50 and SLC25A13 support mitochondrial import and mitochondrial metabolite transport, while HSD17B4 supports peroxisomal fatty-acid beta-oxidation. STAT1 supplies a signaling and transcriptional link to interferon, interleukin, growth-factor, antiviral, and defense responses. TMEM214 is annotated in ER, Golgi, and cytosolic compartments and is predicted to participate in apoptosis. Taken together, the most defensible interpretation is a broadly distributed organelle-proteostasis and metabolic-stress module with an immune or cytokine-responsive component, rather than a narrowly defined pathway.

**Cell composition.** This does not look like a plasma-protein or strongly secreted module: only a minority of its genes are classified as secreted, and UGGT1 and HYOU1 are intracellular or membrane-associated. HPA indicates broadly low tissue specificity for most genes, with liver enrichment for SLC25A13 and liver enhancement for HSD17B4. The single-cell annotations are heterogeneous, including reproductive cells, hepatocytes, alveolar cells, corticotrophs, erythrocyte progenitors, neutrophils, oligodendrocytes, plasma cells, and mesothelial or epicardial cells. Immune-cell specificity is low for most genes, with only HSD17B4 immune-cell enhancement. Protein detection is present for most genes in rectum and for a substantial subset in skin, but the absent skin detection of HYOU1 and absent rectum detection of STAT1 show that tissue detection is not uniform.

### Novelty

This is not established as a wholly new molecular pathway or complex. The HYOU1-UGGT1 relationship is already known in STRING, and the individual functions of the genes are well established, particularly for STAT1, HSD17B4, SLC25A13, TIMM50, UGGT1, and HYOU1.
