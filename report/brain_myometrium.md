# Conserved co-abundance modules: brain and myometrium

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: brain and myometrium  
**Genes (21)**: ORM1, ORM2, SERPINA1, SERPINF2, LRG1, SERPINA3, SERPINC1, APOA4, APOH, CP, CFI, HPX, IGHA1, IGHG1, IGHG2, IGHG3, KNG1, SELENBP1, GC, VTN, AZGP1

### Function

The module is best interpreted as an extracellular plasma-protein program involving acute inflammation, humoral immunity, complement regulation, coagulation, hemostasis, and wound healing. The primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for blood microparticles (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 7.58e-10), extracellular vesicles and extracellular space (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00052–0.0165; see the table), acute inflammatory and acute-phase responses (GO:BP acute inflammatory response; q_global = 0.00053) (GO:BP acute-phase response; q_global = 0.0024), defense and immunoglobulin-mediated immunity (6 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00398–0.0213; see the table), complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0104), regulation of coagulation and hemostasis (GO:BP regulation of blood coagulation; q_global = 0.0177) (GO:BP regulation of hemostasis; q_global = 0.0177) (GO:BP regulation of coagulation; q_global = 0.03), platelet alpha granules (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.00774) (GO:CC platelet alpha granule; q_global = 0.0179), and wound healing (GO:BP regulation of wound healing; q_global = 0.0474). These annotations describe the biochemical context of the proteins rather than proving that the proteins are produced by brain or myometrium cells. The module therefore appears to capture a coordinated circulating extracellular milieu, with protease inhibition, complement control, immune proteins, lipid-associated proteins, and vascular or matrix-associated factors.

**Cell composition.** The module is strongly dominated by secreted or plasma-associated proteins: the HPA summary identifies secreted genes throughout the module, and many members are liver-enriched or hepatocyte-enriched. The module also contains immunoglobulin genes associated with plasma cells and B-cell populations, as well as genes associated with neutrophils and other immune-cell categories. HPA further links some members to intestinal, lymphoid, salivary, pancreatic, and endometrial secretory contexts. Protein detection in brain and myometrium is limited for most genes, despite detection of selected members such as IGHA1, GC, and SERPINA3. This composition makes blood, plasma, vascular leakage, extracellular fluid, or shared infiltrating and secretory cell types a more plausible explanation for the cross-tissue co-abundance than a brain-myometrium-specific intracellular program. It also means that the shared module may reflect common sample exposure to circulating proteins or common cellular admixture. The absence of HPA protein detection in a tissue does not by itself exclude local biology.

### Novelty

The broad biological theme is not a new molecular function: circulating acute-phase, complement, coagulation, immunoglobulin, and plasma-protein relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The repeated overlap with known complexes and the extensive STRING support argue against claiming discovery of a previously unknown protein system. The potentially novel aspect is the conservation of this co-abundance pattern across brain and myometrium in the particular dataset and sampling context. That observation could be useful, but it requires validation in independent cohorts, careful control for blood contamination and cellular composition, and comparison with tissue-specific expression or deconvolution results. Absence of some genes from STRING or hu.MAP, such as several immunoglobulin members or AZGP1, does not prove novelty; it may instead reflect incomplete databases, limited evidence types, or the fact that co-abundance is not equivalent to a documented physical interaction.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects a circulating extracellular inflammatory-hemostatic milieu present in both tissue sample sets, rather than a conserved intracellular pathway of neural and uterine cells. Liver-derived acute-phase and protease-regulatory proteins may enter or remain associated with tissue-associated fluid, while immunoglobulin and immune-associated members may reflect plasma-cell or blood-cell contributions. The coordinated abundance of complement regulators, serpins, kininogen, vitronectin, and platelet or vesicle-associated proteins could represent a common response involving vascular permeability, extracellular protease balance, immune surveillance, and tissue repair. A testable prediction is that module abundance will track plasma exposure, blood or immune-cell signatures, inflammatory state, or sample vascularity more strongly than brain or myometrial cell-intrinsic markers.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: brain and myometrium  
**Genes (6)**: A2M, APOB, C1QB, CFH, FGA, ITIH1

### Function

The strongest functional interpretation is a circulating complement, coagulation, and extracellular blood-protein module. (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00909) is the only GO/KEGG term passing global correction and links A2M, C1QB, CFH, and FGA to complement and coagulation cascades. The supplementary STRING results support complement, opsonization, blood microparticles, secretion, and extracellular localization (9 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 0.00024–0.0104). A2M provides protease inhibition, cytokine transport, and negative regulation of complement; CFH regulates complement activation; C1QB contributes to classical complement and synapse pruning; and FGA contributes to fibrin formation, coagulation, platelet activation, and fibrinolysis. APOB and ITIH1 extend the module toward lipid transport and extracellular matrix or hyaluronan-associated biology. Thus, the module is more consistent with a shared systemic extracellular inflammatory and hemostatic signal than with a brain- or myometrium-specific intracellular pathway.

**Cell composition.** The module is overwhelmingly composed of secreted proteins found in blood or extracellular compartments, as indicated by HPA secretome annotations and the supplementary STRING enrichment for secreted proteins and blood microparticles (STRING Component Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 0.001), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 0.0011), (STRING Keyword Secreted; within-query FDR = 0.002), (STRING COMPARTMENTS Extracellular space; within-query FDR = 0.0104). C1QB is associated with monocyte and macrophage populations, whereas several other genes are not detected in immune cells. Protein detection is absent for the module in myometrium and is largely absent in the surveyed brain regions, apart from A2M detection in brain. These observations strongly raise the possibility that the shared co-abundance signal reflects circulating plasma proteins, vascular exposure, blood-derived material, or shared nonparenchymal cell contributions rather than coordinated expression by resident brain and myometrial cells. The HPA evidence does not by itself identify the source of the signal in the analyzed samples.

### Novelty

The broad biological interpretation is not new: complement, coagulation, plasma transport, and blood-microparticle proteins are established interacting and co-occurring systems, as shown by STRING and hu.MAP. The module should therefore not be presented as a newly discovered complement pathway. Its potentially new aspect is the reproducible conservation of this particular co-abundance pattern across brain and myometrium, especially the inclusion of FBLN1 and AFM alongside the plasma complement and coagulation proteins. That claim remains provisional because the fact sheet does not provide an external replication cohort, tissue-depletion control, quantitative comparison with other modules, or a literature search specifically testing this cross-tissue module. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone cannot establish novelty. The most defensible conclusion is that the module is a potentially informative cross-tissue plasma or extracellular signature, but not a demonstrated novel molecular complex or pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved signal arises mainly from a shared vascular or extracellular-blood compartment captured in both tissue groups. Liver-derived secreted proteins may enter tissue-associated fluid or blood microparticles, while FBLN1 may connect the circulating protein signal to extracellular matrix and tissue-interface biology. Within this context, F2 could provide coagulation activity, C1S and C9 could represent complement effector capacity, and SERPING1 could constrain complement activation. Albumin, transferrin, and afamin could stabilize or transport extracellular cargo. This model predicts that the module will weaken after rigorous removal of blood or vascular contamination and will be stronger in samples with greater vascular, extracellular-fluid, or tissue-interface content. It does not establish that these proteins act together locally in brain or myometrium.
