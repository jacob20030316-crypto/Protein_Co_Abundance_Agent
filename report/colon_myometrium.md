# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and myometrium

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and myometrium  
**Genes (9)**: SERPINA1, ALB, TGFBI, FGB, FGG, HPX, LUM, DCN, TF

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a secreted extracellular and plasma-protein module combining extracellular-matrix organization with circulating protein, heme-handling, iron-transport, protease-inhibitory, and coagulation-related biology. The primary evidence is the globally corrected extracellular-matrix structural constituent term (GO:MF extracellular matrix structural constituent; q_global = 0.0152), supported by DCN, FGB, FGG, LUM, and TGFBI, and the platelet alpha-granule lumen term (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.0349), supported by ALB, FGB, FGG, and SERPINA1. The network has a plasma-protein hub centered on ALB and HPX, a fibrinogen pair formed by FGB and FGG, and an extracellular-matrix branch involving LUM, DCN, and TGFBI. The STRING enrichments reinforce secretion, extracellular localization, heme scavenging, thrombosis-related biology, and plasma-protein associations, but they are supplementary rather than globally corrected enrichment evidence. No within-module GO/KEGG term passed its stated correction threshold, while the two terms above passed correction across all module-term tests. Thus, the module is best viewed as a coordinated extracellular and circulating-protein signature rather than evidence for a single intracellular pathway.

**Cell composition.** The module is overwhelmingly secreted: every member is annotated as secreted either to blood or to the extracellular matrix. HPA points strongly toward liver and hepatocytes, with additional signals for placenta, decidual stromal cells, hepatic stellate cells, and selected myeloid populations. ALB, HPX, FGB, FGG, TF, and SERPINA1 are liver- or hepatocyte-associated plasma proteins, and the platelet alpha-granule result further supports a blood-derived component. Most module proteins were not detected by HPA in colon or myometrium, whereas TGFBI was detected in colon and DCN in myometrium. This pattern makes passive plasma carryover, vascular content, or shared extracellular material a major alternative explanation for the cross-tissue co-abundance. It does not by itself establish blood-cell contamination: most genes were not detected in immune cells, although SERPINA1 and TGFBI have myeloid associations and DCN and LUM have stromal or extracellular-matrix associations. The shared module may therefore reflect a mixture of circulating proteins and a smaller tissue extracellular-matrix or stromal component.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. Most of its central plasma-protein relationships and repeated hu.MAP memberships are already documented, and the STRING evidence independently supports many of the same connections. A potentially novel aspect is the observation that this particular extracellular and plasma-protein pattern is conserved in the colon-myometrium tissue comparison, especially the linkage of a small extracellular-matrix branch to a larger circulating-protein branch. However, the provided evidence does not establish whether that tissue-pair co-abundance pattern has been reported previously, and absence from a database would not prove novelty. The most defensible conclusion is a potentially novel tissue-level co-abundance observation built largely from established protein biology.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects a common vascular or extracellular-fluid component present in both tissue sample types, with circulating liver-derived proteins forming the dominant abundance signal and local stromal extracellular-matrix proteins forming a smaller linked branch. In this model, ALB, HPX, TF, SERPINA1, FGB, and FGG track a shared plasma environment involving heme transport, iron handling, protease control, and coagulation-related material, while LUM, DCN, and TGFBI report extracellular-matrix remodeling or stromal context. A secondary possibility is that injury, inflammation, vascular permeability, or extracellular-vesicle release coordinates these signals. This is a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism; tissue localization, matched plasma measurements, cell-type deconvolution, and targeted validation would be needed to distinguish a true tissue program from blood or extracellular-fluid carryover.

### Novelty

The biological theme is not new: the STRING relationships, hu.MAP complexes, plasma secretion patterns and established functions of PLG, A2M, CFH, ITIH2, APOB and SERPINA4 all indicate substantial prior knowledge. The potentially new observation is the reproducible co-abundance of this particular gene set across colon and myometrium. However, the supplied evidence does not establish that this exact cross-tissue module has not been reported previously. Absence of a complete matching module from STRING or hu.MAP would not prove novelty, because those resources represent physical interactions and complexes rather than every co-abundance pattern. A defensible conclusion is that the module may be a new tissue-pair co-abundance observation, but its molecular components and overall plasma-protease/complement biology are well established.

**Hypothesis.** Mechanistic hypothesis: variation in the abundance of liver-derived circulating proteins, entering or remaining in tissue-associated plasma and extracellular fluid, produces the shared colon-myometrium co-abundance signal. In this model, PLG supplies fibrinolytic potential; A2M and SERPINA4 restrain proteases; CFH limits alternative-pathway complement activation; ITIH2 contributes extracellular-matrix and hyaluronan-associated stabilization; and APOB represents the linked lipoprotein and intestinal-hepatic component. Their coordinated abundance could reflect vascular permeability, local tissue injury, inflammation, remodeling or differences in blood content. This is a hypothesis rather than proof of local production or direct mechanistic cooperation in colon or myometrium, particularly because HPA protein detection is largely absent in those tissues.

### Function

This is a mixed module with two biologically coherent components. TAGLN, MYL9, and CNN1 support an actin–myosin smooth-muscle contractile program: TAGLN is an actin-binding calponin-family protein associated with smooth-muscle differentiation, MYL9 regulates myosin activity and muscle contraction, and CNN1 participates in actin organization and smooth-muscle contraction. This interpretation is supported by the co-abundance connectivity among these genes and by the supplementary STRING muscle and myosin-light-chain-kinase annotation (STRING NetworkNeighborAL Muscle protein, and Myosin light chain kinase activity; within-query FDR = 0.0079). HBA1 and HBB form the hemoglobin component and are the only genes in the globally significant GO term for nitric oxide transport (GO:BP nitric oxide transport; q_global = 0.0177). Thus, the strongest functional interpretation is a smooth-muscle contractile program accompanied by an erythrocyte/hemoglobin and nitric-oxide-transport signal. The nitric-oxide annotation is primary evidence; the STRING terms are supplementary.

**Cell composition.** The module is not a secreted-protein module, since HPA reports no secreted genes. Its composition nevertheless suggests mixed cellular contributions rather than a uniform tissue-wide housekeeping program. TAGLN, MYL9, and CNN1 show enrichment in blood-vessel, intestine, or smooth-muscle contexts and in smooth-muscle or related myoid cell types. HBA1 and HBB are erythrocyte- and bone-marrow-associated, and both are undetected by HPA immunohistochemistry in colon and myometrium. The blood-cell associations reported for several module genes, together with the hemoglobin pair, make blood or erythrocyte contribution a plausible explanation for part of the shared signal. The contractile component may instead reflect vascular, visceral, or other smooth-muscle cells shared by the sampled tissues. The module therefore should not be interpreted as evidence that every gene is produced by the same cell type.

### Novelty

The individual biology is not new: smooth-muscle associations of TAGLN, MYL9, and CNN1, the hemoglobin relationship between HBA1 and HBB, and their disease or functional annotations are established. The web-search evidence also reports MYL9 and CNN1 as co-expression or prognostic genes in colorectal cancer, showing that their relevance to colon biology has been described previously. What may be new is the specific conserved co-abundance configuration linking a contractile smooth-muscle group with a hemoglobin/nitric-oxide group across colon and myometrium. That claim remains a discovery-level interpretation rather than a demonstrated novel mechanism. Partial absence from STRING or hu.MAP cannot prove novelty, because those resources capture selected physical, functional, or complex relationships rather than every tissue-specific co-abundance relationship. Independent validation in matched single-cell, spatial, and blood-depletion datasets would be needed to establish whether the cross-tissue module is genuinely novel or reflects shared vascular cells or blood carryover.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects coordinated variation in vascular or visceral smooth-muscle content together with erythrocyte-associated hemoglobin signal. In this model, TAGLN, MYL9, and CNN1 report the contractile state or abundance of smooth-muscle and perivascular cells, whereas HBA1 and HBB report erythrocyte contribution and provide the nitric-oxide-transport signal captured by (GO:BP nitric oxide transport; q_global = 0.0177). A possible biological coupling is that local blood content and smooth-muscle contractile or vascular state vary together in both tissues, producing the observed cross-tissue co-abundance pattern. This is a hypothesis, not proof of direct regulation between the contractile and hemoglobin genes.
