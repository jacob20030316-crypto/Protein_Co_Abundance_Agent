# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and lung

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and lung  
**Genes (19)**: SERPINA1, ALB, APOA2, LPA, APOC3, CLIC5, HPX, HSPB6, IGHM, IGKC, ITIH4, LUM, MYH11, DCN, RBP4, S100A8, S100A9, SPTA1, TF

### Function

The strongest interpretation is an extracellular, circulating protein and lipoprotein-associated module with an inflammatory component. The primary evidence is the enrichment of extracellular region (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.000336), extracellular space (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.000927), blood microparticle (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 0.00405), and extracellular vesicle-related terms (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00953–0.0104; see the table). The module also contains a coherent lipid-transport and lipid-regulatory signal through APOA2, APOC3, LPA, and ALB, including regulation of cholesterol import and negative regulation of lipase activity (GO:BP negative regulation of lipase activity; q_global = 0.0397) (GO:BP regulation of cholesterol import; q_global = 0.0397) (GO:BP negative regulation of cholesterol import; q_global = 0.0397). S100A8 and S100A9 provide an innate-inflammatory axis: their pair is associated with autocrine signaling, Toll-like receptor four binding, arachidonate-related binding, neutrophil aggregation, and the calprotectin complex (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.0397–0.0397; see the table) and (GO:BP neutrophil aggregation; q_global = 0.0397) (GO:CC calprotectin complex; q_global = 0.0397) (GO:MF fatty acid binding; q_global = 0.0468). Thus, the module is better described as a blood-derived extracellular proteome program combining hepatic plasma proteins, lipoprotein biology, extracellular-matrix proteins, and myeloid inflammatory proteins than as a single intracellular pathway.

**Cell composition.** The module is dominated by secreted or extracellular proteins. HPA classifies most members as secreted, with many enriched in liver and hepatocytes, including ALB, APOA2, APOC3, HPX, ITIH4, RBP4, SERPINA1, and TF. This composition strongly suggests that the shared colon–lung signal may reflect circulating plasma exposure, vascular content, or sample-associated extracellular material rather than coordinated expression by colon and lung parenchymal cells. The HPA protein data show little direct protein detection in either colon or lung for most members, which reinforces caution about interpreting the module as a locally produced tissue program. There are also cell-type signals compatible with blood or infiltrating cells: S100A8 and S100A9 are associated with classical monocytes and neutrophils, IGHM and IGKC with B-cell or plasma-cell populations, and SPTA1 with erythrocyte progenitors. The presence of MYH11, DCN, LUM, CLIC5, and HSPB6 indicates additional vascular smooth-muscle, stromal, extracellular-matrix, or structural components. Overall, the module could reflect plasma carryover together with shared vascular, stromal, and immune cell content.

### Novelty

This module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular complex or pathway. Many central relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the hepatic plasma-protein, lipoprotein, extracellular-matrix, and S100A8–S100A9 components are established biology. A potentially useful finding is the conserved co-abundance organization across colon and lung, especially the way plasma proteins, extracellular-matrix markers, vascular or smooth-muscle markers, and myeloid inflammatory proteins occur together. That cross-tissue organization may be novel in this particular dataset, but the fact sheet does not provide an external tissue-pair comparison, replication cohort, perturbation experiment, or independent validation. Absence of some genes from STRING or hu.MAP, such as HSPB6, CLIC5, MYH11, DCN, or LUM in particular resources, is not evidence of novelty. The appropriate conclusion is that the module is a biologically coherent conserved co-abundance pattern with a potentially novel sample-level organization, not a newly discovered protein complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared colon–lung module reflects a circulating extracellular-protein state that is assembled from hepatic secretion and blood or vascular admixture, with inflammatory myeloid activity superimposed. In this model, hepatic proteins and lipoprotein components provide the dominant plasma background; extracellular-matrix and smooth-muscle proteins report vascular or stromal material; and S100A8–S100A9 reports neutrophil or monocyte-associated inflammation. The module may therefore arise when systemic lipid transport, acute-phase or tissue-injury responses, and vascular or immune-cell content covary across samples. This is a hypothesis because co-abundance alone cannot establish secretion source, physical binding, causal regulation, or directionality.

### Function

This module most strongly represents a circulating extracellular innate-defense and protease-regulatory program. The primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for blood microparticle (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 2.55e-12), complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 6.54e-06), complement activation (GO:BP complement activation; q_global = 0.00225), alternative-pathway complement activation (GO:BP complement activation, alternative pathway; q_global = 0.00348), regulation of complement activation (GO:BP regulation of complement activation; q_global = 0.0046), humoral immune response (GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.0217), and several peptidase-regulator terms (GO:MF endopeptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0164), (GO:MF peptidase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0164), (GO:MF endopeptidase regulator activity; q_global = 0.024), (GO:MF peptidase regulator activity; q_global = 0.0399). The extracellular vesicle, secretory-granule, and extracellular-space terms (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00156–0.00168; see the table), (GO:CC platelet alpha granule lumen; q_global = 0.00378), (GO:CC extracellular space; q_global = 0.00462), (GO:CC platelet alpha granule; q_global = 0.00863), (GO:CC extracellular region; q_global = 0.00982), and (GO:CC vesicle; q_global = 0.031) support a secreted or vesicle-associated context. The Staphylococcus aureus infection (KEGG Staphylococcus aureus infection - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0028) and neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (KEGG Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00597) terms are pathway annotations and should not be interpreted as evidence that the module specifically represents either disease or neuronal signaling. Structurally, C3, CFB, CFH, and CP occupy the most connected portion of the co-abundance network, with links extending to PLG, SERPING1, GC, and KNG1. Overall, the established interpretation is coordinated plasma complement regulation coupled to coagulation, kinin, fibrinolytic, extracellular-matrix, and protease-control biology.

**Cell composition.** The module is overwhelmingly composed of secreted proteins found in blood, rather than proteins characteristic of resident colon or lung cells. HPA identifies every module gene as secreted to blood and shows liver enrichment across the module, with hepatocytes especially prominent among the enriched cell types. Most genes are not detected in immune cells, although C3, CFH, and SERPING1 show immune-cell enhancement or enrichment in selected populations. Protein detection is absent in colon and lung for nearly all genes, with GC the notable exception. Thus, the shared colon-lung module is more plausibly driven by circulating plasma content, blood microparticles, or a shared vascular or sample-composition signal than by a tissue-specific colon-lung transcriptional program. The HPA result does not support interpreting this module as primarily blood-cell derived, but it does strongly support a liver-produced plasma-protein origin.

### Novelty

The biological pathway represented here is not a new discovery. Absence of a particular edge from STRING or hu.MAP would not prove novelty, and the extensive known-complex overlap argues against claiming a new molecular complex or pathway.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular interaction network. Its dominant collagen, basement-membrane, proteoglycan, plasma-protein, and hemoglobin relationships are already represented by STRING and hu.MAP, and the enriched biology is well established. A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance behavior of this particular combination across colon and lung, especially the joint occurrence of stromal ECM, circulating proteins, and erythrocyte-associated genes. That claim remains provisional because the fact sheet does not compare this module with published cross-tissue co-abundance modules, independent cohorts, cell-composition estimates, or perturbation data. Absence of a hu.MAP or STRING entry for individual genes such as BGN, HSPG2, VWA1, or FBN1 does not establish novelty; it may reflect database coverage, interaction type, or the distinction between co-abundance and direct molecular interaction. The most defensible conclusion is that the biology is largely known, while the cross-tissue co-abundance configuration may be a useful observation requiring external replication.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module reflects a shared stromal vascular niche in colon and lung in which fibroblast- and pericyte-associated cells produce and organize collagen-I, collagen-VI, collagen-IV, fibrillin, biglycan, perlecan, nidogen, and vitronectin. This matrix could provide structural support and regulate cell adhesion, integrin signaling, growth-factor presentation, vascular integrity, and tissue remodeling. Secreted plasma proteins may bind or traffic with matrix and extracellular vesicles, while hemoglobin genes may represent blood exposure or erythrocyte carryover rather than a core stromal mechanism. A testable prediction is that the ECM submodule will track fibroblast or pericyte abundance and matrix remodeling across samples, whereas the APOA1, A2M, CLU, VTN, HBA1, HBB, and HBD signal will track vascular content, plasma leakage, or blood contamination. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal pathway.

### Novelty

The biological theme is not a new molecular function: transcription, RNA processing, RNA binding, RNA export, splicing, RNA modification, and ribosome-related nuclear functions are established processes for several members. The direct STRING links and the hu.MAP association also indicate that portions of the module are already known. However, the conserved co-abundance organization across colon and lung may still be a useful systems-level observation, particularly because the module joins transcriptional machinery, RNA-processing factors, RNA-binding proteins, and regulatory proteins into one cross-tissue pattern. It should be described as a candidate conserved module or integrative finding rather than a newly discovered complex or pathway. The absence of many STRING or hu.MAP links does not establish novelty; it may reflect incomplete interaction maps, context dependence, indirect co-regulation, or co-abundance relationships that are not physical interactions.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved nuclear gene-expression program that coordinates transcription with maturation, modification, export, and surveillance of RNA in colon and lung cells. POLR2A may provide the transcriptional axis; SCAF8 and INTS7 may couple polymerase activity to transcription termination and small nuclear RNA processing; THOC5 and QKI may influence RNA export, splicing, localization, and stability; and ZC3H13 may connect the program to RNA methylation and nuclear RNA handling. MCM3AP, BAZ2A, USP36, RBBP6, RBM19, RBMXL1, and ZC3H7B could represent chromatin, nucleolar, ubiquitin-related, or post-transcriptional regulatory inputs. This model predicts coordinated regulation of RNA output and processing rather than a single direct protein complex. It remains a hypothesis because co-abundance, enrichment, and partial interaction overlap do not demonstrate physical assembly, causal regulation, or cell-type-specific mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module reflects variation in the abundance or activation state of circulating or tissue-infiltrating neutrophils across colon and lung samples. ### Novelty

The individual biological relationships are not new: the CCT subunits belong to a well-established chaperonin complex, the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases have canonical translation functions, and the GMPS–IMPDH2 relationship is already represented in STRING. Absence of a gene from STRING or hu.MAP cannot by itself establish novelty. The potentially novel observation is the conserved co-abundance pattern across colon and lung that combines a CCT chaperonin core with selected aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, translation initiation, RNA modification and purine biosynthesis genes. The fact sheet does not provide a comparison against previously published tissue co-abundance modules, so it cannot justify calling the module a definitively new biological discovery. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue network organization of largely known biology, requiring replication in independent cohorts and comparison with existing colon, lung and pan-tissue co-abundance modules.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a shared epithelial or proliferative cell-state program in which CCT/TRiC maintains folding of newly synthesized proteins, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and NSUN2 support accurate and properly modified translation-related RNA metabolism, EIF4G1 promotes cap-dependent translation, and GMPS with IMPDH2 supplies purine nucleotides for RNA and DNA synthesis. ### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered molecular machine. Its principal components and several subnetworks are already well established through STRING and hu.MAP, particularly the MCM replicative helicase, MutSalpha mismatch-repair complex, nuclear-pore components, and RNA-processing associations. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance arrangement across colon and lung, linking these established nuclear subsystems in one tissue-pair module. That arrangement may reflect coordinated regulation, shared proliferation state, DNA damage burden, or cell-composition effects, but the supplied evidence does not demonstrate a new physical complex or a new gene function. Absence of a relationship from STRING or hu.MAP is not evidence of novelty, especially for the weaker bridges. A genuine novelty claim would require replication in independent cohorts, cell-type-resolved analysis, and testing whether the cross-subnetwork correlations persist after controlling for proliferation and tissue composition.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a coordinated nuclear genome-maintenance state in proliferating or stressed cells shared by colon and lung. In this model, the MCM helicase core licenses and supports replication, MSH2-MSH6 surveils replication-associated mismatches, RUVBL2 and SMARCA4 help coordinate helicase, chromatin, and repair activities, and SMC4 supports chromosome organization. DDX42, DDX46, SRRT, TCERG1, and XRN2 may couple DNA-replication or chromatin states to RNA processing and transcriptional output, while the nucleoporin group may reflect the nuclear transport environment required by these activities. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of direct physical cooperation among every module member. An alternative explanation is that the module is generated by shared abundance across proliferating progenitor-like or other cell populations in the samples.

### Novelty

The individual functions of these genes and the ERCC3–MED23 relationship are established, so the module should not be described as a discovery of a new molecular pathway. The conserved co-abundance combination across colon and lung may represent a new systems-level observation, particularly because the remaining relationships are not represented by the reported STRING or hu.MAP evidence. However, absence from those resources does not prove novelty: the pattern could arise from indirect regulation, shared transcriptional control, tissue sampling effects, or incomplete database coverage. The most defensible conclusion is that the module is a potentially novel co-abundance configuration built from largely known cellular processes.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: across colon and lung samples, variation in a general nuclear gene-expression state coordinates transcriptional activation, transcription-coupled DNA repair, messenger RNA processing and export, and surveillance of faulty transcripts. MED23 and ERCC3 may form the most biologically grounded bridge between transcriptional activity and genome maintenance, while THOC2, RBM12B, and UPF2 may track downstream RNA maturation and quality-control demands. This is a mechanistic hypothesis generated from annotations and co-abundance, not a demonstrated physical pathway; it requires validation with independent cohorts, perturbation, and measurements of transcription, DNA repair, RNA export, splicing, and nonsense-mediated decay.
