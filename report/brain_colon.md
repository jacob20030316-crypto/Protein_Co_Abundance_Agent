### Function

The module does not support a single statistically established pathway-level function: no GO/KEGG term passed global correction, and STRING functional enrichment was absent. Its structure instead suggests a heterogeneous intracellular cell-biology program assembled around membrane and vesicle biology, cytoskeletal or junctional regulation, calcium and lipid handling, proteostasis, autophagy, and cellular stress control. LZTS3 is the principal network hub and connects genes associated with vesicles, dendritic-spine biology, calcium transport, phosphoinositide signaling, actin regulation, detoxification, and protein trafficking. CPNE8 forms a second prominent connection point linking membrane-associated calcium-responsive biology with autophagy, lipid handling, RNA surveillance, and vesicular enzymes. These convergent annotations support a cautious interpretation as a conserved cellular maintenance and trafficking module rather than a narrowly defined pathway. The absence of a globally significant GO or KEGG term does not invalidate the module; it indicates that the genes do not form a sufficiently coherent annotated pathway under the stated correction procedure.

**Cell composition.** The HPA profile argues against a module composed mainly of plasma proteins or secreted proteins: only PCSK1 is annotated as secreted, while the other genes are intracellular or membrane-associated. Most genes have low tissue specificity and protein detection is broad in both brain and colon, consistent with a broadly expressed cellular program rather than a tissue-exclusive signature. Nevertheless, cell-type effects are plausible. Several genes show enhanced expression in neutrophils or neutrophil progenitors, and other genes are associated with smooth muscle, endothelial, stromal, neuronal, or epithelial cell types. Immune-cell specificity is generally low, although a minority of genes show immune-cell enhancement or group enrichment. The shared signal could therefore reflect conserved biology in multiple resident or admixed cell types, with possible contributions from blood or stromal cells, rather than a uniform brain-colon cell population. TAGLN is particularly compatible with smooth-muscle or stromal content and is detected in colon but not brain by the supplied HPA immunohistochemistry summary. RBP1 and GSTM3 also show tissue-restricted detection patterns, reinforcing the possibility that part of the module reflects cell composition.

### Novelty

The module may represent a previously unrecognized cross-tissue co-abundance association, but the supplied evidence is not sufficient to call it a new biological mechanism. Its novelty is supported by the limited overlap with known STRING interactions and the fact that the hu.MAP matches are only partial. However, absence from STRING or hu.MAP cannot establish novelty because those resources are incomplete, biased toward well-studied proteins, and focused on physical or curated relationships rather than co-abundance. Several genes and functions in the module are already well established, including autophagy through ATG4B and ATG5, vesicle trafficking through RABEP1 and SORT1, calcium handling through ATP2B4 and CPNE8, redox biology through GSTM3 and GGT7, and smooth-muscle biology through TAGLN. The defensible conclusion is that the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern is potentially novel, whereas most individual molecular functions are not.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a conserved cellular adaptation program that coordinates membrane and vesicle trafficking with calcium and lipid sensing, cytoskeletal remodeling, autophagic quality control, RNA or protein quality control, and redox protection. In brain, this could operate in neuronal or glial membrane-trafficking and synaptic maintenance contexts; in colon, it could operate in epithelial, smooth-muscle, stromal, endothelial, or immune-associated contexts. LZTS3 and CPNE8 may organize or report the shared trafficking and membrane state, while ATG4B and ATG5 provide autophagic quality control, GGT7 and GSTM3 provide redox-related handling, and TAGLN marks a contractile or stromal contribution. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated pathway: the absence of globally significant GO/KEGG enrichment, sparse known interaction support, and mixed HPA cell-type patterns require validation by cell-resolved expression, perturbation, and physical interaction experiments.

### Novelty

The molecular functions represented here are not new: spliceosome, chromatin-remodeling, transcriptional, rRNA-processing, ubiquitin-related, and kinetochore roles are already supported by annotations, STRING, hu.MAP, and the literature. The potentially novel result is the conserved co-abundance organization of these functions in a brain–colon module. The fact sheet does not establish that this exact cross-tissue module has been reported previously, and absence of an edge from STRING or hu.MAP cannot by itself prove novelty because those resources are incomplete and are designed primarily around physical interactions or complexes. A cautious conclusion is that the module may represent a new systems-level co-abundance configuration, but novelty requires comparison with published tissue co-expression studies, independent cohorts, and replication under alternative normalization and cell-composition controls.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared nuclear maintenance state that coordinates transcriptional output with RNA maturation, chromatin accessibility, genome surveillance, and cell-cycle fidelity in cells present in both brain and colon. In this model, chromatin regulators and transcriptional machinery establish or maintain transcriptional programs; spliceosomal and RNA-processing proteins mature the resulting transcripts; ubiquitin or SUMO-related factors and DNA-repair components manage transcription-associated damage; and kinetochore or spindle components couple the state to proliferative cell populations. The coordinated abundance could therefore arise from a common demand for genome and transcriptome maintenance, rather than from direct physical interaction among all module members. This remains a hypothesis because the co-abundance data do not demonstrate causal regulation, physical assembly, or directionality.

### Function

The module does not have any GO or KEGG term passing global correction, so no pathway-level enrichment can be claimed. Its structure instead suggests a heterogeneous cellular homeostasis module, with a central co-abundance hub, membrane and vesicle biology, endoplasmic-reticulum lipid and protein quality control, intracellular trafficking, RNA processing, mitochondrial metabolism, and cytoskeletal or signaling functions. The strongest functional anchors are the established ERLIN1–ERLIN2 relationship, nuclear-pore components NUP160–NUP88, and the hu.MAP complexes containing ERLIN1, ERLIN2, RMDN3, PREPL, AARS2, ADAR, TRNT1, and other partners. These observations support a broad cell-maintenance and proteostasis interpretation rather than a single sharply defined biological pathway. The absence of globally significant GO or KEGG terms does not invalidate the module; it indicates that its genes are functionally diverse or that the signal is driven partly by shared cellular composition.

**Cell composition.** The module is not predominantly composed of plasma or secreted proteins: only one gene is annotated as secreted, and most genes are intracellular or membrane-associated. It is also not strongly blood-cell specific overall. HPA indicates predominantly low tissue specificity, with many genes showing cell-type enhancement across oligodendrocytes, excitatory and inhibitory neurons, astrocytes, microglia, and some neutrophil-related populations. Brain is a recurring tissue context, while liver and skeletal muscle also appear among enhanced tissues. Protein detection is broadly compatible with both brain and colon. Thus, the shared signal is more consistent with broadly expressed cellular machinery plus contributions from shared or related cell types than with a simple plasma-protein or blood-contamination module. Nevertheless, the presence of immune-cell-enhanced genes means that varying immune or glial composition could contribute to part of the co-abundance pattern.

### Novelty

This is plausibly a new tissue-pair co-abundance observation, but it cannot be called a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. The module contains known relationships, especially ERLIN1–ERLIN2 and NUP160–NUP88, and several genes participate in established hu.MAP complexes. At the same time, the overall cross-tissue grouping is weakly supported by known interaction resources, lacks globally significant GO or KEGG enrichment, and combines genes from several cellular systems. Therefore, its novelty lies primarily in the reproducible brain–colon co-abundance organization and its particular combination of known components, not in proving previously unknown physical interactions. Absence of STRING or hu.MAP support for many edges is evidence of limited prior annotation, not proof that those relationships are novel or physical.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared cellular stress-adaptation and trafficking state present in selected brain and colon cell populations. In this model, the GPRC5B-centered co-abundance pattern links membrane signaling and vesicle biology with ER lipid-raft and ER-associated degradation machinery represented by ERLIN1 and ERLIN2, while nuclear transport, RNA processing, mitochondrial translation, lipid metabolism, and cytoskeletal functions provide parallel maintenance responses. Differences in neuronal, glial, epithelial, and immune-cell abundance could generate the observed cross-tissue conservation. This hypothesis should be tested with cell-resolved data, perturbation of ERLIN1 or ERLIN2, and independent validation of the module across cohorts rather than inferred as a direct interaction network.

### Novelty

This module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex. Several components and relationships are already established through STRING and hu.MAP, particularly the NDUFA9–NDUFA10 respiratory pair, the ROGDI–WDR7 relationship, V-type ATPase-associated components, endocytic machinery, and receptor phosphatase complexes. Nevertheless, the conserved co-abundance of this particular combination across brain and colon appears potentially novel as a systems-level observation, because most module members lack direct STRING or hu.MAP links to one another and the module combines phosphatase, phosphoinositide, vesicle, calcium, cytoskeletal, mitochondrial, and vacuolar features. Absence from interaction resources is not evidence of novelty by itself. A defensible conclusion is that the module may represent a new cross-tissue co-abundance signature built from partly known biological components, requiring replication in independent cohorts, cell-composition adjustment, and perturbational or spatial validation before claiming a new mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved membrane-signaling and trafficking state in which receptor-type phosphatases and myotubularin-family phosphatases tune tyrosine and phosphoinositide phosphorylation, while SYNJ1 and endosomal machinery remodel membrane identity and vesicle cycling. V-type ATPase-associated proteins may support acidification of vesicular compartments, and calcium transport, actin or spectrin scaffolding, and mitochondrial energy production may maintain the associated cellular architecture and energetic demand. In brain, this could support neuronal polarity, synaptic vesicle handling, and axonal or dendritic maintenance; in colon, an analogous program could operate in specialized epithelial, enteroendocrine, neural, or stromal cells. The shared abundance pattern may therefore reflect a conserved cell-biological program rather than identical tissue-specific physiology. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway.

### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a newly discovered ribosome-biogenesis pathway. Its central function is already well established by globally corrected GO and KEGG evidence (13 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.91e-15–1.31e-06; see the table), the extensive STRING relationships (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 5.66e-25–4.52e-14), and multiple hu.MAP complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_01743.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11931.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14264.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_08896.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14364.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_07467.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_02116.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10039.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_08647.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09803.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11077.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_12350.1). What may be novel is the tissue-pair observation: coordinated abundance of this nucleolar program is conserved between brain and colon, and the exact module boundary and co-abundance organization may not have been reported previously. However, absence of an identical module from STRING or hu.MAP would not establish novelty, because those resources catalogue molecular interactions and complexes rather than every tissue-level co-abundance pattern. A credible novelty claim would require comparison with published brain-colon co-expression or proteomic modules, independent cohorts, and tissue or cell-type controlled analyses. The current evidence supports a potentially novel cross-tissue organization of known biology, not a novel molecular function.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared brain-colon module represents a conserved nucleolar production-and-quality-control program that adjusts small-subunit ribosome output to cellular biosynthetic demand. In this model, UTP4, NOL11, HEATR1, WDR43, and associated processome factors organize early preribosome assembly; DDX21 and related RNA helicases remodel rRNA-containing intermediates; PDCD11, NOL6, UTP14A, and connected factors support cleavage, maturation, and trafficking of small-subunit precursors. The co-abundance of these components in both tissues may therefore reflect a shared requirement for maintaining ribosome production in metabolically active or renewing cells. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal model and should be tested by perturbing representative processome genes and measuring pre-rRNA processing, mature ribosome output, and cell-state responses in relevant brain and colon cell types.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a nuclear DNA-replication and genome-maintenance module. The primary evidence is the strong, globally corrected enrichment for the MCM complex and CMG complex (GO:CC MCM complex; q_global = 1.05e-09), DNA replication initiation and regulation (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 5.9e-08–0.000456; see the table), DNA helicase and ATP-dependent DNA activity (13 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 3.45e-08–7.14e-06; see the table), homologous recombination and double-strand-break repair (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.39e-08–9.99e-05; see the table), and cell-cycle DNA replication (7 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000146–0.0361; see the table). The module therefore reflects coordinated licensing or operation of replication machinery together with DNA repair and chromatin-associated genome maintenance. The broad nucleic-acid and nucleotide-binding terms (27 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 3.28e-07–0.0319; see the table) are consistent with this interpretation but are less specific. The association is biologically coherent rather than dependent on a single annotation: the MCM genes form the structural core, while CHD4, MSH2, DDX46, and NSUN2 extend the module toward chromatin remodeling, mismatch repair, RNA helicase or processing, and RNA modification. The stress-response annotation (GO:BP cellular response to stress; q_global = 0.0424) is compatible with genome surveillance but does not by itself define the module.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module. HPA places the proteins predominantly in the nucleoplasm or nuclear speckles, and only NSUN2 is classified as intracellular and membrane-associated in the secretome field. The module has low immune-cell specificity across its genes, so it does not look like a conventional blood-cell marker module. However, HPA shows repeated enhancement in bone marrow, lymphoid tissue, and progenitor populations, especially erythrocyte, megakaryocyte, and monocyte progenitors. This pattern is compatible with cell proliferation and progenitor-state composition, because DNA-replication machinery is expected to be abundant in dividing cells. It should therefore not be assumed that the brain-colon conservation represents a tissue-specific neuronal or epithelial mechanism; shared proliferative or progenitor-like cell states, technical covariation, or cell-composition effects remain plausible contributors. Protein detection in both tissues supports biological detectability, while the lack of brain detection for some core MCM proteins cautions against interpreting the module as uniformly active in every brain cell.

### Novelty

The biological functions and the central MCM assembly are not new: they are supported by established annotations, high-confidence STRING relationships, and multiple hu.MAP complexes. The cross-tissue co-abundance observation may still be useful, particularly because it links a replication and genome-maintenance program across brain and colon, but the fact sheet alone cannot establish that this is a previously undescribed molecular module. Absence of DDX46, NSUN2, and CHD4 from the listed STRING or hu.MAP relationships does not prove novelty; it may reflect incomplete interaction coverage or indirect co-regulation. The most defensible conclusion is that the module is a new network-level observation only provisionally, while its core molecular machinery is well established. Novelty would require comparison with prior brain-colon co-abundance modules, replication in independent cohorts, adjustment for cell composition and proliferation, and experimental testing of the peripheral connections.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module represents a shared nuclear genome-maintenance state in proliferating or replication-competent cells present in both tissues. The MCM2-MCM4-MCM5-MCM6-MCM7 core may coordinate replication-fork licensing or progression, with MSH2 contributing mismatch surveillance and repair, CHD4 contributing chromatin remodeling around damaged or replicated DNA, and DDX46 or NSUN2 coupling nuclear RNA processing or RNA modification to the replication state. In this model, the observed brain-colon conservation reflects a common cellular program rather than a tissue-specific pathway. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of direct protein interaction or causal regulation, and it should be tested by cell-type-resolved expression analysis, proliferation-state adjustment, perturbation of the MCM core, and measurement of DNA-repair, chromatin, and RNA-processing responses.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a cytosolic proteostasis and protein-folding module centered on the chaperonin-containing TCP-complex. The strongest primary evidence is the repeated global enrichment of CCT3, CCT5, CCT7, and CCT8 in the chaperonin-containing T-complex (GO:CC chaperonin-containing T-complex; q_global = 5.28e-08), ATP-dependent protein-folding chaperone activity (GO:MF ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone; q_global = 9.21e-06), protein-folding chaperone complex (GO:CC protein folding chaperone complex; q_global = 3.57e-05), unfolded-protein binding (GO:MF unfolded protein binding; q_global = 0.000355), and protein folding (GO:BP protein folding; q_global = 0.00431). The same subunits also account for globally corrected terms involving protein stabilization and nuclear or Cajal-body localization (15 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.9e-07–0.0403; see the table). The telomere-maintenance and telomerase-RNA-localization annotations (22 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 5.28e-08–0.00165; see the table) support a possible connection between CCT-dependent folding and telomere-related protein or RNA handling, but they should not be interpreted as proof that the whole module is a dedicated telomere module. The fertility and cell-recognition terms (8 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.46e-06–0.00437; see the table) are likely consequences of the same CCT-subunit annotation overlap and do not, by themselves, establish a reproductive function. EIF3C and DARS1 extend the module toward translation initiation and aminoacyl-tRNA charging, respectively, but they are not responsible for the principal enrichment signal.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein, secreted-protein, or blood-cell-enriched module. HPA reports no secreted genes, low immune-cell specificity for every member, and detection of all members in both brain and colon. All genes have low tissue specificity, consistent with broadly expressed cellular machinery rather than a tissue-restricted program. The dominant composition is therefore housekeeping-like intracellular protein-handling machinery, with a smaller translation-related component. The shared brain-colon signal is more plausibly a conserved cellular program, or a shared abundance of common cell types, than an artifact caused by circulating proteins or blood contamination. Cell-type-enhanced HPA annotations for several members, including neural and germ-cell contexts, indicate that expression can vary among cell types, but do not establish that those cell types drive the cross-tissue module.

### Novelty

The molecular core is not a new biological complex: its membership is strongly supported by established STRING relationships (11 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 7.76e-08–2.9e-07) and repeated hu.MAP complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_07938.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_06559.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09191.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10656.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14584.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_01951.1). The conserved brain-colon co-abundance pattern could still be a useful systems-level observation, especially because it links a recognized intracellular chaperone core with EIF3C and DARS1 in these tissues. However, the fact sheet does not demonstrate that this tissue-pair co-abundance pattern is absent from prior transcriptomic or proteomic studies, nor does absence from a particular STRING or hu.MAP edge prove novelty. The defensible conclusion is that the module provides a potentially novel tissue-pair network observation built around a well-established CCT core, rather than a newly discovered protein complex or mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both brain and colon, coordinated abundance of the CCT subunits supports folding and stabilization of newly synthesized or stress-sensitive proteins, while coupling to translation-related machinery represented by EIF3C and DARS1. This proteostasis activity may also facilitate correct localization or assembly of proteins involved in nuclear bodies and telomere maintenance, consistent with the globally corrected Cajal-body, nuclear-localization, and telomere annotations (37 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.9e-07–0.0403; see the table). The hypothesis predicts that perturbing the CCT core would alter folding capacity and possibly the stability or localization of selected translation and telomere-associated client proteins. This remains a mechanistic proposal: the co-abundance network and enrichment results do not establish directionality, physical binding of every member, or causal regulation of telomere maintenance.
