### Function

The strongest primary functional signal is phospholipase inhibitor activity (GO:MF phospholipase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0268), driven by ANXA2 and ANXA5. This supports a module related to calcium-dependent phospholipid binding, membrane-associated signaling, and regulation of phospholipase-sensitive membrane processes. The gene annotations additionally connect the module to phospholipid translocation through ATP10D and ANO3, ER protein folding and stress homeostasis through HSPA5, cell motility and cytoskeletal organization through ACTBL2, and extracellular-matrix remodeling through MMP1. The STRING supplementary signals are consistent with annexin biology, calcium/phospholipid binding, exosome-associated proteins, epithelial or cancer-associated samples, and stress-related biology (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 0.0021–0.0488), but they are supporting context rather than globally corrected enrichment. Thus, the most defensible interpretation is a membrane and vesicle-associated module linking phospholipid regulation with cellular stress, cytoskeletal behavior, and matrix remodeling. No additional GO or KEGG term beyond (GO:MF phospholipase inhibitor activity; q_global = 0.0268) passed global correction.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary identifies three secreted genes, while the individual annotations specifically identify ANXA2 as secreted to blood and MMP1 as secreted to the extracellular matrix. The module is also not uniformly blood-cell enriched. HPA reports mixed immune-cell patterns, including one immune-cell-enriched gene, one group-enriched blood pattern, low immune-cell specificity for some genes, and no immune-cell detection for others. All genes show cell-type enhancement, but across heterogeneous cell types rather than one coherent blood lineage. Tissue specificity is mixed, with low tissue specificity for several genes, tissue enhancement for others, and enrichment across sites including esophagus, gallbladder, stomach, brain, and epididymis. Protein detection is broadly compatible with the breast and colon samples for most genes, although one gene is not detected and one has no data in each tissue summary. The module may therefore reflect a shared epithelial, stromal, membrane-stress, or extracellular-remodeling context, with possible contribution from sample composition, rather than a simple circulating-protein or blood-cell signature.

### Novelty

The module may represent a potentially interesting conserved co-abundance pattern because it combines an annexin-centered phospholipid signal with ATP10D and ANO3 membrane-phospholipid biology, HSPA5 ER stress biology, ACTBL2 cytoskeletal biology, and MMP1 extracellular-matrix remodeling. However, the available evidence does not justify calling it a new biological complex or a definitively novel pathway. ANXA2–ANXA5 and ANXA2–HSPA5 are already supported by STRING, and the component genes have established individual functions and disease or literature associations. Conversely, the absence of a hu.MAP complex and the limited STRING connectivity indicate that the full seven-gene combination is not already represented as a well-established complex in these resources. The appropriate conclusion is that the conserved breast–colon co-abundance configuration is a candidate novel systems-level association, while novelty of the underlying biology remains unproven and requires independent validation, tissue-cell deconvolution, and perturbation or replication studies.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated membrane remodeling under epithelial or stromal stress. In this model, ATP10D and ANO3 influence phospholipid distribution and membrane ion or scrambling processes; ANXA2 and ANXA5 bind calcium-dependent phospholipid surfaces and inhibit phospholipase-related activity; HSPA5 provides an ER proteostasis and stress-response component; ACTBL2 links the state to cytoskeletal organization or motility; and MMP1 links it to extracellular-matrix turnover. The co-abundance pattern could therefore arise when membrane trafficking, ER stress, cell movement, and matrix remodeling are jointly engaged in breast and colon samples. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not an established causal pathway. A key alternative explanation is shared cellular composition or a common injury, remodeling, or tumor-associated state.

### Novelty

The exact module may represent a previously unreported cross-tissue co-abundance pattern, but the supplied evidence is insufficient to call it a new biological mechanism. Its lack of a globally enriched GO or KEGG term and its absence as a coherent STRING network make it difficult to compare with a canonical pathway. The AFTPH–KIF13A relationship is not novel in a broad molecular sense because it recurs in multiple hu.MAP complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_03095.1) through (hu.MAP huMAP3_12394.1) and is consistent with known adaptor-mediated trafficking. Several other components, including Wnt-regulatory, cell-cycle, mitochondrial, metabolic, and immune-associated genes, also have established individual functions. Thus the defensible conclusion is that the cross-tissue co-abundance combination is potentially novel, whereas its component biology is largely known and the module should not be presented as a newly discovered complex or pathway. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone cannot demonstrate novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module captures a shared tissue-state program rather than a single physical complex. In epithelial-rich breast and colon samples, variation in endosomal and cytoskeletal trafficking, membrane turnover, cytokinesis, and Wnt-related growth restraint may covary with variable representation of neutrophils and other immune or epithelial cell types. AFTPH and KIF13A could form a trafficking-centered anchor, while CHMP4C, TTL, CEP135, and related genes reflect membrane remodeling, microtubule organization, and cell division. CTNNBIP1 and MCC could reflect restraint of Wnt-dependent epithelial proliferation, whereas ARG1 and CRACR2A could mark immune or inflammatory composition. This model predicts that the module will separate with cell-composition measures and trafficking or cytoskeletal state, rather than behaving as one stoichiometric complex. It requires validation with independent cohorts, purified cell populations, protein-level data, and perturbation experiments.
