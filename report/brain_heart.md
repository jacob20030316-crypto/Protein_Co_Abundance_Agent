# Conserved co-abundance modules: brain and heart

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: brain and heart  
**Genes (12)**: SERPINA1, ABHD10, ACADM, ACADVL, CDH13, COQ8A, COQ9, CRYM, IGHA1, IGHG1, LDHD, LGALS3BP

### Function

The module does not have a GO/KEGG term passing global correction, so no GO/KEGG enrichment is claimed. Its gene composition and network topology nevertheless suggest a mixed metabolic and tissue-state signal. The strongest coherent biological theme is mitochondrial energy metabolism: ACADM and ACADVL participate in fatty-acid beta-oxidation, while COQ8A and COQ9 are linked to ubiquinone biosynthesis and mitochondrial respiratory function. LDHD is the network hub and is annotated for lactate catabolism, providing a possible connection to cellular redox and carbon metabolism. CRYM adds evidence for amino-acid and thyroid-hormone metabolism, whereas CDH13 adds vascular, neuronal, and cell-adhesion relevance. Because immunoglobulin and secreted inflammatory proteins are also present, the module should not be interpreted as a clean mitochondrial pathway. The most defensible interpretation is a conserved brain-heart co-abundance pattern combining oxidative metabolism with tissue or circulating-cell composition effects.

**Cell composition.** This is not a uniformly tissue-restricted intracellular module. HPA annotations show a mixture of mitochondrial and cytosolic proteins, cell-surface proteins, secreted proteins, and immunoglobulin components. SERPINA1 and LGALS3BP are secreted to blood, while IGHA1 and IGHG1 are immunoglobulin genes associated with plasma or memory B-cell compartments. The presence of these genes, together with immune-cell enrichment for several members, raises a substantial possibility that part of the shared brain-heart signal reflects blood, vascular, or shared immune-cell content rather than coordinated expression within cardiomyocytes and neurons. This interpretation is supported by the absence of SERPINA1 and IGHG1 protein detection in the sampled brain and heart tissues, despite their co-abundance relationships. At the same time, the module is not simply a blood signature: several genes are detected in brain and heart, and HPA links multiple members to heart muscle, brain neurons, cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, and skeletal muscle. Thus the composition is mixed, with a metabolic tissue component overlaid by secreted and immune-associated signals.

**GO/KEGG enrichment**: 0 terms pass global BH (q_global < 0.05).

### STRING and hu.MAP

STRING provides only two high-confidence pairwise relationships within the module: ACADM with ACADVL, and COQ8A with COQ9. These relationships agree with established biochemical coherence between fatty-acid oxidation enzymes and between coenzyme-Q-related proteins. The lack of additional high-confidence STRING partners means that most module edges are co-abundance relationships rather than previously catalogued physical or functional interactions in STRING. No hu.MAP complex contains multiple module members, so there is no independent complex-level support for the module as a single physical assembly. Overall, the external interaction resources support specific metabolic substructures but do not support treating the whole module as one known protein complex.

### Novelty

The module may be novel as a cross-tissue co-abundance pattern, but the evidence does not establish a new molecular pathway. Its strongest internal relationships connect proteins with already established functions in fatty-acid oxidation, ubiquinone biology, lactate handling, immunoglobulin biology, and extracellular or vascular processes. The lack of a matching hu.MAP complex and the limited STRING support indicate that the exact combined organization is not represented as a known physical complex. However, absence from those resources alone cannot prove novelty, especially because co-abundance modules can reflect shared cell composition, tissue mixture, or coordinated regulation rather than direct binding. The appropriate claim is therefore a potentially novel brain-heart co-abundance configuration built from mostly known biological components, requiring validation in purified cell populations and independent cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module captures a conserved energetic-stress or metabolic-demand program shared by brain and heart, centered on mitochondrial fatty-acid use and coenzyme-Q-dependent respiration, with LDHD-linked lactate handling providing a complementary carbon and redox axis. The immunoglobulin, SERPINA1, and LGALS3BP connections may arise from vascular or circulating immune-cell admixture, or from coordinated responses to tissue injury and inflammation, rather than from direct participation in the mitochondrial mechanism. A testable prediction is that the metabolic submodule will remain coordinated after removal of blood-derived cells, whereas the immunoglobulin and secreted-protein associations will weaken. A second prediction is that perturbation of COQ8A, COQ9, ACADM, or ACADVL will alter respiratory or lipid-oxidation phenotypes, while perturbation of the secreted and immunoglobulin genes will primarily affect the composition-associated branch.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: brain and heart  
**Genes (10)**: A1BG, A2M, SERPINC1, CA1, CP, C3, HBD, HPX, SERPING1, GC

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a circulating extracellular protease, complement, heme, metal, and coagulation-associated module rather than a brain- or heart-intrinsic pathway. Blood microparticle is the primary globally corrected cellular-component signal (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 2.38e-06), with nearly the entire module represented. Regulation of the humoral immune response (GO:BP regulation of humoral immune response; q_global = 0.00182) and regulation of complement activation (GO:BP regulation of complement activation; q_global = 0.0464) provide primary biological-process support. C3, SERPING1, and A2M directly support complement regulation; HPX supports heme binding, heme transport, and protection from oxidative stress; CP supports copper and iron homeostasis; SERPINC1 supports regulation of coagulation. CA1 and HBD form a small erythrocyte-associated branch. The module therefore most plausibly reflects coordinated abundance of blood-borne proteins involved in innate immunity, protease control, heme and metal handling, and vascular or blood-associated processes.

**Cell composition.** The module is strongly dominated by secreted plasma proteins and liver-associated proteins. HPA identifies most genes as secreted to blood, with liver enrichment especially frequent and hepatocytes the most frequent enriched single-cell population. The blood microparticle signal (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 2.38e-06), together with the STRING blood-microparticle results (STRING COMPARTMENTS Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 2.21e-07) and (STRING Component Blood microparticle; within-query FDR = 2.23e-07), supports a circulating or sample-compartment explanation. C3 has immune-cell enrichment, SERPING1 has enhancement in neutrophils and plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and the CA1-HBD branch is associated with erythrocytes and erythrocyte progenitors. However, most members are not detected in immune cells, so the module is not best described as a conventional leukocyte-intrinsic program. Its conservation between brain and heart may therefore reflect shared exposure to blood, vascular material, plasma carryover, or a shared extracellular compartment rather than a conserved neuronal-cardiomyocyte mechanism. HPA protein detection is limited in both sampled organs, which further weakens an interpretation based on local tissue protein production.

### Novelty

The underlying biology is not a new discovery: complement regulation, plasma protease inhibition, heme transport, metal handling, coagulation, and erythrocyte-associated proteins are well established, and many module relationships occur in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel result is the coordinated co-abundance of this particular subset across brain and heart. That claim remains provisional because the composition is highly plasma and liver associated, the module has limited internal connectivity beyond the extracellular hub relationships, and blood microparticle enrichment is exceptionally prominent. Absence from an interaction resource would not establish novelty, and here the repeated STRING and hu.MAP support argues against describing the module as a newly discovered molecular complex. Independent validation using tissue-dissection controls, blood-content markers, cell-resolved measurements, and replication cohorts would be needed before calling the cross-tissue module a new biological mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a shared circulating extracellular buffering system sampled in both organs. Liver-derived plasma proteins may travel with blood or blood microparticles into brain and heart samples, where complement and protease activity are restrained by C3, SERPING1, A2M, and SERPINC1, while HPX, CP, and GC manage heme, iron, copper, and vitamin-associated cargo. The CA1-HBD pair may report an erythrocyte-related component or blood exposure. In this model, the apparent brain-heart conservation reflects shared vascular access and extracellular transport rather than coordinated transcription within neurons and cardiomyocytes. A competing hypothesis is that some members participate locally in barrier, vascular, or inflammatory regulation, but the present evidence cannot distinguish that possibility from blood-derived co-abundance.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered mitochondrial pathway. Its principal function and many of its relationships are already well established by GO, KEGG, STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially new result is the conserved co-abundance organization of these known mitochondrial components across brain and heart, including the way substrate metabolism, respiratory complexes, ATP synthesis and mitochondrial architecture covary in the two tissue groups. Absence of some genes from hu.MAP or absence of particular pairwise STRING links does not demonstrate novelty, because co-abundance can reflect shared regulation, organelle abundance, cell state or pathway coordination rather than direct binding. Novelty therefore rests on the cross-tissue network context and its reproducibility, which would require independent cohorts, preservation testing and comparison with existing brain, heart and mitochondrial modules before claiming a new module.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in neurons and cardiomyocytes, coordinated regulation of mitochondrial mass, inner-membrane organization and oxidative substrate use produces the shared module. ACO2, CS, IDH2, DLAT and PDHB would route carbon into the TCA cycle; NDUFA10, NDUFA9, NDUFS1, NDUFS2, SDHA and UQCRC2 would support electron transfer; and ATP5F1A, ATP5F1C and ATP5PB would convert the proton gradient into ATP. IMMT may couple this metabolic program to cristae organization, while NNT may help maintain NADPH and redox balance. The co-abundance pattern could therefore report a coordinated mitochondrial capacity state rather than a novel physical supercomplex. This remains a hypothesis: the fact sheet establishes co-abundance and known annotations, but does not establish causal regulation, direct contact among all module members, or directionality between genes.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a coordinated energy-and-structural program shared by metabolically demanding neural and cardiac or striated-muscle cells. Its mitochondrial component includes acetyl-CoA and short-chain acyl-CoA handling through ACSS1 and PCCA, branched-chain amino-acid catabolism through DBT, MCCC1 and AUH, pyruvate entry into the Krebs cycle through PDHX and PDK2, respiratory-chain activity through NDUFV1, NDUFA6, BCS1L and COQ3, mitochondrial dynamics through OPA1, and energy-state regulation through SIRT3, SIRT5 and SLC25A4. A second component comprises the dystrophin-associated and basement-membrane apparatus, including DMD, DTNA, SGCD, LAMA2, LAMB2, ITGA7, HSPG2, PRELP and SORBS1, together with cardiac calcium handling represented by RYR2. This combination is biologically coherent with contraction, cell-matrix attachment, mitochondrial ATP production and metabolic flexibility in brain and heart. However, no GO or KEGG term passed global correction, so this functional interpretation is based on the individual gene annotations, network organization, STRING relationships and hu.MAP complexes rather than on statistically significant GO or KEGG enrichment. The absence of a globally enriched term does not invalidate the module.

**Cell composition.** The HPA profile argues against this being primarily a plasma-protein or blood-cell module. Most genes show cell-type enhancement in myonuclei or cardiomyocytes, with additional representation of astrocytes and related neural cell types, and the most frequent tissue associations are tongue, skeletal muscle and heart muscle. The module therefore plausibly reflects shared contractile, neural-support and metabolically active cell populations in the sampled tissues. It is not composed predominantly of secreted proteins, although extracellular-matrix and secreted components are present, including LAMA2, LAMB2, HSPG2, TINAGL1 and PRELP, as well as blood-associated CFB, VTN and IGHG3. These genes could reflect a minor vascular, stromal or blood-derived contribution, but the broad detection of many module proteins in both brain and heart and the strong muscle and neural cell-type pattern make blood contamination an insufficient explanation for the module as a whole.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular mechanism. Many of its relationships and complexes are already known, particularly mitochondrial respiration, branched-chain and pyruvate metabolism, the dystrophin-associated complex, laminin or integrin adhesion, and cardiac calcium handling. At the same time, the specific conserved co-abundance organization across brain and heart, including the connection between mitochondrial metabolic genes and muscle or basement-membrane genes, may represent a new systems-level observation in this dataset. The lack of a STRING enrichment result and the absence of some genes from hu.MAP do not establish novelty; they may reflect incomplete databases, tissue context or network construction. A defensible novelty claim is therefore limited to the particular cross-tissue module architecture and its reproducibility, which would require comparison with independent cohorts, preservation statistics and prior co-abundance networks.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this conserved module reflects a coupled cell-state program in which mitochondrial substrate utilization and respiratory capacity are coordinated with sarcolemmal or synaptic structural integrity. In cardiomyocytes and myonuclei, laminin-integrin and dystrophin-associated structures may stabilize force transmission while SLC25A4, NDUFV1, NDUFA6, ACSS1, PDHX, DBT and related enzymes maintain ATP production and metabolic flexibility. In neural tissue, the same program may operate in astrocytes, Bergmann glia and other metabolically active cells, with LAMA2, DTNA and associated matrix proteins supporting cell architecture and intercellular signaling. SIRT3, SIRT5 and OPA1 could couple nutrient state, mitochondrial protein regulation, oxidative stress control and organelle organization to this structural program. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway: co-abundance cannot by itself establish physical interaction, directionality or regulation.

### Novelty

The individual molecular functions and many relationships are not new: the CCT, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, EIF3, proteasome, and RAN-XPO1 relationships are supported by STRING and hu.MAP. The co-abundance organization across brain and heart may nevertheless be a useful systems-level observation, especially because it places protein folding, translation, RNA handling, nuclear transport, and protein turnover in one conserved cross-tissue module. However, the fact sheet does not establish that this exact module has not appeared in earlier co-expression or proteomics studies. Lack of a matching STRING or hu.MAP complex would not prove novelty, and here the extensive recovery of known complexes argues for caution. The defensible conclusion is that the module may be a new cross-tissue network-level combination of established machinery, but it is not evidence for a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both brain and heart, coordinated demand for cytoskeletal and other newly synthesized proteins induces a coupled quality-control program. CCT3, CCT5, CCT7, and CCT8 could fold selected substrates; EIF3C and EIF3L could support their translation; DARS1, KARS1, and QARS1 could maintain accurate aminoacyl-tRNA supply; DHX9, RAN, and XPO1 could coordinate RNA processing and nuclear-cytoplasmic traffic; and PSMB3, PSMC2, and PSMD13 could remove damaged or surplus proteins. NES and EZR may connect this intracellular program to tissue architecture or cytoskeletal state. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal chain: the co-abundance data and annotations support coordination, but do not show that one component directly regulates the others or that the module specifically controls telomeres.
