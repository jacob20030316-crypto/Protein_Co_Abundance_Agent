# Conserved co-abundance modules: lung and ovary

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: lung and ovary  
**Genes (12)**: SLC1A5, ACSL3, EMC1, PNPT1, PRKDC, QSOX2, SLC25A13, HM13, SUPV3L1, DARS2, TMEM214, TRAP1

### Function

The strongest function is mitochondrial RNA surveillance and degradation, supported by the globally corrected mitochondrial degradosome term (GO:CC mitochondrial degradosome; q_global = 0.0297), which contains PNPT1 and SUPV3L1. PNPT1 is a mitochondrial polynucleotide phosphorylase involved in mitochondrial RNA processing and degradation, while SUPV3L1 is a mitochondrial RNA helicase and degradosome component. The network also connects this RNA-quality-control axis with mitochondrial translation and metabolism through DARS2, TRAP1, and SLC25A13, and with amino-acid transport through SLC1A5. ACSL3 adds a lipid-metabolic component, whereas EMC1, HM13, and QSOX2 suggest connections to ER or Golgi membrane biology and protein maturation. These latter functional themes are gene-level interpretations and are not additional globally significant GO or KEGG enrichment claims. No STRING functional-enrichment term was reported.

**Cell composition.** This is not primarily a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary identifies only one secreted gene, and most proteins are intracellular, mitochondrial, ER, Golgi, or membrane-associated. Most genes have low tissue specificity, and protein detection is broadly compatible with both lung and ovary. The module is therefore unlikely to represent a simple circulating-protein signature. HPA does show cell-type enhancement for most genes, including neutrophils and several reproductive or stromal cell types, but the blood-cell-specificity summary is generally low. Shared cell populations, tissue handling, or a common cellular stress and metabolic state could therefore contribute to the cross-tissue pattern. SLC1A5 has immune-cell enhancement in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and several genes are enhanced in neutrophils, so a blood or immune-cell contribution should be tested rather than assumed absent.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial degradosome component is established rather than novel because PNPT1 and SUPV3L1 are linked by STRING and both occur in the globally significant term (GO:CC mitochondrial degradosome; q_global = 0.0297). Several additional relationships are also represented in hu.MAP (hu.MAP huMAP3_09592.1) through (hu.MAP huMAP3_08167.1). However, the cross-tissue co-abundance arrangement of the full module may still be a useful observation, particularly because most pairwise module edges are not represented as high-confidence STRING interactions and EMC1 and TMEM214 have no reported hu.MAP membership. This supports possible novelty of the network-level organization, not discovery of a new molecular function. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP alone cannot establish novelty. Independent replication, cell-composition adjustment, and comparison with published lung and ovary co-abundance modules would be needed before calling the module a new finding.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in a shared lung and ovary cell state, mitochondrial RNA turnover and quality control are coordinated with mitochondrial translation, amino-acid exchange, lipid activation, protein folding, and ER or Golgi membrane processing. In this model, the PNPT1-SUPV3L1 degradosome pair manages mitochondrial RNA quality, DARS2 supports mitochondrial translation, TRAP1 buffers mitochondrial protein-folding and respiratory stress, and SLC25A13 and SLC1A5 supply or exchange metabolites that support this state. ACSL3 could couple the state to fatty-acid metabolism, while EMC1, HM13, and QSOX2 could reflect membrane-protein insertion, intramembrane processing, or oxidative protein maturation. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not demonstrated causality; it should be tested with cell-type-resolved data, perturbation of the PNPT1-SUPV3L1 axis, and measurements of mitochondrial RNA processing and respiratory function.

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a conserved RNA-protein and translation-support module. Primary enrichment shows a ribonucleoprotein complex (GO:CC ribonucleoprotein complex; q_global = 0.00134), RNA binding (GO:MF RNA binding; q_global = 0.00303), nucleolar localization (GO:CC nucleolus; q_global = 0.0233), signal recognition particle binding (GO:MF signal recognition particle binding; q_global = 0.0339), and nucleic-acid binding (GO:MF nucleic acid binding; q_global = 0.0365). Together with the supplementary STRING results, this supports coordinated ribosome biogenesis, ribonucleoprotein assembly, translation initiation and elongation-related activity, and SRP-dependent targeting of nascent secretory or membrane proteins. The module is not simply a generic translation signal: it combines nucleolar ribosome-production factors, ribosomal proteins, translation-initiation factors, and the SRP subunits SRP68 and SRP72.

**Cell composition.** The module is composed of broadly expressed intracellular proteins rather than plasma proteins or secreted proteins. HPA assigns low tissue specificity to every module gene, identifies no secreted genes, and assigns low immune-cell specificity throughout. Thus, the shared lung–ovary signal is not strongly suggestive of blood contamination or a blood-cell-driven module. Several genes show cell-type-enhanced expression in oocytes, trophoblast-related cells, decidual stromal cells, or other epithelial and reproductive cell types, but there is no single dominant cell type in the supplied evidence. Protein detection is generally present in lung and ovary, although detection is incomplete for some genes, supporting biological presence while leaving open the possibility that cell composition contributes to the shared signal.

### Novelty

The molecular functions represented here are established: ribosome biogenesis, RNA binding, translation initiation, ribosomal structure, and SRP-mediated targeting are well-known activities of the encoded proteins. The module therefore should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular complex. Its potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance of this particular combination across lung and ovary, especially the coordinated relationship among nucleolar factors, ribosomal proteins, translation-initiation factors, and SRP components. That claim remains a systems-level hypothesis rather than proof of a new physical complex. Existing STRING links and the partial hu.MAP matches argue against novelty at the level of all pairwise relationships. Absence from a curated database alone cannot establish novelty; independent tissue cohorts, replication, perturbation, and comparison with published lung and ovary co-expression modules would be needed.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared lung and ovary cell populations, this module coordinates nucleolar ribosome production with cytoplasmic translation initiation and selective delivery of newly translated secretory or membrane proteins to the endoplasmic reticulum. BRIX1, RRS1, TCOF1, AATF, and DDX46 may support ribosomal or RNA-processing capacity; RPL3 and RPL6 may provide the ribosomal core; EIF4G1 and EIF3L may couple messenger-RNA recruitment to translation initiation; and SRP68 with SRP72 may connect translation to cotranslational membrane targeting. HSPH1 and CTBP2 may reflect additional stress-handling or transcriptional context rather than a core of the same physical complex. This model predicts that coordinated perturbation of the module would affect ribosome assembly, translation capacity, and handling of proteins entering the secretory pathway, but the supplied data do not establish directionality or causation.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a nuclear post-transcriptional RNA-processing module, centered on RNA binding, spliceosome-linked mRNA processing, exon-exon junction biology, nuclear speckles, nuclear ribonucleoprotein granules, and mRNA surveillance. The overall evidence favors a broadly expressed nuclear housekeeping or regulatory program rather than a blood-contamination signature, but the cross-tissue module should not be assumed to be specific to lung or ovary cells.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular pathway. Its dominant biology is already well established by the RNA-binding, splicing, exon-junction, nuclear-speck, and mRNA-surveillance annotations, by several direct STRING relationships, and by repeated hu.MAP overlap with known RNA-processing complexes. At the same time, the co-abundance organization across lung and ovary may represent a useful systems-level observation: the exact combination of central RNA-processing genes with the RSL1D1–UTP14A and SEC16A–CLINT1 peripheral branches is not demonstrated as a single canonical complex in the fact sheet. The limited STRING connectivity for several module members and the absence of hu.MAP support for some genes do not establish novelty; they may reflect incomplete interaction knowledge, indirect co-regulation, or tissue-dependent co-abundance. The defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially informative cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built largely from known biology, not a newly discovered molecular mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both lung and ovary, coordinated activity of nuclear RNA-processing machinery maintains transcript maturation, export, exon-junction marking, surveillance, and translation competence, with the module’s co-abundance reflecting a shared demand for RNA quality control and gene-expression plasticity. ACIN1, BCLAF1, ALYREF, RBM15, SNW1, FIP1L1, and PYM1 may form or participate in partially overlapping processing and export assemblies, while UTP14A and RSL1D1 could couple this program to nucleolar and ribosome-related capacity. The SEC16A–CLINT1 branch may reflect parallel cellular trafficking or membrane-biogenesis demands rather than direct incorporation into the same RNA complex. This hypothesis requires testing through cell-type-resolved expression, perturbation, RNA-splicing measurements, and physical interaction assays in relevant lung and ovary cell populations.

### Novelty

The module is not a new molecular-complex discovery in the strict sense. Its central mitochondrial relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the ATP synthase and complex III components have well-established functions. The potentially novel aspect is the coordinated co-abundance of mitochondrial energy machinery, ER organization and trafficking proteins, signaling components, and membrane adhesion proteins across lung and ovary. The fact sheet does not provide a reference network, tissue-specific replication, perturbation experiment, or comparison with previously reported cross-tissue modules, so a claim that this exact lung-ovary module is a new biological finding would be premature. Absence of hu.MAP or STRING support for individual edges is not evidence of novelty; it may simply indicate indirect regulation, context dependence, or incomplete database coverage. The defensible conclusion is that the module contains a plausible cross-tissue organization that merits validation, while its core molecular functions are established.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared lung and ovary cell populations, coordinated regulation of mitochondrial inner membrane architecture and respiratory ATP production is coupled to ER membrane remodeling, protein translocation, glycosylation, and vesicle trafficking. The mitochondrial arm, involving ATP5F1A, ATP5F1B, UQCRC1, UQCRC2, IMMT, ACO2, CPT2, and RDH13, could supply and regulate energy and membrane homeostasis, while ATL3, RTN4, SEC22B, SEC61G, and DDOST could organize ER structure and the movement and maturation of membrane proteins. ATP1A1, GNAI3, GNA11, and ALCAM could connect membrane energetic state to ion gradients, receptor signaling, adhesion, and cell-cell interactions. This is a mechanistic model rather than a demonstrated causal pathway; it requires validation by cell-type-resolved expression, protein-level measurements, and perturbation of mitochondrial or ER components.

### Novelty

The exact conserved co-abundance module across lung and ovary may be a new dataset-level observation, but the biological theme is not a new protein complex or pathway. Its genes are already connected by established plasma, HDL, extracellular-particle, complement, coagulation, and transport relationships in STRING and hu.MAP. The complete blood-microparticle overlap, liver and hepatocyte enrichment, secretion to blood, and absence of lung or ovary protein detection provide a parsimonious explanation based on circulating material. Therefore, the defensible claim is novelty of the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern, not novelty of the underlying biology. Absence of VTN or CLU from hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because the module is small and hu.MAP represents selected complexes rather than every functional association. Experimental validation would be needed to distinguish a reproducible biological transport signature from blood-content or sample-composition effects.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: variation in circulating plasma particles or extracellular vesicles produces a conserved abundance signal in lung and ovary samples. HDL-associated APOA1 and CLU may coordinate lipid transport and particle uptake, while ALB, TF, and VTN act as abundant carrier or adhesive components; SERPINC1 contributes anticoagulant regulation, and HBA1 reports erythrocyte or blood-particle contribution. The observed co-abundance could therefore arise from a shared vascular or sample-exposure compartment, with endocytosis and extracellular-particle handling accounting for the enriched biological-process terms. This hypothesis predicts that module abundance will track plasma or blood-contamination markers and will diminish in carefully perfused or cell-type-resolved tissue data. It does not establish that resident lung or ovarian cells synthesize the proteins.

### Novelty

This module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex or a newly discovered pathway. Much of its internal structure is already known through STRING interactions and hu.MAP representations of the CCT chaperonin, proteasome, spliceosome, and RNA-associated complexes. The potentially novel finding is the conserved co-abundance of these established systems across lung and ovary, including their particular network-level coupling. That claim is plausible because the module integrates several established complexes and has broad protein detection in both tissues, but it requires replication in independent cohorts, control for cell composition, and comparison with previously reported tissue co-abundance modules. The absence of a hu.MAP entry for some individual genes, or the absence of a STRING edge, does not establish novelty. The enrichment itself is robust because the listed globally significant GO/KEGG terms passed global correction; the STRING terms are supplementary and were not corrected across modules.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared lung and ovary cell populations, a common proteostasis and RNA-metabolism state coordinates CCT-mediated folding with spliceosomal RNA processing, RNA surveillance, Cajal-body or telomerase-RNA localization, and proteasomal removal of damaged or misassembled proteins. CCT subunits may support the folding or stability of RNA-processing and telomere-associated factors, while ATP-dependent helicases and proteasome ATPases provide remodeling and turnover capacity. This could be a general response to high macromolecular-processing demand or cellular stress, but the present co-abundance data do not establish directionality, physical co-assembly between all module members, or a causal role in telomere maintenance. The hypothesis can be tested by cell-type-resolved expression analysis, perturbation of CCT or proteasome activity, and measurement of spliceosome function, RNA surveillance, Cajal-body localization, and telomere-related readouts.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a DNA replication and replication-associated DNA repair module. The primary evidence is the coherent set of globally significant terms covering the MCM complex (GO:CC MCM complex; q_global = 3.81e-09), break-induced replication (GO:BP double-strand break repair via break-induced replication; q_global = 1.72e-08), the CMG complex (GO:CC CMG complex; q_global = 1.06e-07), replication initiation (GO:BP regulation of DNA-templated DNA replication initiation; q_global = 2e-07), the replication preinitiation complex (GO:CC DNA replication preinitiation complex; q_global = 2e-07), DNA replication (GO:BP DNA replication; q_global = 3.09e-06), helicase activities (5 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 4.15e-06–5.35e-06; see the table), the human DNA replication pathway (KEGG DNA replication - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 1.3e-05), homologous recombination (KEGG Homologous recombination - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00012), double-strand break repair (GO:BP double-strand break repair via homologous recombination; q_global = 0.000243), and the cell cycle (KEGG Cell cycle - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000243). NASP and RRM1 extend the module toward chromatin assembly and deoxyribonucleotide supply, respectively, and KPNA2 may connect nuclear import with DNA-associated processes. Thus, the strongest interpretation is coordinated replication licensing, fork unwinding, chromatin handling, nucleotide provision, and repair rather than a tissue-specific signaling pathway.

**Cell composition.** This is not a module dominated by plasma or secreted proteins: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and the proteins are predominantly nuclear, with RRM1 localized to the cytosol. The composition is instead strongly proliferation-associated and enriched for cell types involved in hematopoietic development. HPA annotations repeatedly point to erythrocyte progenitors, monocyte progenitors, and megakaryocyte progenitors, with additional enrichment in trophoblast and germ-cell populations. Several genes are tissue-enhanced in bone marrow or lymphoid tissue, whereas NASP and RRM1 have low tissue specificity. All genes have low immune-cell specificity, so this should not be described as a mature immune-cell module. The shared lung–ovary signal may therefore reflect proliferating cells or shared progenitor-like cellular states, and could also be influenced by blood or hematopoietic-cell content in tissue samples. It is not evidence for secretion. Protein detection in lung and ovary is consistent with the module being measurable in both tissues, but the differing detection patterns argue against assuming that every gene is equally active in every cell type.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a newly discovered molecular complex or a new DNA replication pathway. Its central pattern is already well established by the MCM complex, CMG-related hu.MAP assemblies, STRING relationships, and the globally significant GO and KEGG terms. The potentially new aspect is the observation that this particular combination of MCM genes, NASP, RRM1, and KPNA2 co-abunds across lung and ovary. That tissue-pair co-abundance observation may be novel in the present dataset, but the fact sheet does not provide an external replication cohort, perturbation experiment, or comparison with previously reported cross-tissue modules. Therefore the appropriate conclusion is contextual or dataset-level novelty, not discovery of a previously unknown mechanism. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP entry would not by itself establish novelty, and in this case several relevant relationships are already present.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared lung–ovary module reflects a conserved proliferative state in which MCM-mediated replication licensing and fork unwinding are coordinated with chromatin handling by NASP, nucleotide production by RRM1, and nuclear import involving KPNA2. In tissues containing proliferating progenitor-like or other cycling cells, this coordinated program could produce the observed cross-tissue co-abundance. The strong hematopoietic-progenitor pattern in HPA makes shared cellular composition or blood-associated progenitor content a competing explanation. Testing this hypothesis would require cell-type-resolved data, replication-state markers, and independent lung and ovary cohorts; the co-abundance data alone do not establish physical interaction, causal regulation, or active DNA damage.
