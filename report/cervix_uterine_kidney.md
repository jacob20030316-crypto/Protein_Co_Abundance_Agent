## Module 0

### Function

The strongest and most coherent interpretation is an endoplasmic-reticulum and organelle-membrane module. The primary evidence is the convergence of all globally corrected terms on organelle membranes, the ER membrane, ER subcompartments, the nuclear-outer-membrane–ER network, the ER, and organelle subcompartments (6 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 5.61e-05–0.0022; see the table). The extensive overlap among these terms indicates that the module is not best described as a single narrow pathway, but as a membrane-associated cellular program centered on ER organization, membrane protein handling, lipid metabolism, protein folding, and trafficking. STRING supplementary terms independently support transmembrane, integral-membrane, organelle-membrane, and ER localization (7 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 1.01e-06–0.0016), (STRING Keyword Endoplasmic reticulum; within-query FDR = 0.0177) (STRING Keyword Signal-anchor; within-query FDR = 0.0308) (STRING Component Membrane; within-query FDR = 0.0309). Several genes add connected functional layers: CANX and ERO1A support ER protein folding and redox quality control; EMC1 supports insertion of membrane proteins into the ER; ATL3 and RTN1 support ER tubular-network organization; ACSL3, ACSL4, DHCR24, and HACD3 support lipid or fatty-acid metabolism; and CD44, ITGB1, SLC3A2, and SLC1A5 support cell-surface adhesion or nutrient transport. PNPT1, GFM1, SUPV3L1, OPA1, and related genes indicate a mitochondrial RNA-processing, translation, and organelle-maintenance component. Thus, the module appears to capture coordinated membrane-organelle homeostasis rather than a single biochemical reaction.

**Cell composition.** This is not predominantly a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: only a minority of the genes are annotated as secreted, while most have intracellular, membrane, ER, mitochondrial, Golgi, or peroxisomal locations. HPA tissue specificity is generally low, so the shared signal is compatible with broadly expressed cellular machinery rather than a sharply tissue-restricted program. Protein detection is broadly concordant in cervix_uterine and kidney, supporting biological presence in both tissues, although this does not establish cell-type origin. The HPA single-cell annotations show frequent enhancement in neutrophils, neutrophil progenitors, monocytes, trophoblast-related cells, epithelial cells, and epicardial cells. Blood-cell specificity is low for most genes, but several genes show immune-cell enhancement or enrichment. Therefore, blood contamination or a shared immune-cell component should be considered, especially for CD44, GPD2, CKAP4, NCSTN, ADPGK, and related genes. At the same time, the prevalence of ER and mitochondrial proteins, together with broad tissue expression, argues against explaining the entire module as a plasma-protein or blood-cell artifact. It may instead reflect shared epithelial or stromal cellular programs, with a possible contribution from infiltrating myeloid cells.

### Novelty

The data support a potentially interesting conserved co-abundance pattern, but they do not establish a wholly new molecular complex or pathway. The ER-centered interpretation is strongly consistent with established biology and with known STRING and hu.MAP relationships, particularly the CANX–GANAB–ERO1A–EMC1 branch, the lipid-associated ACSL and HACD3–DHCR24 relationships, and the mitochondrial RNA-processing branch. Consequently, absence of some edges from STRING or hu.MAP cannot be used as proof of novelty. The potentially novel aspect is the coordinated cross-tissue association of ER membrane organization, lipid handling, cell-surface transport or adhesion, and mitochondrial maintenance in cervix_uterine and kidney. Whether that association is tissue-conserved biology, shared cellular composition, or a technical or sampling effect requires replication in independent cohorts, cell-type-resolved data, and ideally perturbation or protein-level validation. The most defensible conclusion is therefore that this is a potentially novel module-level association built from partly established subnetworks, not a demonstrated novel mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in shared epithelial or stromal cell populations of cervix_uterine and kidney, an ER membrane homeostasis program coordinates membrane-protein insertion and folding with ER topology, lipid remodeling, nutrient transport, and mitochondrial quality control. EMC1, CANX, ERO1A, GANAB, ATL3, RTN1, and related genes could maintain ER architecture and the folding or processing of membrane proteins; ACSL3, ACSL4, DHCR24, and HACD3 could adjust the lipid environment of those membranes; CD44, ITGB1, SLC3A2, and SLC1A5 could connect the membrane state to adhesion and nutrient uptake; and PNPT1, GFM1, SUPV3L1, OPA1, and associated mitochondrial genes could preserve organelle function. A secondary hypothesis is that part of the shared signal comes from common myeloid or neutrophil-associated cells in the two tissue datasets. These hypotheses should be tested by cell-type-resolved coexpression, spatial localization, protein abundance measurements, and perturbation of ER or lipid-homeostasis genes.

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a broadly expressed, intracellular gene-expression and biosynthetic program rather than a specialized cervix or kidney pathway. Primary evidence comes from globally corrected terms for nucleotide and ATP binding (12 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 5.77e-10–2.09e-07; see the table), ATP-dependent activity (GO:MF ATP hydrolysis activity; q_global = 0.000152), RNA binding (GO:MF RNA binding; q_global = 0.000534), chaperone activity (GO:MF ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone; q_global = 7.46e-05), (GO:MF protein folding chaperone; q_global = 0.00173), (GO:MF unfolded protein binding; q_global = 0.00573), aminoacyl-tRNA ligase activity and tRNA aminoacylation (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.00595–0.00742; see the table), the aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis pathway (KEGG Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0095), nucleobase-containing compound biosynthesis (GO:BP nucleobase-containing compound biosynthetic process; q_global = 0.00332), and gene expression (GO:BP gene expression; q_global = 0.0262). The module therefore combines protein translation and folding, purine and one-carbon-supported biosynthesis, RNA processing, and genome maintenance. The spliceosome terms (GO:BP RNA splicing, via transesterification reactions; q_global = 0.0482) (GO:BP RNA splicing, via transesterification reactions with bulged adenosine as nucleophile; q_global = 0.0482) (GO:BP mRNA splicing, via spliceosome; q_global = 0.0482) and telomere-related terms (GO:BP telomere maintenance; q_global = 0.0324), (GO:BP positive regulation of telomere maintenance via telomerase; q_global = 0.0368), (GO:BP telomere organization; q_global = 0.0386) support additional RNA-processing and DNA-maintenance components, but do not imply that telomere biology is the sole module function. The sperm-related and Cajal-body terms are driven by the CCT subunits and should be treated as narrower annotations within a larger housekeeping program.

**Cell composition.** The module is dominated by intracellular, broadly expressed proteins rather than plasma or secreted proteins. HPA classifies most genes as having low tissue specificity, and almost all have low immune-cell specificity; only a small minority are secreted or intracellular-and-membrane proteins. Protein detection is widespread in both cervix_uterine and kidney. This argues against the module being primarily a plasma-protein or blood-cell contamination signature. The cell-type annotations for spermatids, trophoblasts, cardiomyocytes, and other specialized cells likely reflect shared cellular programs, proliferative state, or annotation overlap rather than a literal common cell population in these tissues. The module is consequently compatible with a shared housekeeping, biosynthetic, proteostasis, and RNA-processing state.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular pathway. Its principal components belong to well-established translation, purine-biosynthesis, chaperone, spliceosome, proteasome, and DNA-repair systems, with direct STRING and hu.MAP support. What may be novel is the reproducible co-abundance organization of these systems as one conserved module across cervix_uterine and kidney. That cross-tissue co-abundance observation is a systems-level finding, but it requires replication in independent cohorts, control for cellular composition, and comparison with other tissue-pair modules before it can be claimed as tissue-specific novelty. Lack of STRING or hu.MAP coverage for individual genes would not establish novelty, and the available evidence instead favors a conserved coordination of known cellular programs.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module represents a coordinated cellular resource-allocation state in which ATP and nucleotide availability support aminoacyl-tRNA charging, purine production, RNA splicing, protein folding, proteasomal handling, and DNA maintenance. In cervix_uterine and kidney, cells experiencing comparable demands for protein turnover, stress adaptation, or biosynthetic activity may co-regulate these programs. The CCT and HSP components could buffer newly synthesized or stress-sensitive proteins, while the aminoacyl-tRNA and purine-biosynthesis components support translation and nucleotide supply; spliceosomal and DNA-repair components could preserve transcript and genome integrity. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway. It should be tested by measuring module activity across purified cell types, perturbing representative hubs, and examining coordinated effects on translation, nucleotide pools, RNA splicing, proteostasis, and DNA repair.

### Novelty

The core molecular functions are established and are represented in STRING and hu.MAP, so the module does not establish a new nucleocytoplasmic-transport mechanism. The potentially novel observation is the coordinated co-abundance of transport receptors, nuclear-pore proteins, vesicle-trafficking factors, translation machinery, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, stress proteins, and genome-maintenance proteins across cervix_uterine and kidney. That cross-tissue organization may be a new systems-level finding, but the fact sheet does not provide an external replication cohort, perturbation experiment, or direct physical-interaction evidence for the complete module. Therefore, novelty should be stated conservatively: the constituent biology is well known, whereas the shared co-abundance configuration and its tissue-pair conservation are candidate novel observations. Absence of some module relationships from STRING or hu.MAP cannot by itself demonstrate novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects a general cellular logistics program that couples nuclear import and export with nuclear-pore function, RNA processing, translation, vesicle trafficking, proteostasis, and genome maintenance. In both tissues, variation in the abundance or activity of this program could coordinate the movement of regulatory proteins and RNAs, the production of new proteins, and the response to replication or proteotoxic stress. The germline and erythrocyte-progenitor signals suggest that the program may be especially prominent in cells with high biosynthetic, proliferative, or differentiation demands, but this is a hypothesis rather than proof of a shared cell population. A direct test would examine whether the module tracks nuclear-cargo flux, cell-cycle state, or stress-response activity at single-cell or perturbational resolution.

The broad biological function is not a new discovery: ribosome biogenesis, nucleolar RNA processing, heterogeneous-ribonucleoprotein organization, and chromatin-associated RNA regulation are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The module may nevertheless be a new tissue-pair observation because it identifies a conserved co-abundance configuration across cervix_uterine and kidney, including links between a core ribosome-biogenesis neighborhood and RNA-binding, chromatin, transport, and cytoplasmic RNA-control components. That tissue-pair specific organization is not established by the supplied databases. However, absence of some module edges from STRING or hu.MAP cannot by itself establish novelty, because database coverage, context dependence, and co-abundance relationships differ from physical-complex evidence. The defensible conclusion is therefore novelty of the integrated tissue co-abundance pattern as a potential observation, not novelty of the underlying molecular functions.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module reflects a conserved cell-state program in which demand for ribosome production and nucleolar RNA maturation is coordinated with broader RNA quality control, RNA transport, chromatin remodeling, and stress-responsive post-transcriptional regulation. In this model, the GTPBP4-DDX21-DCAF13-NOP2-centered nucleolar machinery supports precursor-rRNA processing and ribosomal-subunit assembly, while the HNRNP-MATR3-DHX9 branch handles nuclear RNA packaging and processing. The FACT and NuRD-related components, represented by SUPT16H and the MTA2-RBBP4 pair, could couple transcriptional chromatin state to this RNA-production demand. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than proof of direct physical cooperation among every module member; it should be tested with cell-type-resolved expression, perturbation, and nucleolar or RNA-processing assays in both tissues.
