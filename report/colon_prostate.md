# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and prostate

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and prostate  
**Genes (15)**: ABCF1, PRMT5, CHD4, CNOT1, EIF3C, IPO4, NAA15, PSMC4, PSMC2, PSMD13, RBM12, UPF1, EPRS1, LARS1, RARS1

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a conserved intracellular protein-complex and proteostasis module. The primary evidence is enrichment for intracellular protein-containing complexes (GO:CC intracellular protein-containing complex; q_global = 0.00859) and, more specifically, the proteasome regulatory particle and proteasome accessory complex (GO:CC proteasome regulatory particle; q_global = 0.0142)(GO:CC proteasome accessory complex; q_global = 0.0142). The proteasome signal is driven by PSMC2, PSMC4 and PSMD13, whereas (GO:CC intracellular protein-containing complex; q_global = 0.00859) also captures a translation-related aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase group comprising EPRS1, LARS1 and RARS1, together with genes involved in translation, RNA handling, chromatin regulation and protein maturation. Thus, the module is best viewed as a coordinated housekeeping program involving protein synthesis and quality control rather than as a narrowly tissue-specific pathway. STRING terms concerning amino-acid metabolism, macromolecule metabolism, PTEN regulation, NF-kappaB, hypoxia-related processing and antigen-processing pathways (STRING RCTM PTEN Regulation; within-query FDR = 0.0352)(STRING RCTM Metabolism of amino acids and derivatives; within-query FDR = 0.0352)(STRING Process Cellular macromolecule metabolic process; within-query FDR = 0.0384)(STRING RCTM Activation of NF-kappaB in B cells; within-query FDR = 0.0439)(STRING RCTM Oxygen-dependent proline hydroxylation of Hypoxia-inducible Factor Alpha; within-query FDR = 0.0439)(STRING RCTM ER-Phagosome pathway; within-query FDR = 0.0439)(STRING RCTM Cross-presentation of soluble exogenous antigens (endosomes); within-query FDR = 0.0439) are supplementary context only and do not replace the primary GO evidence.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes. It is also not blood-cell enriched; nearly all genes have low immune-cell specificity, with one gene not detected in immune cells, and no enriched blood-cell category is reported. Most genes have low tissue specificity, and protein detection is reported in both colon and prostate for most genes. The module therefore appears broadly expressed and intracellular, consistent with shared cellular housekeeping or proteostasis activity rather than a blood-contamination signature. HPA single-cell annotations include several cell-type-enhanced genes, but the diverse annotations do not establish a common contaminating cell population. IPO4 is tissue-enhanced in testis, but this isolated observation does not define the module.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex. Its proteasome and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase components reproduce relationships already represented in STRING and hu.MAP (STRING Component Proteasome regulatory particle; within-query FDR = 0.015)(hu.MAP huMAP3_02637.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_13688.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_05064.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_09656.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_13725.1). The potentially novel result is the conserved co-abundance of these established components with RNA surveillance, translation initiation, chromatin remodeling, protein import, RNA decay and protein maturation genes across colon and prostate. Database absence for some individual edges is not evidence of novelty, because incomplete interaction coverage, tissue context and co-abundance-specific regulation can all explain missing records. The appropriate conclusion is a potentially novel tissue-pair co-abundance organization built from largely established cellular machinery; direct novelty would require comparison with prior tissue co-abundance modules and independent replication.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both colon and prostate, variation in cellular protein-production demand or proteotoxic stress coordinates aminoacyl-tRNA charging and translation with proteasome-mediated clearance. The aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase group could reflect increased translational capacity, while the proteasome group could maintain protein quality by removing damaged or misassembled proteins. UPF1, EIF3C, CNOT1, CHD4 and related genes may couple this state to RNA surveillance, translation control, chromatin regulation and gene-expression turnover. The observed edges between these functional groups are co-abundance relationships, not proof that the proteins form one physical machine. This hypothesis can be tested by examining whether module activity tracks translational load, ubiquitin-proteasome activity, unfolded-protein stress and coordinated RNA-surveillance changes in independent colon and prostate cohorts.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: colon and prostate

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a nuclear DNA-replication and chromosome-maintenance program. The strongest primary evidence is the coordinated presence of MCM2, MCM5, MCM6 and MCM7 in the MCM, CMG and DNA-replication preinitiation complexes (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 3.8e-05–3.8e-05; see the table). These genes also support replication initiation, helicase activity, DNA-templated replication and replication-associated homologous-recombination or break-induced-replication processes (21 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000128–0.00656; see the table), (KEGG Homologous recombination - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.0289), (GO:BP double-strand break repair via homologous recombination; q_global = 0.0455). RFC4 extends the interpretation toward replication-fork progression, DNA strand elongation and repair (KEGG DNA replication - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.000128), (GO:MF single-stranded DNA helicase activity; q_global = 0.000188), (4 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 0.000606–0.00155; see the table). NASP, DNMT1 and SMC2 are consistent with replication-dependent chromatin assembly, maintenance of DNA methylation and chromosome organization (GO:BP DNA replication; q_global = 0.00155), (GO:CC nuclear chromosome; q_global = 0.00337), (GO:CC chromosome; q_global = 0.0168). The module therefore most likely reflects proliferative nuclear activity involving replication licensing and fork operation, with links to chromatin handling and chromosome organization. The remaining genes may represent connected translation, nucleotide, metabolic or mitochondrial-support processes, but they are not sufficient to redefine the central function.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes, and most proteins are detected in colon and prostate. The genes are predominantly intracellular, with nucleoplasmic, nuclear-membrane, cytosolic or vesicular locations. HPA reports low immune-cell specificity for the module and no enriched blood-cell category, arguing against a simple blood-cell contamination explanation. However, every gene has cell-type-enhanced annotation, and several genes are enhanced in erythrocyte progenitors, megakaryocyte progenitors, monocyte progenitors or trophoblast populations. Bone marrow and lymphoid tissue also recur among enriched tissues. These patterns are compatible with a conserved proliferative or progenitor-like cell state shared across tissues, rather than evidence that the module is specifically a blood module. The lack of colon detection for NUP155 and DNMT1 in HPA, alongside broad detection of most other module proteins, should be treated as an assay-context observation rather than proof that those genes are absent from the tissues.

### Novelty

This is a potentially novel co-abundance association, because the module has a compact hub-and-neighbors structure without corresponding STRING interactions, hu.MAP complexes, or enriched GO and KEGG terms. ### Novelty

The biological function is not a new pathway: nuclear pores, spliceosomes, RNA helicases, transcriptional regulation, RNA export, and polyadenylation are established processes. The module-level finding may still be novel because it identifies a conserved co-abundance relationship shared by colon and prostate and connects several otherwise distinct but biologically linked nuclear systems. The substantial STRING and hu.MAP overlap means that the module is strongly grounded in known molecular machinery, so it should not be described as a wholly novel protein-interaction complex. Conversely, relationships absent from STRING or hu.MAP cannot by themselves establish novelty, because database coverage, experimental context, and co-abundance relationships differ. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance organization of largely established nuclear RNA-processing and pore components, requiring comparison with prior tissue co-abundance studies and independent datasets before claiming a new biological mechanism.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both colon and prostate, coordinated abundance of nuclear-pore components may couple nucleocytoplasmic transport with the production, splicing, quality control, and export of mature RNA. The nucleoporin group could provide the transport and nuclear-periphery scaffold, while spliceosomal and RNA-binding genes process transcripts and genes such as XRN2, CSTF2, SYMPK, and POLR2B contribute to transcript termination and three-prime-end maturation. Central genes such as SRRT, DDX46, and TCERG1 may help coordinate these activities across nuclear speckles, nucleoplasm, and pore-associated compartments. This is a mechanistic model inferred from co-abundance, annotations, STRING, and hu.MAP; the data do not establish physical recruitment, directionality, or causal regulation. The spermatogenic cell-type signals suggest a possible relevance to highly active or developmentally specialized RNA programs, but that extension remains a hypothesis for testing.
