### Novelty

This module should not be called a wholly new molecular pathway. Several of its strongest relationships and subnetworks are already known in STRING or hu.MAP, especially the plasma-protein, glycogen, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, metabolic, actin, and retromer-related groups. The potentially novel aspect is the co-abundance of these established systems across brain and cervix_uterine, but that observation requires independent replication and adjustment for blood content, cell composition, tissue injury, and technical effects. Absence of an edge from STRING or absence of a complex from hu.MAP does not establish novelty, because those resources are incomplete and the module may reflect co-regulation, shared abundance, indirect biology, or sample composition rather than physical binding. The safest conclusion is that this is a potentially interesting cross-tissue co-abundance pattern built from largely established biological components, with novelty at the module level remaining unproven.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module may arise from a common tissue-state or sample-composition program in which circulating plasma and blood-cell signals are combined with intracellular programs for cytoskeletal maintenance, vesicle and receptor trafficking, redox and intermediary metabolism, glycogen turnover, and protein synthesis. In this model, blood-derived FGG, ORM1, and AHSG provide a shared extracellular signal; SPTB, ACTN1, CAPZB, and WDR1 reflect membrane or actin architecture; SORL1, VPS35, SNX6, ADAM10, and MYO6 reflect endocytic and cargo sorting; and IDH2, PGD, FH, GLUD1, GYS1, and PYGB reflect metabolic adaptation. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal mechanism. It should be tested by controlling for blood and cell-type composition, examining within-tissue reproducibility, and validating protein abundance and spatial localization in independent samples.

### Function

The strongest integrated interpretation is a cytoskeletal–mitochondrial maintenance module. Primary enrichment is restricted to microtubule binding (GO:MF microtubule binding; q_global = 0.0207), contributed by CLASP2, DCTN1, DNM1L, KIF21A and OPA1. This is consistent with microtubule organization, intracellular transport and organelle positioning: CLASP2 regulates microtubule plus ends and Golgi organization; DCTN1 links dynactin to microtubule-dependent transport; KIF21A supports microtubule-based movement and axonal transport; and DNM1L and OPA1 regulate mitochondrial dynamics. The remaining genes add coherent metabolic, vesicular, membrane-scaffold and cytoskeletal functions, including mitochondrial energy metabolism, vesicle trafficking, spectrin-based membrane organization and amino-acid utilization. No additional GO or KEGG term passed global correction. The evidence therefore supports a broad cellular logistics and organelle-homeostasis interpretation more strongly than a narrowly defined pathway.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the secretome annotation is absent for all module genes. It is also unlikely to be a blood-cell-driven module, because nearly all genes have low immune-cell specificity; only KIF21A has a group-enriched blood-cell annotation. Most genes are broadly detected in both brain and cervix_uterine, and many have low tissue specificity, which is compatible with shared cellular machinery rather than a tissue-exclusive program. The tissue-enhanced annotations point mainly toward brain, retina, muscle, tongue and heart, while cell-type-enhanced annotations are distributed across neuronal, epithelial, muscle, vascular and reproductive cell types. Thus, shared cell biology, cellular composition, or general metabolic and structural requirements may contribute to the cross-tissue signal. HPA protein detection supports broad presence in both tissues, with limited exceptions.

### Novelty

The module should not be called wholly novel, because its component biology is already established: microtubule binding is supported by (GO:MF microtubule binding; q_global = 0.0207) and (STRING Function Microtubule binding; within-query FDR = 0.0296), spectrin pairing is supported by (hu.MAP huMAP3_07940.1), (hu.MAP huMAP3_05473.1) and (hu.MAP huMAP3_09165.1), and mitochondrial enzyme association is supported by (hu.MAP huMAP3_12248.1). The individual genes also have substantial prior functional and disease literature. Nevertheless, the particular cross-tissue co-abundance configuration may represent a new systems-level observation, especially because the module combines microtubule and membrane scaffolding, mitochondrial dynamics, vesicle handling and metabolism without being captured as one dominant known complex. Absence of many module relationships from STRING or hu.MAP is evidence of incomplete cataloguing, not proof of novelty. Establishing a new finding would require replication in independent cohorts, assessment of cell-composition effects, and comparison with previously published tissue co-expression modules.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this shared module reflects coordinated maintenance of organelle distribution and energy supply in cells that require substantial intracellular transport and structural stability. Microtubule regulators and motors may coordinate cargo movement with Golgi and vesicle trafficking, spectrins may stabilize the membrane–cytoskeleton interface, and DNM1L–OPA1 may adjust mitochondrial architecture and energetic capacity. DBT, PDHB, OXCT1, NNT and GOT1 could provide metabolic support for these demands. In brain, this could be especially relevant to neuronal transport and mitochondrial maintenance; in cervix_uterine, the same signal may arise from epithelial, stromal or other shared cell populations. This is a mechanistic proposal, not a demonstrated physical pathway, and the sparse direct interaction map argues for testing it experimentally.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this conserved module reflects coordinated control of nuclear RNA production and quality control in cells present in both tissues. ### Novelty

The module is not a new discovery of the underlying molecular machinery: many of its central relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, including mitochondrial translation, respiratory-chain or ATP-synthase associations, proteasome membership, and the MRPL11–MRPL13 relationship. However, the cross-tissue co-abundance pattern shared by brain and cervix_uterine could still be a new systems-level observation. Therefore, the defensible conclusion is conditional novelty: the component biology is established, whereas the specific cross-tissue organization and its biological interpretation remain potentially novel but require comparison with published co-abundance modules, tissue transcriptomic studies, and independent datasets. Absence from a database alone would not establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a conserved intracellular program in which mitochondrial protein production and respiratory energy generation are coordinated across brain and cervix_uterine cell populations. Mitochondrial ribosome and translation factors may support production of mitochondrially encoded respiratory-chain components, while ATP synthase, complex-I-associated proteins, SUCLG1, ECHS1, and MMAB couple oxidative phosphorylation to central and cofactor-dependent metabolism. HTATIP2, BAG2, CRIP1, TPD52, and the proteasome pair may connect this metabolic state to stress responses, protein turnover, survival, or cell-state regulation. This is a mechanistic hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal pathway.
