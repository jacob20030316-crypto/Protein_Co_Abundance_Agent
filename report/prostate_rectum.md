### Function

This is a conserved contractile cell–matrix adhesion module, most consistent with a smooth-muscle, vascular, stromal, or myoepithelial structural program. The primary evidence is exceptionally coherent: (GO:CC myofibril; q_global = 1.53e-16) and (GO:CC contractile muscle fiber; q_global = 1.53e-16) identify myofibril and contractile muscle fiber, while (GO:CC sarcomere; q_global = 2.09e-11), (GO:CC I band; q_global = 3.74e-09), (GO:CC Z disc; q_global = 4.32e-08), (GO:CC stress fiber; q_global = 0.000181), (GO:CC contractile actin filament bundle; q_global = 0.000181), (GO:CC actomyosin; q_global = 0.00034), (GO:CC actin filament bundle; q_global = 0.000465), and (GO:CC costamere; q_global = 0.000975) support sarcomeric, I-band, Z-disc, stress-fiber, actomyosin, contractile-bundle, and costamere organization. The amoebiasis term (KEGG Amoebiasis - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00279) is best treated as a pathway annotation artifact or broad host-interface annotation rather than evidence for an infectious function. Overall, the module represents an integrated actomyosin–integrin–basement-membrane system, not merely an isolated muscle-expression signature.

**Cell composition.** HPA strongly favors a resident contractile and stromal interpretation. Smooth-muscle cells are the most frequent enriched cell type, with additional enrichment in vascular smooth muscle cells, hepatic stellate cells, decidual stromal cells, and peritubular myoid cells. Tissue enrichment in blood vessel, skeletal muscle, heart muscle, intestine, and tongue is also consistent with contractile or structural tissues. The module is not dominated by plasma proteins or secreted proteins: only a small subset is annotated as secreted, including extracellular-matrix laminin and collagen components and circulating gelsolin. Therefore, a plasma-protein explanation is unlikely. However, the HPA profile includes neutrophil, basophil, platelet, and megakaryocyte associations for subsets of genes, so some contribution from blood or vascular cells cannot be excluded, particularly in tissue samples containing vessels or inflammatory cells. Protein detection is present for many genes in both prostate and rectum, but several structurally important genes have weak or absent immunohistochemical detection in one or both tissues; this is compatible with transcript-level co-abundance, cell-mixture effects, isoform differences, or assay sensitivity rather than proof that the module is absent.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular pathway. Its principal biology—actomyosin organization, focal adhesion, integrin signaling, laminin-based matrix attachment, and smooth-muscle contractility—is already well established by the globally corrected annotations (21 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 1.53e-16–4.06e-06; see the table), by STRING (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 1.2e-13–5.37e-08), and by hu.MAP complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_09385.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11194.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_06647.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_04827.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_12658.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09068.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_01175.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_08112.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11074.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_03395.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_06153.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09579.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11076.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10673.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_14233.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_08205.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_06763.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_12303.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09542.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09165.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_10980.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_11349.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_13510.1). The potentially novel aspect is the discovery of a co-abundance module conserved specifically across prostate and rectum, linking these established components in a shared tissue-pair expression pattern. That claim requires comparison with published cross-tissue coexpression modules, independent cohorts, and cell-composition-adjusted data. STRING or hu.MAP omissions alone cannot establish novelty, because those databases catalogue molecular interactions and complexes rather than this particular cross-tissue co-abundance pattern.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module reflects a shared population of contractile, vascular, or stromal cells in prostate and rectum whose extracellular matrix is coupled to an actomyosin apparatus. Laminin and collagen components may engage integrin-mediated adhesion through ITGA5, with TLN1, FERMT2, ILK, LIMS1, PARVA, FLNA, and VCL transmitting matrix attachment into actin networks. MYLK, CALD1, PPP1R12A, TPM1, TPM2, CFL2, and related proteins could then regulate calcium- and phosphorylation-dependent force generation, filament stability, and remodeling. This coupling could support tissue tension, barrier or junction maintenance, wound response, and controlled migration. A competing explanation is that the module is driven partly by shared vascular or blood-associated cell content rather than by identical epithelial cells in both organs. This hypothesis should be tested with single-cell deconvolution, spatial localization, and perturbation of integrin–ILK–actomyosin signaling.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial respiratory interpretation is not a new biological discovery: the principal complex I, complex III, and complex IV relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the individual gene functions are well established. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance pattern shared by prostate and rectum, particularly the way respiratory-chain genes are coordinated with mitochondrial dynamics, redox defense, lipid metabolism, RNA handling, and nuclear-envelope genes. However, the fact sheet does not provide an external catalogue of previously reported prostate–rectum modules or an independent replication set. Therefore this should be described as a potentially useful cross-tissue association and a hypothesis-generating organization of known biology, not as a proven new module or newly discovered molecular complex. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP annotation for peripheral genes would not establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module represents coordinated variation in mitochondrial respiratory capacity across prostate and rectum. A core involving complex I, complex III, and complex IV may regulate electron transfer, proton motive force, and ATP production, while CHCHD3, IMMT, MTX1, and DNM1L may couple this activity to cristae architecture and mitochondrial dynamics. ETFA, HADHB, DLAT, SCP2, HSDL2, and TECR may connect respiratory output to fatty-acid, pyruvate, sterol, and membrane-lipid metabolism, while PRDX3 may buffer the associated oxidative burden. Under this model, the shared signal could arise from common metabolic demand or mitochondrial content across relevant cell populations in both tissues. This is a mechanistic hypothesis from co-abundance and annotation concordance; it does not establish directionality, causality, altered respiratory flux, or a disease mechanism.
