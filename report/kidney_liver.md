# Conserved co-abundance modules: kidney and liver

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: kidney and liver  
**Genes (17)**: ABCF1, CD2AP, DDX46, EIF3L, FUS, EIF4E, MAT2A, MYG1, NSFL1C, NUP107, NUP210, PPM1G, RCC1, SAE1, SRRT, FARSB, VTA1

### Function

The strongest interpretation is a broadly expressed nuclear gene-expression and nucleocytoplasmic-transport module. The primary enrichment (GO:CC nuclear lumen; q_global = 0.0457) is nuclear lumen, supported by broad representation of nuclear and nucleoplasmic proteins. Its genes also connect RNA processing, pre-mRNA handling, translation initiation, RNA export, protein modification, and cell-cycle or chromosome-regulation functions. The STRING result (STRING RCTM Processing of Capped Intron-Containing Pre-mRNA; within-query FDR = 0.0335) provides supplementary support for capped intron-containing pre-mRNA processing, but it is not global enrichment evidence. The module therefore appears to coordinate nuclear pore function with RNA maturation, export, and downstream translation, rather than representing a narrowly specialized biochemical pathway.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: HPA reports no secreted genes. Most genes have low tissue specificity, and immune-cell specificity is generally low, arguing against a module driven primarily by blood contamination or a shared immune-cell population. Protein detection is common in kidney and liver, although not universal. The HPA single-cell annotations show enrichment in several specialized cell types, especially germ-cell states, with additional annotations in enterocytes, oocytes, neural or glial cells, and progenitor populations. These annotations suggest that some components may be especially active in proliferative or differentiating cells, but the overall composition is more consistent with intracellular housekeeping and nuclear machinery than with a tissue-specific secretome or blood signature.

### STRING and hu.MAP

Known molecular relationships are concentrated around the nuclear pore. NUP107 and NUP210 have a strong STRING relationship, and NUP107 also connects to RCC1 in STRING. The hu.MAP results independently place NUP107 and NUP210 together in nuclear-pore-related complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_07492.1) and (hu.MAP huMAP3_09710.1), while RCC1 and NUP107 co-occur in a broader nuclear-pore and Ran-regulation complex (hu.MAP huMAP3_12581.1). STRING additionally links NUP107, NUP210, FUS, DDX46, EIF4E, and SRRT to capped intron-containing pre-mRNA processing (STRING RCTM Processing of Capped Intron-Containing Pre-mRNA; within-query FDR = 0.0335). These observations support a real nuclear transport and RNA-processing backbone, but the module is not simply a single known complex: only a small portion of all possible gene pairs has strong STRING support, and most module genes are not represented by shared hu.MAP complexes. Co-abundance therefore adds a tissue-pair expression relationship that extends beyond the currently catalogued physical interactions.

### Novelty

The module should not be called entirely novel because its central relationships are already established: NUP107-NUP210 is supported by STRING and hu.MAP (hu.MAP huMAP3_07492.1) (hu.MAP huMAP3_09710.1), and NUP107-RCC1 is supported by STRING and hu.MAP (hu.MAP huMAP3_12581.1). However, the complete kidney-liver co-abundance configuration may be a new systems-level observation because many of its links are not catalogued as high-confidence physical interactions or as a single hu.MAP complex. The evidence supports cautious novelty as a conserved co-abundance association connecting nuclear-pore, RNA-processing, translation, sumoylation, trafficking, and cell-cycle proteins. Absence from STRING or hu.MAP is not proof of a new molecular interaction; independent replication, perturbation, and tissue-cellular-resolution testing would be required.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in kidney and liver cells, coordinated abundance of nuclear-pore components such as NUP107 and NUP210 may couple nucleocytoplasmic transport to RNA maturation and export, while FUS, DDX46, SRRT, EIF3L, and EIF4E support processing and translation of the resulting transcripts. RCC1, SAE1, and PPM1G may connect this program to Ran-dependent nuclear organization, sumoylation, phosphorylation control, and cell-cycle regulation. The shared module could therefore represent a conserved demand for nuclear gene-expression throughput in viable, metabolically active, or differentiating cells. This is a mechanistic model inferred from co-abundance and annotations, not a demonstrated physical pathway for every module member.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: kidney and liver  
**Genes (5)**: ACADM, BCKDHA, SUCLG2, ACAT1, ACAA2

### Function

The module is most consistent with a mitochondrial matrix program for branched-chain amino-acid and related acyl-CoA metabolism. The primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation (KEGG Valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 0.00343), which contains ACAA2, ACADM, ACAT1 and BCKDHA, together with globally corrected mitochondrial-matrix localization (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.0257), which contains every module gene. ACAT1, ACAA2 and ACADM support acyl-CoA processing and fatty-acid oxidation; BCKDHA supports the branched-chain alpha-keto-acid dehydrogenase step; and SUCLG2 supports succinyl-CoA and tricarboxylic-acid-cycle metabolism. The STRING pathway annotations for propanoate metabolism and branched-chain amino-acid degradation (STRING KEGG Propanoate metabolism; within-query FDR = 0.0161) (STRING KEGG Valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation; within-query FDR = 0.0168) reinforce this interpretation but are supplementary rather than globally corrected evidence.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein, secreted-protein or blood-cell-enriched module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes and low immune-cell specificity for every gene. Protein detection is present for every module gene in both kidney and liver. The module is strongly tissue and cell-type associated, especially with liver and hepatocytes, while also showing associations with kidney, skeletal muscle, tongue, parietal cells and other epithelial or neural cell types. BCKDHA has low tissue specificity, whereas the other genes are tissue enhanced. Thus, the shared signal is more plausibly a conserved mitochondrial metabolic program in relevant parenchymal cells than blood contamination, although shared cell composition could contribute to the observed co-abundance.

### Novelty

The biochemical theme is not novel: the individual enzymes, their mitochondrial localization and several of their functional relationships are already represented by STRING and by extensive gene and disease literature. The absence of a hu.MAP complex does not establish novelty, and absence from a database cannot by itself prove a new biological discovery. What may be novel is the conserved kidney-liver co-abundance organization of these genes in the analyzed data, but the fact sheet does not compare this module with prior tissue co-abundance studies or establish that the specific co-abundance pattern has not been reported. The appropriate conclusion is that this is a biologically credible, potentially context-specific module rather than a demonstrated new molecular complex or definitively new pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: coordinated abundance of mitochondrial matrix enzymes in kidney and liver reflects shared regulation of branched-chain amino-acid, fatty-acid and succinyl-CoA flux in metabolically active parenchymal cells. ACAT1, ACAA2 and ACADM may form the principal acyl-CoA and beta-oxidation-associated core, while BCKDHA links the module to branched-chain amino-acid entry into mitochondrial catabolism and SUCLG2 links it to succinyl-CoA and tricarboxylic-acid-cycle handling. This hypothesis predicts that module activity will vary with hepatocyte or related epithelial cell abundance and with physiological states that alter amino-acid or lipid oxidation; the co-abundance data alone do not establish flux, physical complex formation or causal regulation.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered metabolic pathway. Its dominant functions, including fatty-acid oxidation, branched-chain amino-acid degradation, aldehyde metabolism, propionyl-CoA processing, and peroxisomal metabolism, are well established, and many of its relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the identification of this particular conserved kidney-liver co-abundance configuration, including its mixture of mitochondrial, peroxisomal, cytosolic, and liver-derived proteins. However, the fact sheet alone cannot establish that this configuration is absent from prior co-expression, proteomic, or tissue-network studies. Absence of some members from hu.MAP or absence of STRING links for individual genes is not evidence of novelty. The appropriate conclusion is that the biological program is established, while the specific conserved module organization remains a candidate for further validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this module reflects a shared differentiated epithelial metabolic state in kidney and liver that coordinates mitochondrial and peroxisomal oxidation of fatty acids and organic acids with branched-chain amino-acid, propanoate, aldehyde, and xenobiotic catabolism. EHHADH, SLC27A2, ACADS, DECR1, ETFDH, ALDH2, ALDH6A1, PCCA, and PCCB could form functionally coupled routes that transfer carbon substrates into oxidative metabolism while limiting accumulation of reactive aldehydes and organic-acid intermediates. CAT and related oxidoreductases may help manage oxidative by-products, whereas C3 and SERPINC1 may represent a liver-secretory interface superimposed on the intracellular metabolic program. This is a mechanistic interpretation of co-abundance and association evidence, not proof of physical assembly or causal regulation.

### Novelty

The conserved kidney-liver co-abundance pattern may be a new systems-level observation, but the molecular biology represented by the module is not, by itself, a new discovery. Several member functions are well established, and the PSMC2-UCHL5 relationship is represented in STRING and multiple hu.MAP proteasome complexes (hu.MAP huMAP3_05064.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_09656.1)(hu.MAP huMAP3_13725.1). ADAR-NSUN2 is also represented in hu.MAP (hu.MAP huMAP3_12301.1), while EWSR1-HNRNPH1 is represented in STRING. The limited overlap with known complexes means the exact co-abundance organization may be novel, especially as a conserved tissue-pair module, but absence of additional STRING or hu.MAP links cannot establish novelty. A defensible claim is therefore that this is a potentially novel cross-tissue co-abundance configuration built largely from known housekeeping, RNA-processing, and proteostasis machinery, rather than a newly discovered molecular complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated demand for RNA surveillance and processing coupled to proteasome-mediated protein quality control in cells shared by kidney and liver. In this model, ADAR, NSUN2, U2SURP, HNRNPH1, CNOT1, NUP85, and related nuclear factors maintain RNA editing, modification, splicing, export, and turnover, while PSMC2 and UCHL5 provide linked ubiquitin-dependent proteostasis. PRMT5, GPS1, and the vesicle-associated genes may connect this core to transcriptional regulation, signaling, and intracellular trafficking. This is a mechanistic proposal from co-abundance and known annotations, not a demonstrated causal pathway; the lack of globally enriched GO or KEGG terms and the sparse direct interaction structure make experimental validation essential.

### Novelty

The underlying functions are not new: RNA splicing, ribonucleoprotein biogenesis, nuclear transport, ribosome production, chaperoning, and proteasomal turnover are well-established processes, and many module relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel observation is the conserved co-abundance of these partially connected subsystems across kidney and liver. The supplied evidence does not establish that this exact cross-tissue module has been reported previously, and absence from a particular STRING or hu.MAP entry would not prove novelty. I would therefore describe it as a potentially informative conserved network organization, not as a newly discovered molecular complex or definitively novel biological pathway. A rigorous novelty claim would require comparison with published tissue co-expression modules, prior kidney-liver proteomic networks, and independent cohorts.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module represents a coordinated nuclear gene-expression maintenance state in which transcription and chromatin access are coupled to RNA helicase activity, spliceosome assembly, mRNA processing and export, nucleolar ribonucleoprotein production, translation capacity, and selective protein quality control. DDX5 and DHX9 may help organize RNA-remodelling and transcription or splicing interfaces; EFTUD2, PRPF31, and SNRPA1 may support spliceosomal maturation; NUP62, NUP98, NUP93, and RAN may connect processed RNA to nucleocytoplasmic transport; GAR1 and NOP56 may connect the program to nucleolar RNA modification and ribosome biogenesis; and the CCT and proteasome components may maintain the folding and turnover of the resulting protein machinery. This model predicts that module activity will track broad cellular biosynthetic demand or nuclear stress rather than a secreted, immune, or liver-specific function. It remains a hypothesis and should be tested by perturbation, cell-type-resolved measurements, and independent kidney-liver datasets.

The primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for mitochondrial gene expression (GO:BP mitochondrial gene expression; q_global = 1.24e-06), mitochondrial translation (GO:BP mitochondrial translation; q_global = 0.000211), the mitochondrial matrix (GO:CC mitochondrial matrix; q_global = 0.0012), the mitochondrion (GO:CC mitochondrion; q_global = 0.00811), and mitochondrial and organellar ribosomes (GO:CC organellar ribosome; q_global = 0.0196)(GO:CC mitochondrial ribosome; q_global = 0.0196). The genes span mitochondrial ribosomal components, mitochondrial translation factors, mitochondrial RNA processing, respiratory-chain complex-I assembly, pyruvate oxidation, metabolite transport, and mitochondrial metabolism. Thus, the module appears to represent coordinated mitochondrial gene-expression and bioenergetic capacity rather than a narrowly isolated pathway. The STRING enrichments (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 6e-07–0.0184) independently support this interpretation, but they are supplementary and are not treated as global enrichment significance.

**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes. It is also unlikely to be driven by blood contamination or a shared immune-cell population, because every gene has low immune-cell specificity. Most genes have low tissue specificity, consistent with broadly distributed mitochondrial machinery rather than a kidney- or liver-exclusive program. Several genes are cell-type enhanced in diverse populations, including epithelial, reproductive, cardiac, trophoblast, and other cells, so cellular composition could still influence the co-abundance signal. Protein detection is broadly compatible with the kidney and liver observations, although several individual genes have no liver detection or no kidney detection in HPA. Overall, the shared module is more consistent with a conserved intracellular mitochondrial program than with circulating proteins or a blood-cell artifact.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial functions and several physical or functional relationships in this module are established rather than new. In particular, mitochondrial translation, mitochondrial ribosomes, complex-I assembly, pyruvate metabolism, and mitochondrial RNA processing are supported by annotations, STRING, and hu.MAP. The module may nevertheless represent a potentially novel conserved co-abundance pattern shared by kidney and liver, because database complexes do not by themselves document this tissue-pair organization. That possibility should be treated as a finding requiring replication in independent cohorts, control for cell-type composition, and comparison with previously published tissue co-expression modules. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship for some edges is not evidence of novelty; it may reflect incomplete database coverage, indirect regulation, or context-specific co-abundance.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects coordinated maintenance of mitochondrial gene expression and respiratory metabolism in shared kidney and liver cell populations. Mitochondrial ribosomal proteins and translation factors may form the central transcriptional or proteomic backbone, while PNPT1 supports mitochondrial RNA handling, NDUFAF4 and NDUFS4 support respiratory-chain complex-I assembly, and PDHB and ME2 connect mitochondrial translation capacity to carbon metabolism. The RAB10-RABGGTA and TBRG4-associated edges may indicate trafficking or organelle-quality-control context rather than define the core function. This model predicts that module activity should covary with mitochondrial translation, respiratory-chain performance, and mitochondrial metabolic flux, but the co-abundance data alone do not establish directionality, physical binding, or causal regulation.
