**Cell composition.** This is not a plasma or secreted-protein module: the HPA summary reports no secreted genes. Most members have low tissue specificity, and the module is dominated by cytosolic, nuclear, or broadly expressed intracellular proteins. This supports a housekeeping-type interpretation involving translation, protein folding, nucleotide metabolism, and cellular maintenance rather than a specialized bone or colon extracellular program. HPA protein detection is broadly positive in colon for nearly all assessed members, whereas there is no matching HPA tissue result for bone, so the apparent conservation between bone and colon may reflect shared intracellular cell programs rather than a tissue-specific secreted system. There is limited evidence for a blood or immune-cell explanation overall: most genes have low immune-cell specificity. However, PGD is group-enriched in several myeloid blood-cell categories and bone marrow-related compartments, and RRM2 is enhanced in bone marrow and lymphoid tissue. These features leave open a contribution from proliferative or hematopoietic cells in some samples, but they do not support the claim that the whole module is blood-cell derived. The HPA enrichment of several genes in reproductive or progenitor cell types also argues for caution in interpreting the module as a bone-colon-specific mechanism.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular complex. Its principal components are already well known: aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase relationships are supported by STRING and hu.MAP, and the CCT subunits are represented in multiple hu.MAP complexes and have extremely strong STRING support. The enrichment for translation-related functions and chaperonin biology is therefore a rediscovery of established cellular machinery at the module level. There may nevertheless be novelty in the tissue-pair co-abundance observation: the specific coordinated behavior of these genes across bone and colon, the inclusion of MAGEA4 as a highly connected co-abundance hub, and the apparent coupling of aminoacylation, CCT folding, nucleotide metabolism, and cell-cycle-associated genes are not established as a single bone-colon program by the supplied resources. Because absence from STRING or hu.MAP does not prove novelty, the MAGEA4-centered links and the cross-block organization should be treated as hypotheses requiring replication in independent cohorts, cell-composition adjustment, and perturbation or protein-level validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared intracellular demand for translational capacity and proteome quality control in cells present in both bone and colon. Coordinated aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase expression could support protein synthesis, while the CCT chaperonin complex could fold newly synthesized proteins and maintain cytoskeletal or other client proteins. GMPS, PGD, ASNS, OLA1, and RRM2 may represent supporting nucleotide, metabolic, redox, or proliferative requirements, while MAGEA4 may mark a cell-state-dependent regulatory or transcriptional component that links otherwise distinct submodules. A plausible alternative is that the pattern partly reflects shared proliferative, progenitor, reproductive-like, or marrow-associated cell states rather than a direct mechanistic interaction between all module members. This hypothesis is consistent with the primary enrichment and network structure but is not established by co-abundance alone.

### Novelty

The biological function itself is not a new discovery: the module is strongly aligned with established nucleolar ribosome-biogenesis and RNA-processing biology, and many of its core relationships are already represented in STRING and partially in hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the coordinated co-abundance of this network across bone and colon. However, the fact sheet does not provide an external cross-tissue replication set, perturbation evidence, or a direct comparison with previously reported bone-colon modules. Absence from hu.MAP or STRING for several genes cannot by itself demonstrate novelty. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a biologically credible conserved co-abundance module whose cross-tissue conservation may be useful, but it cannot yet be called a newly discovered molecular complex or a definitively novel pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: a shared demand for high-quality ribosome production and RNA maturation in bone and colon cells produces coordinated abundance of a nucleolar assembly-and-surveillance program. In this model, small-subunit processome components such as UTP14A and associated factors coordinate early pre-rRNA cleavage, RNA helicases such as DDX10 remodel structured pre-rRNA, PPAN and related nucleolar factors support preribosome maturation, and USP36 may tune the stability or activity of nucleolar regulators through deubiquitination. QKI and other RNA-binding factors could connect this core program to broader RNA processing and cell-state regulation. This hypothesis predicts that module activity should track nucleolar transcriptional or translational demand and should respond to perturbation of rRNA processing, rather than reflecting a secreted or blood-derived signal.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a wholly new molecular pathway. Its condensin, mismatch-repair, proteasome, and translation-initiation components are established through STRING and hu.MAP, and many member functions are well documented. The potentially novel aspect is the coordinated co-abundance of these established machinery components across bone and colon. That cross-tissue association may reflect a shared proliferative, progenitor, blood-derived, or cell-quality-control state, but novelty requires independent replication, adjustment for cell composition, and comparison with previously reported tissue co-abundance modules. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship for some pairs does not establish novelty, because such resources record selected physical or functional evidence rather than every tissue-level co-abundance relationship. The most defensible conclusion is that this is a biologically coherent cross-tissue module with a potentially novel tissue-level organization, not a newly discovered set of protein functions.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared proliferative or progenitor-like cell state present in both bone and colon, in which condensin-mediated chromosome organization and CDK1-linked mitotic progression are coupled to proteasomal turnover, translation initiation, RNA surveillance, and ribosome-quality control. The HPA enrichment in bone-marrow, lymphoid, erythroid, and megakaryocytic populations raises the additional hypothesis that shared blood-derived or progenitor-cell content contributes to the cross-tissue co-abundance. A further possibility is that stress, oxygen regulation, or rapid protein turnover reinforces this program through EGLN1, the proteasome, and RNA-quality-control components. This is a testable hypothesis, not an established tissue-specific mechanism; it should be evaluated using cell-deconvolution, matched cell-type data, perturbation experiments, and independent bone and colon cohorts.

### Novelty

The module-level finding may be novel as a conserved co-abundance pattern shared by bone and colon, but the functional machinery represented by the module is not itself new. Several relationships and complexes are already documented by STRING and hu.MAP, especially spliceosomal and nucleolar components. Therefore, absence of some module relationships from those resources cannot establish novelty. The defensible conclusion is that this is a potentially novel tissue-pair co-abundance organization of largely established nuclear RNA-processing and ribosome-biogenesis machinery. Demonstrating genuine novelty would require comparison with published bone and colon co-abundance modules, independent cohorts, control modules and replication at the protein or transcript level.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared bone-colon module reflects coordinated demand for nuclear RNA production and maturation in cells that maintain active transcription, pre-mRNA splicing and ribosome assembly. A common cellular state, such as proliferation, differentiation or tissue renewal, could increase the abundance of transcriptional, spliceosomal and nucleolar components together. VIRMA, EHMT2 and BAZ2A could provide regulatory coupling between RNA modification or processing and chromatin state, while POLR2A supports transcriptional output, the SNRNP200-EFTUD2-RBM22 group supports spliceosome function, and the DDX21-NOL6-NOP56-PDCD11-UTP20 group supports ribosomal RNA maturation. This mechanism is plausible from the annotations and network structure but is not proven by co-abundance alone; it should be tested with cell-type-resolved data, perturbation experiments and measurements of transcription, splicing and ribosome-biogenesis activity.

### Novelty

The biological function itself is not a new discovery: the module is strongly aligned with established ribosome-biogenesis, nucleolar and rRNA-processing knowledge, and many of its relationships are already represented in STRING. The potentially novel result is the conservation of this coordinated expression pattern across bone and colon in the particular co-abundance analysis. That claim remains provisional because the fact sheet does not provide an external comparison with other tissue-pair modules, published co-abundance studies or independent replication. The absence of some genes from hu.MAP cannot establish novelty, because database coverage and complex membership are incomplete. Overall, this is best described as a biologically credible and possibly novel cross-tissue co-abundance observation built from a largely established molecular program, not as a newly discovered ribosome-biogenesis complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared bone-colon module reflects a conserved demand for high-capacity ribosome production and rRNA quality control in active or renewing cells. Coordinated abundance of small-subunit processome factors, rRNA cleavage and maturation factors, nucleolar scaffolds and large-subunit maturation factors could couple ribosome output to cell growth, differentiation or tissue repair in both tissues. The presence of broadly expressed nucleolar proteins and the lack of secreted or immune-enriched genes favor this intracellular model. This hypothesis predicts that module activity will track nucleolar activity, ribosome production and cellular renewal rather than a tissue-specific extracellular pathway; it requires validation with independent expression datasets, cell-state deconvolution and functional perturbation.

On the supplied evidence, the appropriate conclusion is a biologically coherent conserved association with partial reuse of known molecular relationships, not proof of a new pathway.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared state of transcriptionally active, proliferating cells in bone and colon in which coordinated RNA maturation, alternative splicing, RNA export or stability, chromatin regulation, and cell-cycle progression are jointly elevated. ### Novelty

The molecular function itself is not a new discovery: the nucleoporin relationships are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and the genes have established roles in nuclear pore organization and transport. The potentially novel aspect is the conservation of this particular co-abundance module across bone and colon, especially the joint behavior of the nucleoporin core with RBM34, DDX24, NOC3L, SDAD1, SPIN1 and other peripheral genes. However, the fact sheet does not provide an external cross-tissue comparison, perturbation experiment, independent cohort, or literature result specifically documenting this bone-colon co-abundance pattern. Therefore, this should be described as a biologically plausible tissue-shared module and a candidate novel regulatory association, not as a definitively new biological finding. Absence of some module relationships from STRING or hu.MAP would not by itself establish novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in both bone and colon, cellular states requiring sustained growth, differentiation, repair or transcriptional activity coordinately increase nuclear pore capacity and RNA-processing capacity. The nucleoporin core may regulate the throughput of protein import and RNA export, while RBM34, DDX24, NOC3L and SDAD1 may couple that transport capacity to nucleolar RNA maturation, ribosome-related processing or RNA surveillance. SPIN1 and the peripheral cytoskeletal or signaling genes could provide context-dependent links to transcriptional regulation, chromatin-associated programs or cell architecture. This model predicts that the module should track nuclear transport and RNA-processing activity rather than a tissue-specific secretory or immune program, but it remains a hypothesis requiring validation with independent tissue cohorts, protein-level measurements and perturbation experiments.
