Protein detection is generally compatible with expression in heart and kidney, although some members are not detected in one of those tissues. Several genes show cell-type enhancement in reproductive or other specialized cell types, but these annotations are dispersed and do not establish that such cells drive the heart–kidney module. Overall, the composition is most consistent with a broadly expressed housekeeping and nuclear/cytosolic machinery module, while sample cellular composition remains a possible contributor to co-abundance.

### Novelty

The module should not be presented as a wholly new molecular system. Its dominant components are established complexes and conserved cellular processes, including the CCT chaperonin, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, nuclear transport, RNA processing, septins, and genome maintenance. The absence of some module genes from STRING or hu.MAP does not demonstrate novelty, because those resources capture physical interactions or curated complexes rather than every abundance relationship. The potentially novel aspect is the conservation of this particular cross-tissue co-abundance configuration in heart and kidney, especially the coupling of CCT proteins with RUVBL2, RNA-localization machinery, translation-related enzymes, and DNA-repair factors. Establishing that as a new finding would require comparison with published tissue co-abundance modules, replication in independent cohorts, and testing whether the association persists after accounting for cell composition and broad expression level.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: this module represents a shared intracellular maintenance program that couples ATP-dependent protein folding by the CCT complex with RNA trafficking and processing, nuclear transport, translation capacity, and protection of chromosome and telomere integrity. In heart and kidney, cells with sustained proteostatic and transcriptional demands may co-regulate these systems so that newly synthesized proteins are folded, RNA and macromolecular components are transported and processed, and DNA or telomere-associated structures are maintained. RUVBL2, CCT subunits, DDX39B, RAN/XPO1, and XRCC6 provide a plausible functional bridge between these processes, but the proposed coupling is mechanistic inference rather than demonstrated causation.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: heart and kidney  
**Genes (17)**: GOT2, ACSL1, SLC25A4, ALDH2, SERPINC1, MAOA, MAOB, CRAT, C3, DHRS4, ETFA, ETFDH, DGLUCY, GPD1L, IDH2, SUCLG1, ACAA2

### Function

The most coherent interpretation is a mitochondrial oxidative-metabolism module, particularly involving fatty-acid handling, electron transfer, the tricarboxylic-acid cycle, amino-acid metabolism, redox balance, and mitochondrial nucleotide exchange. ACAA2, ACSL1, CRAT, ETFA, ETFDH, SUCLG1, IDH2, GOT2, SLC25A4, and ALDH2 provide complementary biochemical links: fatty acids are activated and processed, reducing equivalents are transferred toward the respiratory chain, succinyl-CoA and isocitrate metabolism are connected to energy production, and aldehydes or amines can be oxidatively detoxified. The network topology supports this interpretation because ACAA2 is the most connected member and links lipid metabolism to several mitochondrial and redox-related proteins, while ETFA, ETFDH, ALDH2, and SUCLG1 form a relatively well-supported metabolic core. However, no GO/KEGG term passed global correction, so this is an integrated biological interpretation rather than a globally significant enrichment claim. There are no globally significant GO/KEGG terms and no STRING enrichment supplementary STRING terms; the individual gene annotations and network relationships are therefore the primary support.

**Cell composition.** This does not look primarily like a plasma or secreted-protein module: only SERPINC1 and C3 are annotated as secreted to blood, whereas most members are intracellular enzymes or mitochondrial proteins. It also does not look primarily blood-cell derived. Most genes have low immune-cell specificity or are not detected in immune cells, although ACSL1 is neutrophil-enriched and DGLUCY, ALDH2, and other members have some immune-cell enhancement or association. The stronger compositional signal is tissue and cell-type specificity for liver and hepatocytes, with additional representation of skeletal muscle, heart muscle, enterocytes, tongue, and myonuclei. Thus the shared heart-kidney signal may reflect a broadly conserved oxidative-metabolism program in metabolically active cells, but liver-associated expression and a small blood-derived component should be considered as possible confounders. Protein detection is widespread in both heart and kidney for most members, which is consistent with a genuine shared tissue program, while the absence of heart or kidney detection for some liver-secreted proteins weakens a purely local heart-kidney interpretation.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a wholly new molecular pathway. Several of its central relationships are already represented in STRING, and MAOA–MAOB is supported as a hu.MAP complex (hu.MAP huMAP3_06867.1); additional metabolic organization is represented by (hu.MAP huMAP3_13468.1). The likely novelty is instead the cross-tissue co-abundance organization of these known metabolic components in heart and kidney, especially the way fatty-acid oxidation, electron transfer, central metabolism, amine oxidation, and mitochondrial transport are combined. That interpretation remains provisional because the module has no globally corrected GO/KEGG term, no STRING enrichment term, and includes liver-associated and circulating proteins that could generate a shared abundance pattern. Absence of a hu.MAP or STRING relationship for some pairs is not evidence that those relationships are novel. A defensible novelty claim would require comparison with previously published heart-kidney co-abundance modules and independent replication.

### Novelty

The mitochondrial respiratory-chain and substrate-oxidation biology is not a new discovery: it is strongly represented in STRING, hu.MAP, curated gene functions, disease annotations, and the literature. Therefore the module should not be presented as a newly discovered mitochondrial pathway or as evidence that its individual genes have unknown functions. A potentially novel finding is the conserved heart–kidney co-abundance organization that brings complex I, complex II, complex III, pyruvate dehydrogenase, succinyl-CoA synthetase, branched-chain amino-acid metabolism, and fatty-acid oxidation into one reproducible module. However, the fact sheet does not compare this module against prior tissue co-abundance studies, independent cohorts, or alternative network constructions. Consequently, module-level novelty remains plausible but unproven. Absence of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship is not evidence of novelty, especially for genes whose association may reflect coordinated abundance rather than direct physical interaction.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in energetically demanding heart and kidney cells, mitochondrial abundance and respiratory demand coordinate the production and use of reducing equivalents across several metabolic entry points. Complex I components may form the central co-abundance backbone, while SDHA, UQCRC2, DLAT, DBT, SUCLA2, SUCLG2, ACADM, and CPT2 report linked flux through the respiratory chain, pyruvate oxidation, branched-chain-amino-acid catabolism, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and fatty-acid oxidation. The conserved module may therefore represent a shared mitochondrial capacity state: cells with greater oxidative workload co-express electron-transfer machinery with pathways that supply acetyl-CoA, succinyl-CoA, and fatty-acid-derived reducing equivalents. This is a mechanistic model, not a demonstrated causal pathway. It should be tested using independent heart and kidney cohorts, cell-composition adjustment, mitochondrial-content markers, protein or metabolite measurements, and perturbation of representative complex-I and substrate-oxidation genes.

### Novelty

### Novelty

The biological ingredients are not new in isolation. A careful conclusion is that this may be a new tissue-pair-level co-abundance module or a new systems-level association, but it is not demonstrated to be a new physical pathway or complex without independent replication, comparison with existing tissue co-expression resources, and experimental testing.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in heart and kidney cells, a shared intracellular maintenance program coordinately couples spliceosomal RNA handling with proteasome-dependent protein quality control.
