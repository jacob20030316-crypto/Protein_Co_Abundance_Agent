# Conserved co-abundance modules: cerebrospinal_fluid and lung

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: cerebrospinal_fluid and lung  
**Genes (10)**: SERPINA1, A2M, ALB, APOA1, C3, COL6A2, HBA1, HBB, IGKC, TF

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating extracellular-protein and inflammatory homeostasis module rather than as a tissue-specific intracellular pathway. Its constituents include albumin and apolipoprotein A-I, which support plasma transport and lipid handling; transferrin, which supports iron transport; complement C3, which is central to complement activation and inflammatory signaling; alpha-2-macroglobulin and alpha-1-antitrypsin, which regulate proteases and inflammatory cascades; and hemoglobin subunits, which are associated with erythrocytes and oxygen-related functions. The STRING tissue annotations repeatedly connect most of the module to cardiovascular tissues and plasma or serum proteomic datasets (15 supplementary STRING terms; within-query FDR 2.24e-05–0.0149). However, no GO or KEGG term passed global correction, so these functional interpretations are based on individual gene annotations, network evidence, STRING supplementary results, and hu.MAP relationships rather than on globally significant GO/KEGG enrichment.

**Cell composition.** The module is strongly dominated by secreted or blood-associated proteins: most genes are secreted to blood or are immunoglobulin, erythrocyte, or extracellular-matrix products. HPA indicates that secreted genes are the clear majority, with frequent liver and hepatocyte enrichment, especially for ALB, C3, SERPINA1, APOA1, and related proteins. The presence of HBB and HBA1 points to erythrocyte material, while IGKC points to plasma-cell or B-cell contribution. HPA also shows enrichment involving monocytes, neutrophils, plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and other immune populations. Thus, the shared cerebrospinal-fluid–lung signal may primarily reflect circulating plasma proteins, blood contamination or leakage, vascular exposure, and shared immune or blood-derived material rather than coordinated production by lung and cerebrospinal-fluid cells. A lung-associated signal is plausible for A2M, COL6A2, HBB, and SERPINA1, but the absence of lung immunohistochemical detection for several major proteins and the strong liver signature argue against treating the module as a lung-specific program.

### Novelty

The conserved co-abundance observation across cerebrospinal fluid and lung may be a useful dataset-level finding, but the molecular content itself is not plausibly novel as a protein interaction or complex. Most central relationships are already represented by high-confidence STRING links and recurring hu.MAP complexes, and the module is strongly recognizable as a plasma, liver-derived, immune-associated, and erythrocyte-associated protein set. The absence of a globally significant GO/KEGG term does not invalidate the module, but it limits claims that it defines a newly established pathway. A potentially novel aspect is the reproducible co-abundance of this particular combination across the two tissue groups, especially if the result persists after controlling for blood exposure, hemolysis, sample quality, and shared cell-type composition. That claim requires independent replication and cannot be established from the supplied annotations alone.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared extracellular transport and inflammatory-buffering compartment sampled in both cerebrospinal fluid and lung. Liver-derived plasma proteins may enter or equilibrate with these compartments, while complement, protease inhibition, iron transport, lipid transport, and hemoglobin-associated signals jointly report vascular permeability, local inflammation, or blood-derived material. A secondary possibility is that lung extracellular matrix and immune-cell activity contribute to the co-abundance pattern through COL6A2, C3, A2M, SERPINA1, and IGKC. This hypothesis predicts that module abundance will track plasma leakage or blood-cell contamination markers and inflammatory state more strongly than tissue-specific transcription. It should be tested by adjusting for hemolysis and plasma-protein abundance, comparing paired plasma and tissue-fluid samples, and validating the module in independent cohorts.

The strongest interpretation is an extracellular-matrix structural and organization module. **Cell composition.** All genes in the module are annotated as secreted, and most are extracellular-matrix or blood-secreted proteins. In the shared cerebrospinal-fluid and lung signal, the module could therefore reflect a common matrix-producing cell population or blood and plasma carryover rather than a mechanism unique to both tissues. The HPA protein evidence is especially limited for cerebrospinal fluid and shows detection for only HSPG2 in lung, so transcript or protein abundance in the source material should not be treated as direct proof of local tissue production.

### Novelty

The constituent biology is not new: collagen-VI, fibrillin, decorin, lumican, perlecan, fibulin, vitronectin, and fibrinogen are established extracellular or circulating proteins, and many of their relationships are already represented in STRING or hu.MAP. Accordingly, this module should not be claimed as a newly discovered molecular complex or a new extracellular-matrix pathway. What may be novel is the reproducible co-abundance pattern shared by cerebrospinal fluid and lung in this particular dataset, especially the juxtaposition of a coherent stromal matrix core with a liver-associated circulating-protein component. That dataset-level observation requires validation in independent cohorts, adjustment for blood contamination and cellular composition, and comparison with other tissue pairs before it can be called a tissue-specific discovery. Known database relationships strengthen biological plausibility but do not by themselves establish the novelty of the cross-tissue module.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared module represents an extracellular matrix and vascular-interface program contributed by fibroblast-like or stromal cells, with circulating liver-derived proteins superimposed on that scaffold. COL6A1 and COL6A3 may provide collagen-VI filaments, while DCN and LUM organize collagen fibrils and FBN1 and HSPG2 stabilize microfibrillar and basement-membrane architecture. FBLN1 and VTN may connect the scaffold to adhesion receptors, whereas FGA, APOA2, and CP may mark blood or plasma exposure and help explain the cerebrospinal-fluid/lung sharing. A testable prediction is that the module will track extracellular-matrix-producing cells and vascular or blood-content measures more strongly than neuron-specific or lung-epithelial programs.
