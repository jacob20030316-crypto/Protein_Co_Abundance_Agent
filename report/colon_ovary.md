# Conserved co-abundance modules: colon and ovary

## Module 0

**Tissue pair**: colon and ovary  
**Genes (7)**: SERPINA1, ALB, APOA1, CLU, C3, FGA, TF

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a circulating plasma-protein program involving lipid transport, complement and coagulation, extracellular vesicles, and humoral defense. The strongest primary evidence is the globally corrected enrichment for blood microparticles (GO:CC blood microparticle; q_global = 3.09e-07), endoplasmic-reticulum lumen and secretory granule lumen (GO:CC endoplasmic reticulum lumen; q_global = 7.82e-07)(GO:CC secretory granule lumen; q_global = 2.44e-06), vesicle-lumen compartments (GO:CC cytoplasmic vesicle lumen; q_global = 2.56e-06)(GO:CC vesicle lumen; q_global = 2.7e-06), and complement and coagulation cascades (KEGG Complement and coagulation cascades - Homo sapiens (human); q_global = 5.09e-05). Humoral immune response (GO:BP humoral immune response; q_global = 0.000144), regulation of vesicle transport and endocytosis (GO:BP positive regulation of endocytosis; q_global = 0.00237)(GO:BP regulation of vesicle-mediated transport; q_global = 0.00541)(GO:BP positive regulation of receptor-mediated endocytosis; q_global = 0.0106)(GO:BP regulation of endocytosis; q_global = 0.0181)(GO:BP regulation of receptor-mediated endocytosis; q_global = 0.0454), defense response (GO:BP defense response; q_global = 0.0138), complement activation (GO:BP complement activation, classical pathway; q_global = 0.0267), reverse cholesterol transport (GO:BP reverse cholesterol transport; q_global = 0.0267), and receptor binding (GO:MF signaling receptor binding; q_global = 0.0475) provide convergent functional support. Because most of the primary terms are compartmental or extracellular, the module should not be interpreted as evidence for a single intracellular pathway. Rather, it represents coordinated abundance of secreted blood proteins with complementary roles in transport, innate immunity, protease control, iron handling, and hemostasis.

**Cell composition.** All genes are annotated as secreted to blood, and the module is strongly liver-associated in HPA, with hepatocytes the most frequent enriched cell type. HPA also links subsets to enterocytes, cholangiocytes, endometrial secretory cells, and several myeloid or other immune populations. None of the encoded proteins was detected by HPA in colon or ovary. This composition strongly suggests that the shared colon–ovary module may reflect circulating plasma abundance, vascular or blood exposure, or shared sample-associated cell material rather than a resident colon- or ovary-specific program. The module is not dominated by canonical intracellular housekeeping proteins. Its immune-cell evidence is mixed: some genes are not detected in immune cells, whereas others are enriched or enhanced in monocytes, neutrophils, plasmacytoid dendritic cells, or basophils. Thus, blood contamination or shared vascular and immune components should be considered before assigning the module to epithelial tissue biology.

### Novelty

The module is unlikely to represent a newly discovered molecular pathway: its genes, functions, STRING relationships, and hu.MAP memberships are largely established plasma, lipid-transport, complement, coagulation, and secreted-protein biology. The potentially novel observation is the coordinated co-abundance of this circulating-protein set in the particular colon–ovary tissue comparison. However, the fact sheet provides no independent replication, cohort information, tissue-deconvolution result, perturbation experiment, or comparison with previously published co-abundance modules. Therefore, this cross-tissue module can be described as a potentially useful systems-level observation, but not as a demonstrated new biological complex or disease mechanism. Its apparent conservation may primarily reflect shared exposure to blood-derived proteins or common sample composition.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: variation in the abundance of liver-derived plasma proteins entering or associated with colon and ovary samples produces a conserved extracellular module. Within this circulating pool, APOA1 and CLU may organize lipid-associated particles, C3 may provide complement and inflammatory activity, SERPINA1 may restrain protease-mediated tissue injury, FGA may represent coagulation and platelet-related material, ALB may contribute carrier and plasma-particle structure, and TF may contribute iron transport. The coordinated signal could therefore arise from a shared vascular or extracellular environment, altered barrier permeability, local inflammation, or technical carryover of blood proteins. This hypothesis is consistent with the enrichment and HPA evidence, but it is not established by the co-abundance network alone and requires plasma-depletion controls, cell-type deconvolution, and validation in independent colon and ovary cohorts.

## Module 1

**Tissue pair**: colon and ovary  
**Genes (8)**: ARGLU1, MATR3, NUP98, PES1, POLR2A, SRRT, SNRNP200, EFTUD2

### Function

The module is best interpreted as a nuclear RNA maturation and gene-expression module. The primary evidence is global enrichment for nucleic acid binding (GO:MF nucleic acid binding; q_global = 0.0367) and mRNA processing (GO:BP mRNA processing; q_global = 0.0495). The participating genes span spliceosome function, RNA polymerase II transcription, nuclear pore-mediated RNA transport, RNA-associated structural regulation, and ribosome biogenesis. Supplementary STRING results reinforce RNA metabolism, capped pre-mRNA processing, and both major and minor spliceosomal pathways (STRING RCTM mRNA Splicing - Minor Pathway; within-query FDR = 0.0102)(STRING RCTM Processing of Capped Intron-Containing Pre-mRNA; within-query FDR = 0.0102)(STRING RCTM Metabolism of RNA; within-query FDR = 0.0102)(STRING RCTM mRNA Splicing - Major Pathway; within-query FDR = 0.0132). Thus, the most defensible functional interpretation is coordinated post-transcriptional RNA processing linked to transcription and nuclear RNA handling, rather than a tissue-specific metabolic or extracellular pathway. The absence of within-module significance does not invalidate the module; it indicates that the global evidence is concentrated in the two terms that survived the stricter correction.

**Cell composition.** This is a predominantly nuclear, broadly expressed protein set rather than a plasma-protein, secreted-protein, or blood-cell-enriched module. HPA reports no secreted genes and low immune-cell specificity for every gene. Most genes have low tissue specificity, while selected genes show tissue or cell-type enhancement in lymphoid tissue, neutrophils, corticotrophs, fallopian epithelial cells, medullary thymic epithelial cells, megakaryocyte progenitors, or germ-cell populations. These isolated enrichments do not establish a blood-contamination explanation. Protein detection is present for many genes in colon and for fewer genes in ovary, with some genes lacking data or detection in one tissue. The shared colon-ovary signal therefore more plausibly reflects a conserved nuclear housekeeping or RNA-processing program, although variation in cellular composition remains a possible contributor.

The primary evidence is exceptionally coherent: the MCM genes MCM2, MCM4, MCM5, MCM6, and MCM7 recur across the globally corrected MCM-complex, CMG-complex, DNA-replication-initiation, helicase, replication-fork, cell-cycle, homologous-recombination, and DNA-damage-response terms (53 globally significant GO/KEGG terms, q_global 4.97e-10–0.0353; see the table). NASP extends the module toward replication-dependent chromatin assembly and histone transport, and is included in the globally supported DNA-replication and chromosome terms (GO:BP DNA replication; q_global = 9.63e-06), (GO:BP DNA metabolic process; q_global = 0.00115), and (GO:CC chromosome; q_global = 0.0073). Thus, the best-supported interpretation is coordinated abundance of replication-fork licensing and helicase machinery, with an associated chromatin assembly component. The repair annotations should be interpreted as compatibility with replication-associated repair functions, not as proof that the module performs a particular repair reaction in these tissues.

**Cell composition.** The module is composed entirely of nuclear, non-secreted proteins; it is not a plasma-protein or secreted-protein module. HPA shows tissue enhancement mainly in bone marrow and lymphoid tissue, with cell-type enhancement concentrated in erythrocyte progenitors and also present in megakaryocyte, monocyte, and related progenitor populations. Every gene has low immune-cell specificity, so this is not evidence for a mature immune-cell signature. The pattern instead suggests a proliferative progenitor or cycling-cell component that may be shared between colon and ovary samples. Because the proteins are broadly compatible with mitotic cells and are detected in colon, while most are also detected in ovary, the shared module could reflect common proliferative cell states or cellular composition rather than a colon-ovary-specific molecular program. HPA protein detection supports biological presence, but does not by itself establish activity of the replication machinery.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a newly discovered protein complex or a novel DNA-replication pathway. Its central MCM relationships are already strongly represented in STRING and hu.MAP, and its annotations are extensively established in curated functional resources. A potentially novel aspect is the observation that this particular co-abundance pattern is conserved across the colon and ovary tissue groups, especially the coupling of the MCM core with NASP. However, the fact sheet does not establish that this exact tissue-pair module has never been reported. Accordingly, the defensible conclusion is contextual novelty of the co-abundance observation, not discovery of a new molecular complex. Lack of a matching entry in a network database would not, by itself, prove novelty.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module marks a shared cycling or progenitor-like cellular state in colon and ovary. In this model, coordinated abundance of the MCM core supports replication-origin licensing and fork helicase function, while NASP helps supply or organize histones for replication-coupled chromatin assembly. Replication stress or fork-associated lesions could explain the accompanying homologous-recombination and break-induced-replication annotations. This hypothesis predicts that module abundance will track proliferation and replication activity, and that its apparent cross-tissue conservation will weaken after controlling for proliferating progenitor or cycling-cell composition. It remains a hypothesis because co-abundance and enrichment do not demonstrate physical assembly, pathway flux, or causal activity in either tissue.
