### Function

This is a mixed cellular maintenance module rather than a cleanly defined pathway. Its strongest coherent themes are mitochondrial metabolism and protein production, with additional endoplasmic-reticulum stress and folding, membrane transport, cell adhesion, and genome-maintenance components. SHMT2, ALDH18A1, and MTHFD1L connect mitochondrial amino-acid and one-carbon metabolism; MRPL4, MRPL11, and DARS2 support mitochondrial translation; TRAP1 and GPD2 are consistent with mitochondrial stress handling and redox-linked metabolism. PDIA4 and HYOU1 support ER protein folding and stress responses, while CTNNA1, CTNND1, and IQGAP1 support cadherin-linked adhesion and cytoskeletal organization. TFRC, SLC1A5, SLC3A2, and VDAC2 add iron, amino-acid, and mitochondrial metabolite transport. PRKDC contributes DNA double-strand-break repair. The subgraph is relatively sparse and links several cellular systems rather than representing one established biochemical complex. No GO or KEGG term passed global correction, so no GO or KEGG enrichment is claimed. The interpretation is therefore based on convergent gene functions and network structure, not on globally significant pathway enrichment.

**Cell composition.** The module does not appear to be dominated by plasma or secreted proteins: only a minority of the genes are annotated as secreted, and most are intracellular mitochondrial, ER, membrane, or nuclear proteins. HPA indicates broad tissue distribution for most genes, with tissue enhancement scattered across liver, salivary gland, brain, and blood vessel rather than concentrated in kidney or rectum. Most genes show cell-type enhancement, but the enriched cell types are heterogeneous and include epithelial, stromal, trophoblast-like, neural, and myeloid categories. Most genes have low immune-cell specificity, although a smaller subset is enhanced in monocytes, dendritic cells, or related myeloid populations. Nearly all proteins are detected in both kidney and rectum, with the stated exception of HYOU1 being detected in rectum but not kidney and SLC3A2 being detected in kidney but not rectum. Overall, the module is more consistent with a shared epithelial or tissue-cell metabolic and stress state than with a simple blood-contamination signature, although variable immune or stromal contributions cannot be excluded.

### Novelty

The module should not be called a newly discovered molecular complex or pathway. Several of its local relationships and functional groupings are already represented in STRING and hu.MAP, particularly the SHMT2 metabolic links, the CTNNA1–CTNND1 adhesion pair, the PDIA4–HYOU1 ER pair, and the MRPL4–MRPL11 mitochondrial ribosome pair. A potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance coupling of these otherwise distinct mitochondrial, ER, adhesion, transport, and DNA-repair components across kidney and rectum. That observation is hypothesis-generating rather than proof of a new mechanism. Absence of some module pairs from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty, and the lack of globally significant GO or KEGG enrichment limits claims about a uniquely defined biological program. Replication in independent cohorts, cell-type-resolved data, and perturbation experiments would be needed to establish whether the cross-tissue coupling is biologically reproducible rather than driven by shared abundance, cellular composition, or general tissue state.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved module reflects a coordinated tissue-cell state in which mitochondrial nutrient use and one-carbon metabolism support protein synthesis and redox balance, while ER folding capacity, membrane transport, cell-junction organization, and DNA repair adjust to the associated metabolic and proteotoxic stress. In this model, SHMT2 and MTHFD1L provide a mitochondrial metabolic core, ALDH18A1 supplies linked amino-acid biosynthetic capacity, TRAP1 and GPD2 modulate mitochondrial stress and redox handling, PDIA4 and HYOU1 protect ER protein folding, and CTNNA1 with CTNND1 preserves epithelial junctional organization. PRKDC may represent a parallel genome-protection response. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not an established causal pathway, because the enrichment tests were negative after global correction and the network contains multiple known submodules rather than one unified complex.

### Novelty

The molecular functions represented here are not new: SRP-mediated targeting, CCT chaperoning, RNA processing, coatomer trafficking and translation initiation are established biological processes, and several within-module relationships are already present in STRING or hu.MAP. The potentially novel observation is the coordinated co-abundance of these components across kidney and rectum as one conserved module. The fact sheet alone cannot establish that this tissue-pair-level pattern is a new finding, because novelty requires comparison with prior co-abundance studies, independent cohorts, technical replication and control for cell composition. Absence of a complete representation in STRING or hu.MAP would not prove novelty. The defensible conclusion is therefore that the module may provide a new cross-tissue co-abundance observation, but it is not evidence for a newly discovered molecular function or complex.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the module reflects a shared intracellular capacity for handling newly synthesized and stress-sensitive proteins across kidney and rectum. SRP68 and SRP72 may mark cotranslational delivery of selected proteins to the endoplasmic reticulum, while COPB1, COPG2 and SCFD1 support ER-Golgi and Golgi-retrograde trafficking. CCT7 and CCT8 could assist folding of newly synthesized cytosolic proteins, and PTBP1, HNRNPK and DDX6 could coordinate RNA processing, translation control and mRNA turnover. The co-abundance pattern may therefore arise when cells in both tissues maintain coupled protein synthesis, folding, quality control and intracellular trafficking. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal pathway; it should be tested by perturbing the SRP, CCT or trafficking components and measuring effects on protein localization, proteostasis and cell-type-specific expression.

The module is a conserved nuclear and nucleolar RNA-production program centered on ribosome biogenesis, rRNA processing, preribosome assembly, and broader RNA processing. This interpretation is supported directly by the globally corrected terms for RNA binding (GO:MF RNA binding; q_global = 4.25e-08), ribosome biogenesis (GO:BP ribosome biogenesis; q_global = 4.4e-08), RNA processing (GO:BP RNA processing; q_global = 1.5e-07), ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis (GO:BP ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis; q_global = 2.61e-07), preribosome (GO:CC preribosome; q_global = 4.18e-06), rRNA processing (GO:BP rRNA processing; q_global = 4.3e-06), rRNA metabolic process (GO:BP rRNA metabolic process; q_global = 1.64e-05), nucleolus (GO:CC nucleolus; q_global = 0.00028), and the PeBoW complex (GO:CC PeBoW complex; q_global = 0.01). **Cell composition.** The module is not composed of plasma proteins or secreted proteins: HPA reports no secreted genes. Most genes have low tissue specificity, low immune-cell specificity, and nuclear, nucleolar, or nucleoplasmic locations. A minority of genes show enrichment in skeletal muscle, bone marrow, placenta, lymphoid tissue, or selected cell types, including RRP12 with neutrophil-related enrichment and HMGB3 with immune-cell enrichment, but the overall composition does not support a blood-cell-driven explanation. The shared module therefore most plausibly reflects a common biosynthetic and nuclear function present in cells from both tissues.

### Novelty

The biological function is not a new mechanism: the principal genes, interactions, and complexes are already represented in STRING, hu.MAP, Gene Ontology, and the cited gene annotations. The module may still be a new cross-tissue co-abundance observation because its specific conservation between kidney and rectum and its particular combination of nucleolar, RNA-processing, ribosomal, and nuclear-envelope genes are not established as novel by the fact sheet alone. Absence of a complete matching hu.MAP complex or absence of some individual relationships from STRING would not prove novelty. A defensible claim is therefore that this is a potentially novel tissue-pair module organization of largely established biology, requiring comparison with published co-expression modules and independent kidney and rectum datasets before calling it a new finding.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney-rectum module reflects coordinated demand for ribosome production and RNA quality control in actively maintaining epithelial and other resident cells. In this model, the PeBoW-related machinery involving BOP1, PES1, and WDR12, together with NOP2, NOP56, PDCD11, UTP15, and associated factors, supports maturation of ribosomal RNA and assembly of preribosomes. DDX21 and DHX9 may couple RNA remodeling to this nucleolar program, while HNRNPL, HNRNPR, HNRNPF, TAF15, and U2SURP connect it to processing of nascent transcripts. Ribosomal proteins and lamin-associated genes may reflect downstream translational capacity and nuclear organization. This is a mechanistic model, not proof of causal regulation; testing it would require perturbation or activity measurements rather than co-abundance alone.

### Novelty

The module should not be described as a wholly new molecular function. Its dominant biology is already well established through the known functions of ADAR, RBM39, DDX46, NSUN2, CNOT1, XRN2, DIS3, EIF4E, EIF4G1, nucleoporins, and spliceosomal factors, and several relationships are represented in STRING or hu.MAP. At the same time, the specific conserved co-abundance organization shared by kidney and rectum may be a new observation, particularly because the module combines components from multiple RNA-handling systems rather than corresponding to one canonical complex. The available evidence supports novelty at the level of tissue-pair network organization or coordinated regulation, not novelty of the underlying biochemical processes. Lack of a STRING or hu.MAP relationship for some module pairs is not evidence that those relationships are newly discovered.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the conserved kidney–rectum module represents a shared epithelial and stromal cellular state in which RNA production, processing, editing, surveillance, export, and translation are coordinately maintained. A possible mechanism is that ADAR- and NSUN2-linked RNA modification, spliceosomal activity involving DDX46 and PRPF4, RNA-binding and splicing regulation involving RBM39 and FAM120A, and RNA decay involving CNOT1, XRN2, DIS3, and ZCCHC8 jointly tune the stability and translation of transcripts. The EIF4E–EIF4G1 axis could connect this nuclear RNA program to cytoplasmic protein synthesis, while NUP98 and NUP210 could connect transcript processing to nucleocytoplasmic transport. This is a mechanistic model rather than a demonstrated causal pathway; testing would require cell-type-resolved measurements and perturbation of selected hub genes.

### Novelty

The molecular functions represented here are not new: aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase complexes, spliceosomal machinery, chaperonins, and nuclear-pore components are established biology, and many of the corresponding relationships are already present in STRING and hu.MAP. The potentially novel aspect is the conserved co-abundance organization of these established systems across kidney and rectum. That observation may indicate shared regulation of translation capacity and RNA processing, but it cannot be called a new biological mechanism from this fact sheet alone. Absence of a particular edge or complex from STRING or hu.MAP would not establish novelty, because those resources are incomplete and are not designed to capture every tissue-specific co-abundance relationship. The appropriate conclusion is that this is a biologically coherent, potentially novel tissue-pair network representation of largely established machinery.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: in kidney and rectum, variation in cellular biosynthetic demand is coordinated across amino-acid charging, translation initiation, RNA surveillance, spliceosome activity, and protein folding. Increased or decreased abundance of the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase group may track the capacity to produce correctly charged tRNAs and sustain translation, while linked spliceosomal and RNA-binding proteins may coordinate transcript maturation and quality control. CCT3 and CCT5 could help buffer the resulting proteostasis burden, and nucleoporins could couple RNA processing with nucleocytoplasmic transport. This hypothesis predicts coordinated changes in translation, splicing, and proteostasis under physiological or stress-related variation, but the co-abundance data do not establish directionality, causality, or direct biochemical regulation.

### Novelty

The module should not be claimed as a newly discovered molecular complex or a novel DNA-replication pathway. Its central MCM, CMG, PCNA and repair relationships are already well established by STRING, hu.MAP, gene annotations and extensive literature. What may be novel is the observation that this established replication program co-abundance pattern is conserved between kidney and rectum, together with its particular peripheral genes and tissue context. That observation is hypothesis-generating rather than proof of a new mechanism. Absence of some peripheral genes from STRING or hu.MAP does not establish novelty; it may reflect incomplete interaction maps, context dependence, indirect co-abundance or technical coverage. A genuine novelty claim would require independent replication across cohorts and tissues, comparison with existing co-expression modules, and experimental or perturbational validation.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: the shared kidney–rectum module reflects a conserved proliferative-state program in which MCM-mediated helicase loading and fork progression are coordinated with PCNA-dependent DNA synthesis and MSH2-associated surveillance. The peripheral links to nucleoporins, chaperone machinery, nucleotide synthesis, protein folding, lipid synthesis and collagen modification may represent tissue-specific support for the biosynthetic, trafficking or stress requirements of proliferating cells, or may reflect mixed-cell composition. This hypothesis predicts that module activity should track proliferating-cell abundance and replication markers in both tissues, and that its peripheral components should vary with the local cell types while the MCM core remains comparatively stable. The data establish co-abundance and known molecular associations, but do not demonstrate directionality, physical interaction for every edge, or causal regulation.

### Novelty

This module is not a new discovery of complex-I composition or function: its genes, interactions, annotations, and complex membership are already strongly established by STRING, hu.MAP, MyGene descriptions, disease resources, and the literature. The potentially novel aspect could be the discovery of this particular conserved co-abundance pattern across kidney and rectum, but that cannot be established as a new biological mechanism from the fact sheet alone. Absence of additional genes from the module or absence of a particular relationship in a database would not establish novelty. The conservative conclusion is that this is a biologically credible tissue-shared co-abundance module that recovers known complex-I biology, with novelty limited to its dataset-specific tissue pattern unless independently replicated.

**Hypothesis.** Hypothesis: kidney and rectum samples share a coordinated mitochondrial complex-I abundance program in which the represented core and accessory subunits vary together with cellular oxidative-energy demand. The coordinated presence of flavin- and iron-sulfur-associated subunits could support efficient NADH-to-ubiquinone electron transfer, maintenance of the proton gradient, and downstream ATP synthesis. The tissue-shared pattern may therefore reflect conserved energetic requirements across selected epithelial or metabolically active cell populations. This is a mechanistic hypothesis, not proof of altered complex-I activity, because co-abundance does not establish catalytic flux, assembly order, or causality.
